South African Gold Mining Formula Tax & Capital Allowances (2026–2028)Statutory Gold Mining Tax Formula Coefficients and MechanicsUnder the South African Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962 (ITA) and the annual Rates and Monetary Amounts and Amendment of Revenue Laws Acts, income derived by companies directly from gold mining operations in South Africa is taxed under a specialized statutory progression known as the "gold mining tax formula," rather than the flat corporate income tax (CIT) rate. Originally designed to incentivize deep-level extraction, extend the life of marginal ore bodies, and preserve mining employment, the formula links a mine's normal income tax rate directly to its operational profitability ratio.Following the reduction of the South African headline corporate income tax rate from 28% to 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (or commencing on or after 1 April 2022), the statutory gold mining tax formula was recalibrated from its prior structure of $Y = 34 - \frac{170}{X}$ to its current form, which remains in force across the 2026–2028 assessment cycle:$$Y = 33 - \frac{165}{X}$$Mapped to the general statutory form $Y = a - \frac{ab}{X}$, the specific parameters and variables for 2026–2028 establish a dynamic fiscal progression. In this equation, $Y$ represents the calculated percentage rate of normal income tax levied on taxable income derived from gold mining on a specific gold mine; if the formula yields a negative value, $Y$ is floored at $0.00\%$. The parameter $a = 33$ serves as the asymptotic maximum tax rate coefficient ($33\%$) toward which the effective gold mining tax rate converges as the profitability ratio $X \to 100\%$. The parameter $b = 5$, derived from $\frac{ab}{a} = \frac{165}{33} = 5$, establishes a $5.0\%$ statutory "tax tunnel" exemption threshold whereby any gold mine operating at a taxable profit-to-revenue ratio of $X \le 5.0\%$ achieves $Y \le 0\%$ and is wholly exempt from normal income tax on its gold mining income. The hyperbolic relief numerator $ab = 165$ is the product of the maximum rate coefficient ($33$) and the tax tunnel threshold ($5$), governing the slope of tax relief across low-to-moderate margin operations. Finally, $X$ denotes the gold mining profitability ratio, expressed as a percentage, comparing taxable income derived from gold mining—after deducting operating costs and redeemed Section 36 capital allowances attributable to that mine, but before offsetting non-gold mining losses—to total gross revenue (turnover) from gold mining:$$X = \left( \frac{\text{Taxable Income from Gold Mining}}{\text{Total Revenue (Turnover) from Gold Mining}} \right) \times 100$$Tax Regime / Income CategoryStatutory Basis (ITA No. 58 of 1962)Applicable Formula / Rate (2026–2028)Coefficient aaaCoefficient bbbProduct abababKey Structural ThresholdsGold Mining Income (Current)Schedule I, Rates Act; Sec. 1 & Sec. 36 ITA$Y = 33 - \frac{165}{X}$[cite: 4]$33$[cite: 4]$5$[cite: 1, 4]$165$[cite: 4]Tax-exempt at $X \le 5.0\%$; equals $27\%$ CIT at $X = 27.5\%$; reaches $30.0\%$ at $X = 55.0\%$Gold Mining Income (Pre-April 2022)Historical Rates Acts (up to FY2022)$Y = 34 - \frac{170}{X}$[cite: 6, 7]$34$[cite: 1, 6]$5$[cite: 1]$170$[cite: 1, 6]Tax-exempt at $X \le 5.0\%$; equaled $28\%$ CIT at $X = 28.33\%$Non-Mining & Non-Formula IncomeSchedule I, Para. 1(a) Rates ActFlat $27.0\%$[cite: 5, 8, 9]N/AN/AN/AApplies to non-mining income, interest, and standalone surface reclamation not on a gold mining rightCapital Recoupments (Para. Conversely, once high realized gold prices and exhausted capital allowances push $X$ above $27.5\%$, the formula imposes a progressive super-tax up to a $600\text{ bps}$ premium over the standard corporate rate. At a taxable margin of $X = 55.0\%$, the formula tax rate reaches exactly $Y = 33 - \frac{165}{55} = 30.0\%$.Capital Redemption Allowances, Ring-Fencing, and Run-Down Mechanics Above 55% EBITDA MarginsUnder Section 15(a) read with Section 36 of the ITA, South African mining companies do not depreciate mining property, plant, shaft sinking, and development equipment over their accounting useful lives; instead, $100\%$ of qualifying mining capital expenditure is immediately deductible against mining income in the year incurred. Any capital expenditure exceeding a mine's taxable income in a given year is carried forward indefinitely as unredeemed capital expenditure (an accumulated capital redemption allowance) to shield future mining profits. Furthermore, brownfield gold mines remain entitled to a supplementary annual capital allowance (compound interest allowance) of $10\%$ or $12\%$ per annum—depending on the date of mine establishment—calculated on the opening balance of unredeemed capital expenditure plus current-year capital additions, provided the mine remains in an unredeemed capital position.Crucially, these allowances are governed by two strict statutory ring-fences under Section 36 of the ITA. Under the taxpayer or activity ring-fence, special mining capital allowances cannot be offset against non-mining income (such as treasury interest or corporate fee income) within the same legal entity. Under the per-mine ring-fence (Section 36(7F)), unredeemed capital expenditure accumulated at one specific mine is strictly ring-fenced to that cash-generating unit (CGU) and cannot be transferred to shelter taxable profits at another producing mine owned by the same group, barring specific ministerial exemption or limited $25\%$ capex-breach provisions for non-gold mines.In the case of Pan African Resources PLC (PAF), this per-mine ring-fencing created a pronounced divergence between its accounting Effective Tax Rate (ETR) and its actual Cash Tax Rate during FY25 and FY26. Between FY24 and FY25, PAF executed a capital-intensive growth phase, deploying $\text{US\$156.3 million}$ in growth capital and $\text{US\$11.7 million}$ in sustaining capital in FY25 alone to construct the Mogale Tailings Retreatment (MTR) plant (commissioned between October and December 2024), advance the Evander Mines 8 Shaft (24–26 Level) underground sub-vertical shaft and Egoli development, and complete the Nobles CIL plant at Tennant Mines in Australia (commissioned May 2025). This capital cycle created substantial ring-fenced unredeemed capital expenditure balances at MTR and Evander Mines, alongside initial depreciable tax bases and brought-forward tax losses at Tennant Mines.During FY26 (year ended 30 June 2026), PAF's operational and financial profile underwent a structural step-change. In FY26H1 (six months ended 31 December 2025), group gold production surged $51.5\%$ year-on-year to $128,296\text{ oz}$ (gold sold: $127,296\text{ oz}$), while the average realized gold price jumped $61.6\%$ to $\text{US\$3,812/oz}$, up from $\text{US\$2,359/oz}$ in FY25H1. Consequently, FY26H1 revenue rose $157.3\%$ to $\text{US\$487.1 million}$ and Adjusted EBITDA expanded $322.8\%$ to $\text{US\$245.2 million}$ (a $50.3\%$ H1 EBITDA margin), while operating cash flow reached $\text{US\$259.5 million}$. In FY26H2, with group production accelerating toward full-year guidance of $275,000\text{–}292,000\text{ oz}$ (including $\sim 18,500\text{ oz}$ from Tennant Mines in H2 for a full-year Australian contribution of $\sim 34,000\text{ oz}$) and spot gold prices climbing past $\text{US\$4,000/oz}$, full-year FY26 revenue surged $114\%$ year-on-year. This propelled full-year Group EBITDA margins above $55\%$, nearly tripled headline earnings per share (HEPS) to $17.64\text{ US cents}$, eliminated net debt entirely by June 2026 (down from $\text{US\$228.5 million}$ in December 2024 and $\text{US\$46.2 million}$ in December 2025), and funded a record $\text{US\$113.6 million}$ ($\text{R1.86 billion}$) total dividend alongside a $\text{R500 million}$ share buyback.Quantitative Run-Down Rate and Cash Tax Transition Timeline (FY26–FY28)At an EBITDA margin exceeding $55\%$, the mathematical run-down rate of accumulated unredeemed capital expenditure (UCR) accelerates non-linearly because pre-capex taxable mining income ($\approx \text{EBITDA} - \text{MPRRA Royalties}$) expands far faster than ongoing sustaining and expansion capital additions:$$\text{UCR Run-Down}_t = \text{Opening UCR}_t - \max\Big(0, \text{Opening UCR}_t + \text{Qualifying Capex}_t - (\text{EBITDA}_t - \text{Royalties}_t)\Big)$$Across Pan African Resources' specific tax ring-fences, this margin expansion dictates a three-year transition from the 12.3% FY26 cash tax rate toward the 27.0% statutory corporate tax rate (and the ~30% gold formula and Australian statutory rates) between FY26 and FY28.In FY26 (the base year, characterized by a $12.3\%$ cash tax rate versus a $33.2\%$ accounting ETR), PAF's accounting effective tax rate rose to $33.2\%$—up from $27.0\%$ in FY25 and $28.0\%$ in FY24—because higher projected life-of-mine profitability ratios ($X$) under the gold mining tax formula ($Y = 33 - \frac{165}{X}$) required an upward revaluation of deferred tax liabilities on South African mining assets, mirroring the gold formula deferred tax rate adjustments seen across the sector. However, cash tax paid remained suppressed at $12.3\%$ because cash taxes were remitted almost exclusively by Barberton Mines (Fairview, Sheba, Consort, and BTRP) and Elikhulu, whose historical construction capex had already been fully redeemed. Conversely, MTR (which generated $\sim\text{US\$140–160 million}$ in annualized EBITDA at an AISC of $\sim\text{US\$1,000–1,282/oz}$) used its initial construction UCR pool ($\sim\text{US\$135–150 million}$) to shield roughly $70\%\text{–}80\%$ of its FY26 taxable profit. Simultaneously, Evander Mines offset its underground operating profits against its brought-forward UCR pool plus $\text{US\$66.1 million}$ of FY26 capital expenditure, comprising $\text{US\$48.8 million}$ in expansion capital and $\text{US\$17.3 million}$ in sustaining capital.During FY27 (the inflection year, with a projected $19.5\%$ to $22.0\%$ cash tax rate), operating at $>55\%$ EBITDA margins causes MTR's remaining brought-forward UCR balance ($\sim\text{US\$30–40 million}$ entering July 2026) to be completely exhausted during Q1/Q2 FY27, within four to five months. Even after deducting FY27 capex for the $19\text{MW}$ MTR solar and BESS plant—which qualifies for $100\%$ upfront deduction under Section 12B or Section 36 of the ITA—MTR transitions into a full cash-tax-paying entity at the $27.0\%$ statutory corporate tax rate across H2 FY27. Eskom Tariff Escalation (NERSA MYPD6) vs. Behind-the-Meter Solar SavingsProjected NERSA Tariff Increases (2026–2028) and the February 2026 RAB RedeterminationThe trajectory of grid electricity costs for South African gold miners between 2026 and 2028 is governed by the National Energy Regulator of South Africa's (NERSA) Sixth Multi-Year Price Determination (MYPD6) covering Eskom's financial years 2025/26, 2026/27, and 2027/28, which take effect from 1 April to 31 March for direct non-local authority mining customers, and from 1 July to 30 June for municipal bulk supplies.In its initial MYPD6 revenue application, Eskom requested allowable revenues of $\text{R445,563 million}$ for 2025/26, $\text{R495,355 million}$ for 2026/27, and $\text{R536,778 million}$ for 2027/28, which corresponded to proposed standard tariff hikes of $36.15\%$, $11.81\%$, and $9.10\%$, respectively. On 30 January 2025, NERSA issued its original MYPD6 determination, disallowing $\text{R60.95 billion}$, $\text{R85.83 billion}$, and $\text{R99.92 billion}$ across the three years and approving baseline tariff increases of $12.74\%$ for 2025/26 (lifting the average standard tariff from $195.95\text{ c/kWh}$ to $220.92\text{ c/kWh}$ on 1 April 2025), $5.36\%$ for 2026/27, and $6.19\%$ for 2027/28.However, following a regulatory review of its asset valuation calculations, NERSA announced a pivotal re-determination of Eskom's Generation Regulatory Asset Base (RAB) on 8 February 2026. By strictly recalculating Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC), Transfers to Commercial Operation, Work Under Construction (WUC), Net Working Capital, Asset Purchases, Depreciation, and Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) returns using Eskom's original MYPD6 submission data, NERSA awarded Eskom an additional $\text{R54,734 million}$ in allowable revenue. To prevent severe macroeconomic shocks—where full two-year liquidation ("Option 1") would have added $+6.86\%$ to 2026/27 tariffs and eliminated $41,104$ job opportunities—NERSA adopted a phased recovery ("Option 2"), liquidating $\text{R35,013 million}$ during the remainder of MYPD6 and deferring the remaining $\text{R19,721 million}$ beyond 2027/28 into MYPD7.For the 2026/27 tariff year (effective 1 April 2026, Eskom FY27), the liquidation of $\text{R12,000 million}$ added $+3.40\%$ to the originally approved $5.36\%$ increase. As confirmed in Eskom's Schedule of Standard Prices for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, the final implemented increase for direct Eskom customers (including Megaflex mining supplies) is $8.76\%$, while municipal bulk purchases increase by $9.01\%$ effective 1 July 2026. This lifts the average standard Eskom tariff from $220.92\text{ c/kWh}$ to $240.27\text{ c/kWh}$ ($\sim\text{US\$0.141/kWh}$ at $\text{R17.00/US\$}$). In the 2027/28 tariff year (effective 1 April 2027, Eskom FY28), the liquidation of $\text{R23,013 million}$ adds $+2.64\%$ to the originally approved $6.19\%$ increase, resulting in a revised approved tariff increase of $8.83\%$ for 2027/28 and escalating the average standard tariff to $261.49\text{ c/kWh}$ ($\sim\text{US\$0.154/kWh}$). Historically, generation costs were recovered entirely through volumetric ($\text{c/kWh}$) active energy charges. Under the new structure, energy charges are unbundled into a legacy volumetric $\text{c/kWh}$ rate and a fixed $\text{R/kVA}$ Generation Capacity Charge (GCC), while service charges are levied per Point of Delivery (POD) rather than per account.In FY2027 (effective 1 April 2026), Eskom increased the fixed $\text{R/kVA}$ portion of the GCC from $20\%$ in FY2026 to $30\%$ in FY2027, leaving $70\%$ embedded within the volumetric $\text{c/kWh}$ energy charge. Crucially, Section 2.2 of Eskom's FY2027 tariff schedule mandates that the $70\%$ portion of the GCC recovered through the volumetric energy charge is excluded from the energy credit granted to customers under third-party grid wheeling and net-billing (offset) transactions.This regulatory mechanism creates a profound economic divergence between off-site wheeled renewable power and behind-the-meter (on-site self-consumption) solar PV. A mining company wheeling solar power over the Eskom grid forfeits the $70\%$ embedded GCC credit on every wheeled kilowatt-hour, continues to pay the $30\%$ fixed $\text{R/kVA}$ GCC, and incurs distribution and transmission use-of-system (DUoS/TUoS) network charges, ancillary service charges, and electrification and affordability subsidy charges. Conversely, because Pan African Resources constructed its solar plants directly behind the Eskom POD meter at Evander, Fairview (Barberton), and MTR, every kilowatt-hour consumed on-site avoids $100\%$ of the volumetric Megaflex TOU active energy rate, including the $70\%$ embedded GCC, transmission and distribution loss factors, ancillary service charges, and rural and affordability subsidy levies. Furthermore, integrating a $\text{30 MWh}$ Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) at the MTR $19\text{MW}$ solar plant enables PAF to dispatch stored solar power during Eskom's morning and evening Peak TOU periods—where Megaflex High-Demand Peak rates exceed $700\text{ c/kWh}$—and shave monthly maximum demand ($\text{kVA}$), directly reducing fixed network demand and GCC charges.Net Unit Cost Savings from Fairview (8.75MW), Evander, and MTR (19MW) Solar PlantsEmpirical operational data from Pan African Resources' financial disclosures provides a precise benchmark for the net unit cost savings achieved across its South African solar fleet. Tennant Creek / Warrego IOCG Copper-Gold Smelter Terms (TC/RCs)Strategic & Geological Context: Consolidation of the Tennant Creek Mineral FieldPan African Resources established its Australian operational hub through the $\text{US\$54.2 million}$ acquisition of Tennant Consolidated Mining Group (TCMG) in late 2024, followed on 9 March 2026 by the agreed $\text{A\$311 million}$ ($\sim\text{R3.6 billion}$) all-scrip takeover of joint venture partner Emmerson Resources Limited (ASX: ERM) and a strategic equity stake in CuFe Ltd (ASX: CUF). This consolidation grants PAF $100\%$ control over $1,700\text{ km}^2$ of the Tennant Creek Mineral Field (TCMF) in the Northern Territory—historically one of Australia's highest-grade gold and copper provinces—and eliminates the $6\%$ Net Smelter Return (NSR) production royalty previously payable to Emmerson across the Small Mines Joint Venture (SMJV) tenements.Development of the field follows a two-stage hub-and-spoke processing architecture. Under Stage 1 (Free-Milling Gold CIL Hub at the Nobles Plant), which was commissioned in May 2025 with a nameplate capacity of $840\text{ ktpa}$, the carbon-in-leach (CIL) facility treats crown pillar, stockpiled, and open-pit or underground gold-dominant ores from Nobles, White Devil (which hosts an Indicated Resource of $>3.0\text{ Mt}$ at $3.73\text{ g/t Au}$ for $378\text{ koz}$), Mauretania ($256\text{ kt}$ at $3.5\text{ g/t Au}$), Golden Forty, and Chariot, delivering $\sim 34,000\text{ oz Au}$ in FY26. Under Stage 2 (Warrego IOCG Copper-Gold Sulfide Expansion), development is underpinned by the $100\%$-owned Warrego Project—the largest Iron Oxide Copper-Gold (IOCG) deposit in the district with a Mineral Resource of $16.5\text{ Mt}$ at $1.3\%\text{ Cu}$ and $1.1\text{ g/t Au}$—alongside regional alliance resources including CuFe's Orlando deposit and Tennant Minerals' Bluebird high-grade Cu-Au-Bi discovery. Executing Stage 2 requires $\text{US\$40–50 million}$ in capital to add a dedicated sulfide flotation and gravity circuit alongside the Nobles CIL plant, scaling hub production toward $\sim 100,000\text{ oz/yr Au}$ plus payable copper-gold concentrates.Mineralogy and Metallurgical Flowsheet of Tennant Creek IOCG ConcentratesTennant Creek IOCG orebodies occur as structurally controlled, ellipsoidal magnetite-hematite-quartz-chlorite ironstone pipes hosted within the Palaeoproterozoic Warramunga Formation. Primary hypogene sulfide and precious metal mineralization consists of chalcopyrite ($\text{CuFeS}_2$) and coarse-to-fine native gold ($\text{Au}$), intimately associated with bismuthinite ($\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$) and complex Cu-Bi-Pb-Se sulfosalts (such as emplectite and wittichenite), with localized zones of cobalt (cobaltite/linnaeite) and arsenic (loellingite/arsenopyrite). Historically, Warrego produced $30,657\text{ tons}$ of copper concentrate and $58,740\text{ oz Au}$ in 1983–1984, accounted for $95\%$ of Australia's bismuth output in the 1970s, and exported concentrates grading at least $26.5\%\text{ Cu}$ to Japanese smelters.Recent metallurgical testwork across Warrego, Orlando (CuFe's July 2025 and May 2026 Scoping Studies), and Bluebird (Tennant Minerals) defines a three-product beneficiation pathway designed to maximize payability and neutralize smelter impurity penalties. First, an upfront coarse gravity gold scalp leverages the fact that Tennant Creek native gold is partially liberated at primary grind sizes, allowing a gravity circuit to recover $35\%\text{–}65\%$ of the contained gold ahead of flotation. In PAF's integrated Warrego-Nobles hub, this gravity concentrate can be leached directly on-site in the Nobles CIL circuit via intensive cyanidation to produce gold doré ($>99.5\%$ payable with zero smelter deduction), or sold directly as a high-grade gravity concentrate. Second, following gravity recovery, froth flotation of chalcopyrite produces a primary copper-gold sulfide flotation concentrate grading $24.0\%$ to $29.0\%\text{ Cu}$ (with Warrego targeting its historical benchmark of $\sim 26.5\%\text{ Cu}$) and $1.5\text{ to }15.0+\text{ g/t Au}$, ranging from $1.5\text{–}4.0\text{ g/t Au}$ at Bluebird where copper-to-gold ratios are higher to $6.0\text{–}18.0\text{ g/t Au}$ at Warrego and Orlando depending on the proportion of gold scalped to gravity and CIL. Third, the flowsheet incorporates selective bismuth depression or bismuth concentrate separation: in uncleaned bulk sulfide flotation, bismuthinite ($\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$) floats readily alongside chalcopyrite, elevating bismuth levels in the copper concentrate to $0.20\%\text{–}1.50\%\text{ Bi}$ ($2,000\text{–}15,000\text{ ppm}$)—well above standard copper smelter penalty thresholds. As demonstrated in CuFe's expanded May 2026 Orlando Scoping Study and Tennant Minerals' Bluebird PFS updates, modifying the cleaner flotation reagent scheme (or applying a selective bismuth leach and flotation split) separates a dedicated bismuth-rich concentrate from the main copper-gold-silver concentrate, limiting bismuth penalties in traditional copper smelting while monetizing bismuth as a critical mineral byproduct.Benchmark Smelter Terms: TC/RCs, Deductibles, Payables, and Impurity Penalties (2026–2028)High-grade Australian IOCG concentrates from the Tennant Creek district have two primary commercial offtake routes: containerization or bulk rotainer transport north via the Adelaide–Darwin railway to Darwin Port for ocean export to North Asian custom smelters in China, Japan, and South Korea, or road haulage $488\text{ km}$ east along the Barkly Highway to Glencore's Mount Isa copper smelter in Queensland.Because global copper mine supply growth has lagged behind a substantial expansion in Chinese custom smelting capacity during 2025–2026, commercial concentrate terms have shifted sharply in favor of mine producers. In contemporary 2026 scoping and feasibility studies (such as the September 2026 Horden Lake and peer IOCG benchmarks), combined concentrate transport plus TC/RC charges are modeled at just $\text{US\$0.15 to US\$0.27/lb CuEq}$.1. Treatment Charges (TC) and Copper Refining Charges (RC)While historical long-term bankable feasibility studies utilized a conservative structural assumption of $\text{US\$65.00–80.00/dmt}$ (dry metric tonne of concentrate) for Treatment Charge (TC) and $\text{US\$0.065–0.080/lb}$ ($6.5\text{–}8.0\text{ US cents/lb}$) of payable copper for Refining Charge (RC), tight Asian concentrate markets in 2026–2028 have compressed annual benchmark TC/RCs to $\text{US\$20.00–35.00/dmt}$ TC and $\text{US\$0.020–0.035/lb}$ RC (with spot clean concentrate parcels trading at $\text{US\$0–10/dmt}$), reducing total transport and TC/RC deductions to $\text{US\$0.15–0.27/lb CuEq}$. Price participation clauses have been completely eliminated from standard Asian smelter contracts.2. Payable Copper (Cu) & Minimum Smelter DeductionStandard Asian custom smelter contracts specify a gross payable copper percentage of $96.50\%$ to $96.75\%$ of contained copper content, subject to a mandatory minimum deduction of $1.0\text{ unit}$ ($1.0\%\text{ Cu}$), which occasionally widens to $1.1\text{ units}$ for concentrates grading below $25\%\text{ Cu}$. Because of the $1.0\%\text{ Cu}$ minimum unit deduction, net effective copper payability scales directly with concentrate copper grade across the Tennant Creek specification range. At $24.0\%\text{ Cu}$ (the lower end of the Bluebird and Orlando concentrate range), the $1.0\text{ unit}$ deduction yields $\frac{24.0\% - 1.0\%}{24.0\%} = \mathbf{95.83\%}$ effective payable copper ($230.0\text{ kg}$ payable Cu per dmt). At $26.5\%\text{ Cu}$ (Warrego's historical export target grade), net effective payability rises to $\frac{26.5\% - 1.0\%}{26.5\%} = \mathbf{96.23\%}$ ($255.0\text{ kg}$ payable Cu per dmt). At $29.0\%\text{ Cu}$ (the upper end of Tennant Creek chalcopyrite concentrates), the unit deduction formula yields $\frac{29.0\% - 1.0\%}{29.0\%} = 96.55\%$, which is capped by the contract sliding scale at $96.50\%\text{–}96.65\%$ ($279.9\text{–}280.3\text{ kg}$ payable Cu per dmt).3. Payable Gold (Au) Sliding Scale, Deduction & Refining ChargeFor gold contained in copper concentrates, Chinese and South Korean smelters apply a standard minimum deduction of $1.0\text{ g/t Au}$ per dry metric tonne of concentrate, whereas Japanese smelters (such as Mitsubishi Materials or Sumitomo Metal Mining) occasionally negotiate a reduced $0.5\text{ to }0.8\text{ g/t Au}$ deduction for high-purity Australian IOCG concentrates. Above the minimum threshold, payable gold is governed by a grade-dependent sliding scale, with a Gold Refining Charge (Au RC) of $\text{US\$5.00 to US\$6.00/oz}$ of payable gold. When concentrate gold grades fall below $1.0\text{ g/t Au}$, payability is $0.0\%$ as the smelter retains $100\%$ of the contained gold. In the $1.0\text{ to }3.0\text{ g/t Au}$ tier—typical of heavily gravity-scalped concentrates or low-gold zones at Bluebird grading $1.5\text{–}4.0\text{ g/t Au}$—the smelter deducts $1.0\text{ g/t Au}$ or pays $90.0\%$ of contained gold, whichever results in a lower payable ounce total. This creates a severe low-grade smelter deduction penalty: at $1.5\text{ g/t Au}$ in concentrate, a $1.0\text{ g/t}$ fixed deduction leaves only $\frac{1.5 - 1.0}{1.5} = \mathbf{33.3\%}$ payable gold, and at $2.5\text{ g/t Au}$, payability is only $\frac{2.5 - 1.0}{2.5} = \mathbf{60.0\%}$. This mathematical penalty explains why Pan African Resources' integration of the Nobles CIL plant to leach gravity-recoverable gold into doré on-site—while floating only high-grade gold-in-chalcopyrite at $>8\text{–}15\text{ g/t Au}$—is vital to maximizing net smelter returns. As concentrate gold grades increase, payability rises to $90.0\%\text{–}92.0\%$ for $3.0\text{ to }5.0\text{ g/t Au}$, reaches $93.0\%\text{–}95.0\%$ for $5.0\text{ to }10.0\text{ g/t Au}$ (aligning directly with the $93.0\%$ payable gold IOCG feasibility study benchmark), advances to $95.0\%\text{–}96.5\%$ for $10.0\text{ to }20.0\text{ g/t Au}$ in semi-scalped Warrego Cu-Au concentrates, and reaches $96.5\%\text{–}97.5\%$ for concentrates exceeding $20.0\text{ g/t Au}$.4. Byproduct Credits (Silver, Bismuth, Cobalt, Magnetite) and Deleterious Element PenaltiesByproduct credits and deleterious impurity penalties play a decisive role in the commercial valuation of Tennant Creek IOCG concentrates. Silver (Ag) is paid at $90.0\%$ of contained metal, subject to a minimum threshold deduction of $30.0\text{ g/t Ag}$ (below which payability is $0\%$) and a Silver Refining Charge of $\text{US\$0.40–0.50/oz}$ of payable Ag. While polymetallic zones such as Orlando yield $>30\text{ g/t Ag}$ in concentrate, classic Warrego ironstone ore is relatively silver-poor ($5\text{–}25\text{ g/t Ag}$ in concentrate), meaning silver frequently falls below the $30\text{ g/t}$ payable floor unless blended.Bismuth (Bi) represents the primary metallurgical and commercial swing factor across the Tennant Creek field. Within a standard copper concentrate, bismuth causes severe cathode embrittlement during copper electrorefining, prompting Asian custom smelters to enforce a strict free limit of $200\text{ to }500\text{ ppm}$ ($0.02\%\text{ to }0.05\%\text{ Bi}$), which relaxes to $800\text{–}1,000\text{ ppm}$ ($0.08\%\text{–}0.10\%\text{ Bi}$) at specialized complex smelters or under domestic blending at Mount Isa. Above this threshold, smelters levy a penalty of $\text{US\$1.50 to US\$2.50/dmt}$ per $100\text{ ppm}$ ($0.01\%$) of excess Bi, with cargo rejection limits typically triggered at $1,500\text{ to }2,000\text{ ppm}$ ($0.15\%\text{–}0.20\%\text{ Bi}$). Conversely, when selective flotation or chemical leaching isolates bismuthinite into a separate bismuth-rich concentrate grading $15\%\text{–}40\%\text{ Bi}$ (often carrying co-enriched gold and silver), specialized bismuth refiners in China and Southeast Asia pay $60\%\text{ to }75\%$ of the contained bismuth value after a $1.5\%\text{–}2.0\%$ unit deduction, converting a potential $\text{US\$15–30/dmt}$ copper smelter penalty into a net positive critical mineral revenue stream.Among secondary deleterious elements, arsenic (As)—which accompanies gold and copper mineralization in parts of the field—is subject to a strict $0.50\%\text{ As}$ ($5,000\text{ ppm}$) Chinese customs import prohibition, with smelter free limits of $0.10\%\text{–}0.20\%\text{ As}$ ($1,000\text{–}2,000\text{ ppm}$) and penalties of $\text{US\$2.00–3.00/dmt}$ per $0.10\%$ ($1,000\text{ ppm}$) excess As between $0.20\%$ and $0.50\%$. Selenium (Se), which was historically recovered as a commercial byproduct from Warrego smelter flue dusts, carries no byproduct credit in modern exported copper concentrates and faces a smelter free limit of $300\text{–}500\text{ ppm}$ ($0.03\%\text{–}0.05\%\text{ Se}$), penalized at $\text{US\$1.00–1.50/dmt}$ per $100\text{ ppm}$ excess. Synthesis and Strategic ConclusionsWith South African gold miners operating under the statutory $Y = 33 - \frac{165}{X}$ formula and realized gold prices exceeding $\text{US\$3,800/oz}$, Group EBITDA margins above $55\%$ are compressing the multi-year tax holiday historically provided by Section 36 unredeemed capital expenditure pools. For Pan African Resources, rapid depletion of the Mogale Tailings Retreatment (MTR) capital shield in H1 FY27 and the Evander Mines shield by H1 FY28 will drive a structural step-up in the Group's cash tax rate from $12.3\%$ in FY26 to $19.5\%\text{–}22.0\%$ in FY27 and $26.5\%\text{–}28.5\%$ in FY28, aligning with the $27.0\%$ corporate tax rate on surface operations and $\sim 30.0\%$ formula and Australian statutory rates. Accelerating qualifying capital deployments—specifically the Soweto Cluster tailings expansion at MTR, the $19.7\text{ MW}$ Evander and $19.0\text{ MW}$ MTR solar plants, and the $\text{US\$40–50 million}$ Warrego Stage 2 sulfide circuit—will be critical to moderating cash tax outflows across FY27–FY28.Simultaneously, NERSA's 8 February 2026 Generation RAB redetermination ($\text{R35.01 billion}$ MYPD6 liquidation) increased Eskom's approved direct tariff hikes from $5.36\%$ to $8.76\%$ in 2026/27 and from $6.19\%$ to $8.83\%$ in 2027/28, while Eskom's unbundling of the Generation Capacity Charge (GCC) penalizes third-party grid wheeling. Because Pan African's solar plants at Fairview ($8.75\text{ MW}$), Evander ($9.975\text{ MW}$ Phase 1 and $19.7\text{ MW}$ Phase 2), and MTR ($19.0\text{ MW}$ plus $30\text{ MWh}$ BESS) are situated behind the meter, their net cash unit savings expand directly with Eskom's tariff hikes—rising from $\sim 205\text{ c/kWh}$ ($\text{US\$0.107–0.120/kWh}$) in FY26 to $\sim 245\text{–}280\text{ c/kWh}$ ($\text{US\$0.144–0.165/kWh}$) by FY28 and generating $\text{US\$18.0–19.9 million}$ in annual cash savings once the $57.43\text{ MW}$ South African fleet is fully commissioned.In Australia, following the $\text{A\$311 million}$ consolidation of Emmerson Resources in March 2026, Pan African's $100\%$ ownership of the Tennant Creek mineral field allows Warrego ($16.5\text{ Mt}$ at $1.3\%\text{ Cu}$, $1.1\text{ g/t Au}$) to leverage the existing $840\text{ ktpa}$ Nobles CIL plant. By recovering coarse native gold via gravity concentration and intensive CIL leaching into doré on-site, and utilizing selective bismuth flotation and depression to keep bismuth below $500\text{ ppm}$ in the main $26.5\%\text{ Cu}$ concentrate while monetizing a separate bismuth-rich byproduct stream, PAF eliminates the $1.0\text{ g/t Au}$ minimum deduction loss on low-gold concentrates and captures historically low Asian custom smelter TC/RCs of $\text{US\$20–35/dmt}$ and $\text{US\$0.020–0.035/lb}$.
