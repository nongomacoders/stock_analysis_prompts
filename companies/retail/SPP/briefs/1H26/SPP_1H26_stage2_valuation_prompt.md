## 1. EXECUTIVE FORENSIC AUDIT SUMMARY

**True economic performance.** SPP's H1 FY2026 is a tale of two halves that the headline numbers obscure. On a **comparable 26-week basis**, continuing operations delivered revenue of R67.5bn (+2.1%), but **operating profit collapsed 47.8% to R730.7m** (margin 1.1% vs 2.1%). The decline is *not* a single story: the presentation's own bridge (Slide 25) decomposes the R670m operating profit decline into **Black Friday overspend (−R212m), KZN operational failure (−R123m), Build-it impairments/closure (−R66m), legacy credit-risk policy change (−R143m), other credit risk (−R73m), and trading impacts (−R86m), partly offset by +R27m**. Critically, **R216m of the decline is "legacy matters" (credit-risk policy change + other credit risk)** — i.e., a *catch-up provisioning event*, not a recurring trading loss. Stripping out the one-off Black Friday, KZN, impairment and legacy credit items, underlying trading profit was roughly **R1.0bn** — down but not catastrophic.

**The real red flag is cash, not profit.** The group generated **negative free cash flow of −R2.0bn** (vs +R693.6m prior), driven overwhelmingly by a **R1.84bn working-capital outflow** — specifically a **R1.66bn reduction in trade payables** (the "creditor payment run on 30/31 March" timing issue flagged on Slide 28). This is the single most important forensic finding: **the balance sheet was flattered at the reporting date by payables that had not yet been paid.** Cash and cash equivalents fell from R2.74bn to R584.5m, and the group is now running on **R166.5m of net cash after banking facilities** — a razor-thin liquidity cushion.

**Balance sheet health.** Net debt of **R7.34bn**, gearing **2.73x** (vs 2.81x prior, covenant <3.5x) — *improved* on the surface, but only because EBITDA is measured on a trailing basis that still captures the stronger H2 FY2025. The **G&L debtors' book is deteriorating**: overdues up from 13.6% to **15.9%**, specific impairment charge up from R112.5m to **R273.1m** (+143%). This is the leading indicator of retailer distress that will feed through to future bad debts.

**Post-balance-sheet liquidity runway.** The **UK disposal (A.F. Blakemore, signed 4 May 2026)** is the critical de-risking event: gross proceeds ~£13m, net broadly breakeven, removing ~£5m of forecast H2 trading losses and the last non-core drag. Combined with the **R5.57bn debt headroom** and **R6.64bn total available funding**, the group has adequate near-term liquidity — *provided* the payables timing reverses and the KZN recovery (3 consecutive months of profit claimed) holds.

**Key findings:**
1. **Profit quality is poor but the decline is largely explained by identifiable, mostly non-recurring items** — the market's −56% HEPS reaction conflates a provisioning catch-up with a structural earnings collapse.
2. **Cash conversion is the true stress point** — negative FCF, payables-timing flattered, and a dividend that remains suspended (correctly).
3. **The debtors' book is the forward risk** — overdues and specific impairments are accelerating, and the "credit risk policy change" of R143m suggests prior provisioning was inadequate.
4. **The UK exit completes portfolio simplification** and removes the last discontinued-operations drag, but at effectively zero net proceeds.
5. **KZN recovery is management's central claim and the key swing factor** — 3 months of positive operating profit is asserted but not independently verifiable from the interim accounts.

## 2. DETAILED FORENSIC REASONING & DETERMINATIONS

### 2.1 Merchandise vs Credit/Other Revenue — the "Other Revenue" Surge

**Raw claim:** Total revenue R68.59bn, of which merchandise R67.48bn and "other" R1.11bn. Other revenue grew **+52%** (R730.2m → R1,109.6m).

**Forensic trace:** Note 3 disaggregates "other" into **marketing and service revenues R839.5m (+78%)**, franchise fees R127.1m, other services R143.0m. The marketing/service revenue surge is the **supplier-income/rebate** line — precisely the mechanism the Black Friday slide (Slide 7) admits was optimised "for supplier income rather than shopper footfall." 

**Determination:** The +52% "other revenue" growth is **not a quality earnings stream** — it is the accounting reflection of the very supplier-rebate optimisation that destroyed gross margin. It is **partially offsetting** the gross-profit decline (GP fell only 0.6% to R7,134m on the presentation's comparable basis, vs the statutory R7,111.7m). A naive reader sees "revenue up 2.1%, GP resilient" — the forensic reality is that **merchandise gross margin was sacrificed to book supplier income**, shifting profit from a sustainable line to a lower-quality, non-recurring one. **Verified: low-quality revenue mix shift.**

### 2.2 Gross Profit Margin Bridge

**Raw claim:** Statutory GP margin 10.5% (R7,111.7m / R67,482.5m); presentation "comparable" GP margin 10.6% vs 10.7%.

**Forensic trace:** Southern Africa GP margin fell from **9.8% to 9.5%** (Appendix 1), driven by "product mix and promotional activity supporting Black Friday and Christmas." Ireland GP margin *improved* 20bps to 13.7% (vaping regulatory switch, alcohol mix, tobacco). The group margin was held up by **Ireland's outperformance masking SA's erosion**.

**Determination:** The margin story is **geographically divergent**. SA G&L is the problem child (KZN + Black Friday + promotional intensity); Ireland is the stabiliser. The **Feb–Mar GP growth turned positive (+2.3%)** per Slide 21 — the first genuine evidence of margin recovery, but only two months of data. **Verified: SA margin erosion real; Ireland genuinely resilient; recovery nascent and unproven.**

### 2.3 Debtors' Book Health & IFRS 9 ECL Provisioning — **THE CRITICAL JUNCTION**

**Raw claim:** G&L debtor book R8.9bn, overdues 15.9% (vs 13.6%), specific impairment charge R273.1m (vs R112.5m).

**Forensic trace:** This is where the forensic trail is most revealing. The operating profit bridge (Slide 25) isolates **"Credit risk policy change −R143m"** and **"Credit risk other −R73m"** = **R216m of legacy credit provisions**. Note 5 (cash flow) shows "movement in provisions raised against trade receivables and loans" of **R150.7m** (vs R45.5m). Appendix 1 explicitly states: *"ECL, debtors write-offs and provisions include additional provisions of R273m (mainly KZN and SR) and R58m of write-offs in G&L."*

**Determination:** The **R143m "credit risk policy change" is an admission that prior-period ECL provisioning was inadequate** — a retrospective tightening of the IFRS 9 methodology. This is a **red flag on prior-period earnings quality**: the FY2025 and H1 FY2025 numbers were overstated by under-provisioning. The overdues jump to 15.9% and the tripling of the specific impairment charge confirm **genuine, accelerating retailer distress**, concentrated in KZN and "SR" (likely a specific retailer group). The mitigants listed (credit committee, early-warning indicators, security monitoring) are **process claims, not outcome evidence**. **Verified: debtors' book materially deteriorating; prior provisioning inadequate; forward bad-debt risk elevated. This is the single largest threat to H2 earnings.**

### 2.4 Store Network Capex and Trading Density

**Raw claim:** Capex R659m group (R373m SA, €15m Ireland); "capex target ~1% of revenue."

**Forensic trace:** Note 11: additions to PPE/intangible R394.0m; capital commitments R1,312.4m (contracted R427.5m). Slide 33 splits capex: **expansion 47% (incl. R118m Perrys acquisition), replacement 43%, strategic 10%**. The Perrys Cash & Carry acquisition (R118.1m, Note 6) generated **R149.8m of goodwill on a R12.2m tangible asset base** — i.e., **92% of the purchase price was goodwill**, and Note 7 discloses **R47.4m goodwill impairment** on two acquired Hajimarkos stores (The Square, 2nd Avenue) within months of acquisition.

**Determination:** Capex discipline is real (1% of revenue target held), but **acquisition quality is questionable** — paying R162m for stores that immediately require R47.4m goodwill impairment, and R118m for a cash & carry whose net tangible assets were R12.2m. The **Build-it imports warehouse closure (R27.5m)** and **GiCom software full impairment (R30.7m)** confirm **capital misallocation in prior periods**. **Verified: capex controlled, but M&A and prior strategic capex have destroyed value; trading density not disclosed at store level (a disclosure gap).**

### 2.5 IFRS 16 Lease Liability Structure

**Raw claim:** Non-current lease liability R6,930.4m; current R1,489.8m; total R8,420.2m. Right-of-use assets R2,654.4m.

**Forensic trace:** The **lease liability (R8.42bn) is 3.2x the ROU asset (R2.65bn)** — a structural feature of the voluntary-trade model where SPP leases properties to sub-lease to retailers (lease receivable R4.08bn non-current + R939.7m current = R5.02bn). Note 7 discloses **ROU asset impairments of R26.9m** (three SA stores + one Irish vacant property). The **lease receivable (R5.02bn) vs lease liability (R8.42bn)** gap of R3.4bn represents the net property-cost exposure the group carries.

**Determination:** The IFRS 16 structure is **broadly stable** (liabilities down slightly from R8.54bn at FY2025), but the **ROU impairments signal store-level distress** consistent with the debtors' book deterioration. The lease receivable is a **quasi-credit exposure to retailers** — if retailers fail, both the debtor book *and* the lease receivable are at risk. **Verified: lease structure stable but correlated credit risk with debtors' book; monitor ROU impairments as leading indicator.**

### 2.6 Post-Year-End Trading & SENS Disclosures

**Raw claim:** Note 13 / Slide 35: UK disposal to A.F. Blakemore, APA signed 4 May 2026, ~£13m gross, net broadly breakeven, completion June–Sept 2026, ~£5m forecast H2 UK losses.

**Forensic trace:** The UK was classified as held-for-sale with **R1,266.9m assets / R1,396.2m liabilities** (net liability position). The disposal **removes a net liability** — economically positive despite zero net cash. Slide 41 asserts "early signs of progress albeit y-o-y growth impacted by Easter timing" post-period. The **SARB +25bps (May 2026)** and **ECB +50bps expected** are confirmed H2 headwinds (Slide 38).

**Determination:** The UK exit is **genuinely the final portfolio-simplification step** and removes ~£5m H2 losses — a real, if modest, positive. The post-period trading commentary is **qualitative and unverifiable**; "Easter timing" is a plausible but convenient excuse. **Verified: UK disposal de-risks; post-period trading claims unproven; H2 macro headwinds (rates, fuel) confirmed and material.**

### 2.7 Cash Conversion & Dividend Payout — **THE SECOND CRITICAL JUNCTION**

**Raw claim:** Operating cash flow **−R974.0m** (vs +R1,346.9m); FCF **−R2,011.9m** (vs +R693.6m); dividend suspended.

**Forensic trace:** Note 5: cash generated from operations **−R297.3m**, driven by **net working capital change of −R1,839.7m**, of which **trade payables −R1,841.7m**. Slide 28 explicitly flags *"creditor payment run on 30/31 March"* — i.e., **the reporting date (27 March) fell just before a major payables settlement**, inflating the payables balance and flattering the cash position by roughly R1.8bn. The **R1.66bn payables outflow** in the cash flow statement is the reversal of this timing.

**Determination:** This is the **most important forensic adjustment**. The reported cash of R584.5m and the "improved" gearing of 2.73x are **both flattered by payables timing**. Normalising for the payment run, **true operating cash flow is deeply negative** and the **underlying net debt position is worse than reported**. The dividend suspension is **correctly justified** ("free cash flow negative, earnings not yet restored, gearing elevated" — Slide 34). A naive reader seeing "gearing improved to 2.73x" would miss that **the improvement is an accounting timing artifact, not a deleveraging achievement**. **Verified: cash conversion is the group's acute stress point; payables timing flattered the balance sheet; dividend suspension appropriate; H2 must demonstrate genuine FCF reversal or liquidity tightens materially.**
