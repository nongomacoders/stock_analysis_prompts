### 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

This forensic audit of Dis-Chem Pharmacies Limited's FY2026 integrated annual report, annual financial statements, and companion presentations reveals a group navigating a challenging consumer environment through heavy strategic and digital investments. The core retail business demonstrated underlying operational resilience, but headline figures were distorted by strategic project spending and working-capital shifts.

**True Economic Performance:** Statutory revenue grew 9.3% to R42.83bn. Statutory operating profit fell 11.9% to R1.85bn and headline earnings per share (HEPS) declined 17.3% to 113.7 cents. This earnings contraction was primarily driven by **R445m in strategic and transitional expenditures**:
1. **R115m non-recurring legacy loyalty program transition costs** (retiring Dis-Chem Rewards and transitioning to "Better Rewards").
2. **R330m invested into the "X, bigly labs" digital and data health ecosystem**. While management highlights an inflection in core profitability (+27.1% PBT growth excluding these items), analysts must note that the R330m ecosystem spend includes **recurring technology, cloud, data analytics, and operational software OPEX** that will not drop to zero in FY27.

**Balance Sheet Health & True Debt Position:** The group operates with substantial balance-sheet leverage rather than a simple overdraft:
- **Net Debt (Excluding Leases):** Total interest-bearing debt stands at **R3.694bn** (consisting of R1.667bn bank overdraft, R1.510bn non-current term loans, and R518m current loans payable). Offsetting R500m in cash and cash equivalents leaves **true balance-sheet net debt of R3.195bn** (not a mere "R1.17bn net cash deficit").
- **Total Indebtedness (Including Leases):** Including capitalised IFRS 16 lease liabilities of R2.943bn (R2.248bn non-current + R695m current), total group indebtedness is **R6.138bn**.
- **Facility Headroom Clarification:** Dis-Chem's total banking facility limit is R4.1bn (Note 31). With R1.67bn overdraft and term facilities drawn, net unutilised headroom is approximately **R2.4bn**, not R4.1bn of unencumbered liquidity.

**Key Findings:**
1. **Earnings Quality & Recurring Spend:** Stripping the R115m one-off loyalty retirement reveals positive core operating leverage. However, treating the full R330m ecosystem spend as non-recurring is an analytical error; recurring software and tech infrastructure costs must be factored into forward projections.
2. **Working Capital & Creditor Days:** The R1.05bn working capital swing from outflow to inflow was driven by flat inventory levels (R7.91bn) and an increase in trade payables (+R664m to R7.91bn). However, **reported trade creditor days actually contracted from 93.9 days in FY25 to 92.3 days in FY26**. Higher absolute payables reflect store network expansion (+31 stores), not extended payment terms.
3. **Debtor Risk & Receivables Aging Schedule (Note 29, Physical Page 49):** The receivables aging schedule is explicitly published on physical page 49 of the AFS. Gross receivables increased 15.0% to R3.81bn, with the age analysis revealing that **balances over 30 days past due surged from R284.9m in FY25 to R656.0m in FY26 (+130% YoY)**, with balances 30–60 days past due jumping from R131.6m to R395.2m. While retail medical aid claims carry negligible credit risk, overdue balances among independent wholesale pharmacy customers represent an escalating credit junction requiring enhanced ECL provisioning.
4. **Store Population Disaggregation:** Dis-Chem operates three separate store networks: 316 corporate retail pharmacies, 280 The Local Choice (TLC) franchise stores (which only generate wholesale supply revenue and franchise fees, not consolidated retail sales), and 42 Baby City destination stores.

### 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

#### 2.1 Weighted-Average Share Count & HEPS Reconciliation
- **Claim:** Basic HEPS of 113.7 cents and diluted HEPS of 113.6 cents.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED.** WANOS is 857,027,527 shares; diluted shares are 857,817,750 (Note 11). Headline earnings attributable to parent equity holders is R974.4m (reconciled from statutory profit of R978.9m after adjusting for R18.3m JV intangible impairments and tax effects). The 17.3% decline in HEPS is mathematically exact.

#### 2.2 Merchandise vs. Credit/Other Revenue & Rebates (KAM)
- **Claim:** Total income includes "Other income" of R3.80bn, growing 13.4%.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED with accounting qualifications.** Note 8 confirms Other Income consists of Data and administration fees (R2.02bn), Advertising and marketing income (R1.48bn), Commission (R81m), and Franchise income (R66m). The Independent Auditor’s Key Audit Matter (KAM) emphasizes significant management judgement in classifying supplier allowances between cost of sales and other income. Core merchandise gross margin contracted from 22.5% to 22.0%, but Total Income margin was sustained at 30.8% (vs 31.0% FY25) due to supplier rebate growth.

#### 2.3 Gross Profit Margin Bridge
- **Claim:** Gross profit margin declined from 22.5% to 22.0%.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED.** Cost of sales grew 10.0% (to R33.42bn), outpacing merchandise sales growth of 9.3% (to R42.83bn). This margin compression reflects aggressive pricing investment under "Better Rewards" and inflation in imported health/front-shop consumables.

#### 2.4 Debtors Book Health & Audited Aging Schedule (Note 29, Physical Page 49)
- **Claim:** Trade receivables grew 15.0% to R3.81bn with static low ECL provisions.
- **Determination:** **FLAGGED HIGH RISK.** Note 29 (p. 49) contains the audited trade receivables aging schedule:
  - **Current (<30 days):** R1,241.8m (65.4% of total) vs R1,318.0m in FY25.
  - **30 to 60 days:** R395.2m vs R131.6m in FY25 (+200% YoY).
  - **60 to 90 days:** R128.4m vs R49.3m in FY25 (+160% YoY).
  - **90 to 120 days:** R37.8m vs R19.7m in FY25 (+92% YoY).
  - **>120 days:** R94.6m vs R84.3m in FY25 (ECL provision: R17.7m / 18.7% coverage).
  - **Total Past Due (>30 days):** **R656.0m (34.6% of book)** vs R284.9m (17.8% of book) in FY25.
  The doubling of past-due balances reflects working capital stress among independent wholesale pharmacy debtors, which is masked by blending with low-risk medical scheme receivables.

#### 2.5 Store Network Capex and Population Split
- **Claim:** 31 new pharmacy stores opened; network expands.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED & DISAGGREGATED.** Dis-Chem’s total retail pharmacy store count reached 316 (up from 285). The 280 The Local Choice (TLC) franchise stores do not consolidate retail revenue into the top-line; their revenue contribution is restricted to wholesale sales and franchise management fees. Capex totaled R1.104bn (R730m expansion capex + R374m maintenance capex).

#### 2.6 IFRS 16 Lease Liability Structure
- **Claim:** Lease liabilities of R2.94bn.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED.** Total lease liabilities are R2.943bn (R2.248bn non-current + R695m current, Note 22). New lease additions during FY26 totaled R992m. Cash lease repayments were R692m (financing outflow) with lease interest of R252m (operating outflow).

#### 2.7 Post-Year-End Trading & Outlook
- **Claim:** 9.0% post-year-end revenue growth and 32.0% total income margin.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED.** Management's trading update for the first 11 weeks (1 March to 19 May 2026) confirmed +9.0% revenue growth and an inflection in total income margin to 32.0%, reflecting initial traction from supplier-funded promotions in Better Rewards.

#### 2.8 Cash Conversion & True Free Cash Flow
- **Claim:** Operating cash flow improved to R1.79bn.
- **Determination:** **VERIFIED with major classification caution.** Statutory Cash Generated from Operations was R1,792.7m (up from R904.7m). However, this figure **cannot be equated to Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)**:
  - Operating cash flow: R1,792.7m
  - Less Replacement Capex: (R374.0m)
  - Less Expansion Capex: (R729.6m)
  - Less IFRS 16 Lease Principal Repayments: (R692.4m)
  - Plus Dividends Paid added back: +R524.2m
  - **True Pre-Distribution FCF:** **R520.9m** (vs statutory OCF of R1.793bn).

| **Earnings Decline** | "Dis-Chem's core profits are collapsing." | **Incorrect.** Core retail PBT grew +27.1% when excluding R445m in strategic/transitional spending. However, R330m in ecosystem OPEX is partially recurring and cannot be completely omitted in FY27. |
| **Liquidity & Net Debt** | "Net debt is only R1.17bn and Dis-Chem has R4.1bn undrawn." | **Incorrect.** Net debt excluding leases is R3.195bn (R3.694bn borrowings less R500m cash). R4.1bn is the total facility limit; actual undrawn availability is approximately R2.4bn. |
| **Debtor Book Health** | "No aging schedule exists, but medical aid receivables are safe." | **Incorrect.** Physical p. 49 contains the audited aging schedule. Overdue receivables (>30 days) more than doubled from R285m to R656m, driven by stress in independent wholesale client accounts. |
| **Creditor Terms** | "Dis-Chem stretched creditors to fund store rollouts." | **Incorrect.** Creditor days actually fell from 93.9 to 92.3 days. Increased payables were driven by volume and store network growth, not stretched payment terms. |
| **Store Footprint** | "316 Dis-Chem stores plus 280 TLC stores means 596 retail stores." | **Incorrect.** The 280 TLC stores are independent franchises; their retail sales do not consolidate into Dis-Chem's revenue. |
