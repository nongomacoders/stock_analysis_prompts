### 2. Footnote & Qualification Deep-Dive

- **No footnote markers (`[^1]`, `*`) appear on pp. 78–81.** The qualifications are embedded in narrative text and table notes:
  - **"The majority of the loan agreements already provide for the JIBAR rates to transition to ZARONIA, the remaining loan agreements will be amended before JIBAR ceases on 31 December 2026."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 78]` — This is a forward-looking qualification. It implies a minority of loan agreements still lack fallback language. The "no material impact" assertion rests on margin calibration replicating JIBAR pricing; this must be verified against actual facility terms in Note 23 (D006). If calibration is not exact, FY2027 finance costs could step up.
  - **"Any margin adjustments required as part of the transition will be calibrated to replicate the equivalent JIBAR based pricing, such that there is no material impact on the overall interest rates agreed."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 78]` — Management assertion, not evidenced by a build-up. Needs verification.
  - **"Dis-Chem has sufficient head room in both these ratios and does not expect these ratios to be defaulted on in the next 12 months."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 79]` — Qualitative only; no quantified headroom. The gearing ratio of 2.6x (2025: 2.3x) is disclosed but the covenant thresholds are not. This is a material gap for going-concern assessment.
  - **"There are no covenants in place for Investec or Supply Chain Finance."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 79]` — Reduces covenant risk on those facilities but also means no lender-imposed discipline; the SCF R1.7bn facility is uncommitted from a covenant perspective.
  - **"There were no material business combinations or foreign exchange differences or other non-cash transfers relating to the carrying amount of liabilities subject to this supply chain finance arrangement."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 79]` — Confirms SCF payables are pure cash-flow driven; no non-cash inflation of the R965m balance.
  - **"Trade receivables primarily relate to amounts recoverable from medical aids. These receivables are deemed to be low credit risk as medical schemes pay mainly within 14 days and amounts are neither past due nor impaired."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 81]` — Company-level assertion. No ageing table supplied to test it. If medical scheme payment behaviour has deteriorated (consistent with the "constrained consumer" narrative), this assertion could be stale.
  - **"The majority of other receivables and accrued income relates to rebates, promotions and advertising receivable from suppliers and are deemed to be low credit risk as the net position with the supplier is a creditor. Other receivables are currently measured in Stage 1 based on 12-month ECLs."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 81]` — The netting argument is a qualification: if supplier relationships terminate or rebate disputes arise, the "creditor net position" offset disappears and these receivables become exposed. All Stage 1 means no lifetime ECL recognised — potentially under-provisioned if supplier stress emerges.
  - **"The majority of the loans receivable are companies within the Dis-Chem Group and based on budgets prepared and current profitability will be able to repay the loans and therefore deemed to be a low credit risk."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 81]` — Company-level; these are eliminated on consolidation. The residual external loans (Uphawu, The Tailor) are expected repaid within 12 months but no ECL is raised.
  - **"Other loans mainly relate to the loans given to Uphawu and The Tailor, companies in the cut and trim industry, which is expected to be repaid within the next 12 months."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 81]` — No impairment assessment disclosed for these external loans.
  - **"A FEC liability of R3.7 million (2025: Rnil) is recognised at 28 February 2026."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 78]` — New FX derivative liability; small but signals hedging activity resumed.
  - **"If the exchange rate... increased or decreased by R2.00... impact... approximately R33.4 million (2025: R33.7 million)."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 78]` — FX sensitivity is material relative to PBT; must be modelled.
  - **"If the interest rate... increased or decreased by 200 average basis points... impact... approximately R58 million (2025: R59 million)."** `[D005, physical PDF p. 78]` — Interest rate sensitivity; implies average net floating debt of ~R2.9bn.
