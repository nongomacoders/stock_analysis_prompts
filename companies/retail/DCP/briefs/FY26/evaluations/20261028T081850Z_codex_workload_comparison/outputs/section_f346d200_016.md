### 2. Footnote & Qualification Deep-Dive

- **Trade receivables >120 days qualification** [D003, physical PDF p. 49]: "Trade receivables over 120 days in the current and prior year are mainly on payment plans and have been meeting the payment requirements and therefore not 100% provided for at year-end." This is a forward-looking collectibility assertion. It supports the 18.7% (Group) / 92.9% (Company) ECL rates on the >120 day bucket rather than 100% provisioning. **What must be verified:** the satisfactory-paid % and collection rate in note 29, and whether the payment-plan population is growing (Group >120 day gross rose R84,300k → R94,593k, +12.2%). If payment-plan balances are growing while ECL coverage stays flat, the forecast debtor cost line is understated.

- **Pledged receivables qualification** [D003, physical PDF p. 49]: "Trade and other receivables relating to CJ Pharmaceutical Enterprises Limited amounting to R400 million (2025: R400 million) were pledged as security for the revolving overdraft facilities with Standard Bank. Under the pledge the bank can claim debtors' receipts if CJ defaults on the bank overdraft." This is a contingent encumbrance on R400m of receivables — material relative to Group gross trade receivables of R1,897,879k (~21%). **What must be verified:** CJ Pharmaceutical's relationship to the Group (subsidiary? associate?), the size of the revolving overdraft it secures, and whether the pledge restricts liquidity in a stress scenario. This affects the going-concern / covenant assessment (Q-740992ca8c3945a3, Q-4497778bcc6a42f2).

- **Loans receivable — related-party concentration** [D003, physical PDF p. 50]: Group related-party loans R111,510k (2025: R95,478k) to BEESECDCP, Tony Ferguson, Sable and Dis-Chem Life; Company related-party loans R949,376k (2025: R861,985k), including a R502m loan to Dis-Chem Distribution at JIBAR+1.3%, repayable at end of 3 years. These are unsecured and repayable on demand. **What must be verified:** impairment assessment on these related-party loans (note 29 credit risk), and whether the R502m Distribution loan is eliminated on consolidation (it should be, since Distribution is a subsidiary — confirm).

- **JIBAR transition qualification** [D003, physical PDF p. 50]: JIBAR ceases 31 Dec 2026, replaced by ZARONIA; "any margin adjustments required as part of the transition will be calibrated to replicate the equivalent JIBAR based pricing, such that there is no material impact on the overall interest rates agreed." This is a forward-looking claim about FY2027 finance costs. **What must be verified:** whether the R502m Distribution loan and other JIBAR-linked facilities have documented ZARONIA fallback language, and whether the "no material impact" assertion holds for the Group's external debt (loans payable R2,027,578k per stored context).

- **Treasury shares / FSP qualification** [D003, physical PDF pp. 51–53]: Treasury shares are held for the FSP and measured at cost. FY2026 treasury purchases of R60.4m (1,761,003 shares at R34.20) are material relative to the R123,237k closing treasury balance. The FSP expense was R35,337k (2025: R29,628k). **What must be verified:** whether the November 2025 buyback at R34.20 was an on-market buyback or FSP share purchase, and whether it is dilutive or anti-dilutive to HEPS.
