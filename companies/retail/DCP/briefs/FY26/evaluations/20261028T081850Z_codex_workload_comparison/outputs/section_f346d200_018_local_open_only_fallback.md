### 2. Footnote and Footnote Analysis

- **Footnote `*` (on the roll-forward tables)** — "Approximately 100% (2025: 100%) of the opening balance has been recognised as revenue in the current year." [D003, physical PDF p. 59]
  - **Implication**: The deferred revenue liability is effectively a one-year revolving balance — essentially all of each year's opening balance converts to revenue within 12 months. This means the R86,912k FY2025 opening balance was almost fully released in FY2026, and the R34,242k closing balance will largely reverse in FY2027. For forecasting, the revenue contribution from the opening balance is highly predictable (~100%), but the *level* of the closing balance depends on points/vouchers issued during the year.
  - **What must be verified**: Whether the collapse in loyalty points issued (R279,042k→R196,770k, −29.5%) reflects a change in the loyalty programme (e.g., points no longer issued, scheme redesign, or shift to instant discounts) or a change in accounting estimate. This materially affects FY2027 revenue recognition and the "other revenue" line.

- **Redemption-rate assumption** — "Assumptions used to calculate the liability are based on current redemption rates at existing stores which are between 90% and 100% (2025: 90% and 100%)." [D003, physical PDF p. 59]
  - **Implication**: The 90–100% redemption assumption is unchanged year-on-year, so the liability fall is not an estimate change — it is a volume/redemption-timing effect. The narrow 90–100% band leaves little estimation headroom; a shift to the low end would reduce the liability further.
  - **What must be verified**: Whether the "low levels of unredeemed loyalty points" statement is consistent with the near-zero closing loyalty balance (R3,053k) — i.e., is the scheme still economically meaningful, or is it being wound down?

- **Gift voucher fair value** — "The fair value of gift vouchers which includes the expected redemption rate attributed to the vouchers, is deferred as a liability…" [D003, physical PDF p. 59]
  - **Implication**: Gift voucher liability fell R31,646k→R22,296k (−29.5%), with vouchers issued R216,897k (2025: R229,016k) and revenue recognised R226,247k (2025: R229,304k). Issuance fell 5.3% while redemptions were broadly flat — a modest net drain. This is a small, stable line but worth monitoring for breakage assumptions.

- **Company vs Group loyalty divergence** — Company loyalty points closing balance is nil (2025: R52,516k) while Group is R3,053k (2025: R45,625k). [D003, physical PDF p. 59]
  - **Implication**: The Company recognised R292,714k of loyalty revenue against R240,198k issued (a R52,516k release), fully draining its liability. This suggests the loyalty scheme is now operated at Group/subsidiary level rather than in the Company entity — a structural shift that should be confirmed against the segment/entity notes.
