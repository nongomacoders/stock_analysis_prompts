### Core Institutional Thesis: A Consumer Finance Book Disguised as a Furniture Chain

Lewis Group Limited (JSE: LEW) is economically a high-yield unsecured consumer lender and microinsurer attached to a physical furniture distribution network. In FY26, pure merchandise sales (R5,452.1m) generated only 52.8% of total revenue (R10,319.9m). Once merchandise gross profit (R2,382.0m) is measured against retail operating costs (R3,509.9m before debtor and insurance service expenses), the standalone physical retail operation runs a structural operating deficit of **R(1,127.9)m**. Group profitability is entirely generated by the **R4,867.8m** financial and ancillary revenue engine—comprising Effective Interest Income (R2,383.4m at a 23.9% weighted contractual lending rate and 28.5% EIR discount rate), Monarch microinsurance revenue (R1,378.4m), and ancillary contract fees (R1,106.0m).

While headline earnings rose 18.4% to R909.4m (HEPS: 1,753.4c) in FY26, our forensic audit of the financial notes identifies four structural headwinds that cap multiple expansion over FY27E–FY28E:

1. **Guided Gross Margin Compression (-220 bps in FY27E):** Following a 24 bps expansion in FY26 to 43.69%, management explicitly guides FY27 gross profit margin down to **40.0%–42.0%** (Page 50). The February 2026 Middle East conflict has triggered severe international oil, sea freight, and domestic fuel inflation, reversing FY26's spot Rand/USD import tailwinds.


2. **Hidden Delinquency & Working Capital Absorption:** Gross debtors expanded 15.2% to R9,167.3m in FY26, outstripping credit sales growth (+9.58%) by 562 bps as collections slowed by 80 bps to 78.1%. Contractual arrears surged 25.1% to R2,331.4m (25.4% of gross debtors), with **R961.0m (10.5% of the entire book)** consisting of arrear instalments sitting inside accounts still classified as "satisfactory paid" (defined generously as customers paying $\ge 70\%$ of cumulative contractual instalments over the life of the contract per Note 2.1, Page 57). Furthermore, management maintained a 70% probability weighting on its macroeconomic downside scenario, lifting the forward-looking ECL overlay to R595.1m (2025: R517.0m) and total ECL coverage to 37.73% (R3,458.6m). Funding this book expansion consumed **R1,204.7m** of working capital cash in FY26 (Note 12.2, Page 62), forcing Net Borrowings up 39.8% to R1,230.5m (and Total Net Debt including IFRS 16 leases to R2,194.1m, or 40.0% gearing). Management's FY27 targets explicitly guide gearing up to **45%–55%** and the borrowings ratio to **25%–30%** (Page 50), confirming continued balance sheet debt absorption.


3. **Insurance Margin Compression & Opex Creep:** Under IFRS 17, Monarch's insurance service expenses jumped 24.3% to R1,053.8m on higher claims ratios, compressing net underwriting profit by 2.5% to R324.6m (net margin down 465 bps to 23.55%) and pushing total operating cost growth (including insurance expenses) to 12.7%—above management's 7%–11% target band.


4. **Speciality Segment Drag:** Despite adding 36 Real Beds stores, the Speciality segment (UFO, Real Beds, Bedzone) saw pre-impairment operating profit fall from R25.6m to R19.5m (3.5% margin) before absorbing R72.8m in ROU asset and trademark impairments (Notes 8 & 10, Pages 60–61), resulting in a R(53.3)m statutory operating loss.



**Valuation Verdict:** At R79.00, LEW trades at a deep discount to its net asset value of R106.22/share (0.74x P/NAV) and on a trailing FY26 P/HEPS of 4.51x (4.80x Basic EPS). This valuation discount is structural and warranted: LEW's high accounting ROE (16.16% on average equity) converts poorly into Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) during credit expansion cycles because 58.7% of post-tax cash generation is reinvested into 36-month receivables, requiring additional debt drawdown to fund the 55% dividend payout. Balancing a depressed 4.65x forward P/E multiple against an 11.9% cash dividend yield and a 3-stage FCFE/Residual Income floor yields a fair 12-month target price of **R84.50/share**.

## 4. Valuation Multiples & DCF / Residual Income Valuation (ZAR per Share)

### Cost of Equity ($K_e$) & WACC Parameterisation

Because LEW is a hybrid credit-retailer where interest income (R2,383.4m) is a top-line operating revenue and debt is actively drawn to fund the trade receivables book, **equity-side valuation methodologies (FCFE DCF, Residual Income, and P/E + P/NAV multiples)** are superior to standard EV/EBITDA, though we present both for institutional completeness.

* **Risk-Free Rate ($R_f$):** **10.25%** (10-Year South African Government Bond generic yield benchmark).
* **Equity Risk Premium (ERP) & Beta ($\beta$):** South African ERP of **6.00%** and an adjusted levered beta of **1.10** (reflecting high sensitivity of LSM 4–7 credit collections to domestic fuel/food inflation and interest rates), plus a small/mid-cap liquidity and credit-book opacity premium of **0.90%**:

$$K_e = 10.25\% + (1.10 \times 6.00\%) + 0.90\% = \mathbf{17.75\%}$$

* **After-Tax Cost of Debt ($K_d$):** Weighted average borrowing rate of **8.30%** (Note 5.1, Page 59) and implied lease discount rate of ~9.3% yield a blended pre-tax debt cost of **8.75%**. At the 27.0% statutory corporate tax rate, after-tax $K_d = 8.75\% \times (1 - 0.27) = \mathbf{6.39\%}$.


* **Target Capital Structure & WACC:** At market value (Market Cap R4,076.4m / 65.0% weight; Total Net Debt incl. leases R2,194.1m / 35.0% weight):



$$\text{WACC} = (17.75\% \times 65.0\%) + (6.39\% \times 35.0\%) = \mathbf{13.77\%}$$

* **Terminal Growth Rate ($g$):** **5.50%** (aligned with long-run South African SARB midpoint CPI inflation of 4.5% plus 1.0% real volume growth). At steady-state terminal growth of 5.50%, the debtors book grows by only 5.5% p.a., releasing substantial cash flow and raising steady-state FCFE conversion to **75.0% of PAT** (consistent with a 19.0% steady-state ROE where $\text{Reinvestment Rate} = \frac{g}{\text{ROE}} = \frac{5.5\%}{19.0\%} = 28.9\%$, leaving **71.1%–75.0%** distributable as free cash flow to equity).
