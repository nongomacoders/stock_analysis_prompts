## 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

LEW Group’s FY26 Integrated Annual Report presents a headline narrative of robust retail resilience: total revenue crossing **R10.3 billion** (+11.1%), operating margin expanding **110 bps to 23.8%**, and headline earnings per share (HEPS) jumping **18.3% to 1,753.4 cents**. However, a sequential forensic reconstruction across the primary financial statements (D005) and alternative performance measures (APMs) reveals that **LEW is economically a high-yield consumer finance and microinsurance operation attached to a low-growth furniture distribution network.**

Key forensic determinations from the sequential working paper audit include:

1. **Earnings Engine & Margin Reality:** Pure merchandise sales grew just **7.3%** (to R5,452.1m) and represent only **52.8%** of total group revenue. The remaining **47.2%** (R4,867.8m) comprises Effective Interest Income (R2,383.4m), Insurance Revenue (R1,378.4m), and Ancillary Services (R1,106.0m), which collectively surged **15.7%**. Furthermore, management’s headline **23.8% operating margin** is a non-standard APM computed by dividing total group operating profit (R1,299.7m) by *merchandise sales only* (R5,452.1m). On a statutory total revenue basis (R10,319.9m), the true consolidated operating margin is **12.59%** (+18 bps YoY).


2. **Credit Book Stress Masked by Provisioning & Denominator Growth:** Gross debtors expanded **15.2%** to R9,167m, outpacing credit sales growth (+9.6%) by **560 bps**. While management highlights an improved debtor cost ratio of **14.3%** (2025: 15.0%), underlying credit health indicators deteriorated: collection rates fell **80 bps** to 78.1%, satisfactory-paid accounts dropped **90 bps** to 82.6%, credit application decline rates jumped **330 bps** to 41.8%, and contractual arrears swelled to **25.4%** of gross debtors (R2,331m vs R1,863m in FY25). Notably, **10.5%** of the entire gross debtors book consists of arrear instalments sitting inside accounts still classified as "satisfactory paid".


3. **Speciality Segment Value Destruction:** The group opened a net **58 new stores** (36 in the speciality bedding brand Real Beds). However, total trading space grew just **2.7%** (indicating very small format additions), and the Speciality segment swung from an R8m operating profit in FY25 to a **R(53)m operating loss** in FY26, dragged down by a **R76.2m impairment** of Right-of-Use (ROU) assets.


4. **Balance Sheet Refinancing & Working Capital Absorption:** Net borrowings rose **39.8%** from R880m to **R1,230.5m** (40.0% gearing including R963.6m in IFRS 16 leases) to fund the R1,212m gross debtors expansion. ## 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

### Junction 1: Merchandise vs. Credit/Other Revenue & Operating Margin Denominator

* **Initial Narrative Claim:** Total revenue increased 11.1% to R10.3bn, merchandise sales rose 7.3% to R5.5bn, other revenue rose 15.7%, and the operating profit margin expanded by 110 bps to 23.8% (`[D002, p. 7, 9; D004, p. 50]`).


* **Forensic Reconciliation (`[D005, p. 54]` & `[D004, p. 50]`):**
* **Revenue Disaggregation:** The audited income statement (`[D005, p. 54]`) uses a single-step format with no stated Gross Profit line:



$$\text{Total Revenue} = \text{Merchandise (R5,452.1m)} + \text{Ancillary (R1,106.0m)} + \text{EIR Interest (R2,383.4m)} + \text{Insurance (R1,378.4m)} = \text{R10,319.9m}$$



"Other revenue" (non-merchandise) equals **R4,867.8m** (2025: R4,207.2m, +15.70%). Credit sales represent **69.4%** of merchandise sales (implied **R3,783.8m**, +9.5%), while cash sales grew just **2.5%** (implied **R1,668.3m**).


* **The Operating Margin Denominator Anomaly:** Audited Operating Profit is **R1,299.7m** (before R76.2m impairments: **R1,375.9m**). If divided by Total Revenue (R10,319.9m), the true statutory operating margin is **12.59%** (2025: R1,152.3m ÷ R9,287.8m = **12.41%**). Management arrives at its reported **23.8%** operating margin (and 25.2% pre-impairment margin) by dividing **total Group Operating Profit (which includes R4,867.8m of financial/ancillary revenue) by Merchandise Sales alone (R5,452.1m)**:



$$\text{Reported Operating Margin APM} = \frac{\text{R1,299.7m}}{\text{R5,452.1m}} = 23.84\% \quad (\text{Pre-impairment: } \frac{\text{R1,375.9m}}{\text{R5,452.1m}} = 25.24\%)$$


* **Insurance Underwriting Compression:** Under IFRS 17, Insurance Revenue rose 16.7% to R1,378.4m, but Insurance Service Expenses surged **24.3%** to R1,053.8m due to higher claims ratios (`[D004, p. 50; D005, p. 54]`). Consequently, net insurance underwriting profit declined **2.5%** from R333.0m to **R324.6m**, compressing the net insurance margin by **465 bps** (from 28.20% to 23.55%) and causing the Group to miss its 7–11% total operating cost growth target (actual: **+12.7%** to R4,563.7m).





### Junction 2: Gross Profit Margin Bridge & Guided FY27 Compression

* **Initial Narrative Claim:** Gross profit margin strengthened by 30 bps to 43.7% (2025: 43.4%), supported by new merchandise ranges and a favourable Rand/USD exchange rate (`[D002, p. 9]`). However, management’s FY27 target table guides GP margin down to **40–42%** (`[D002.2, p. 14]`).


* **Forensic Reconciliation (`[D004, p. 52]` & `[D005, p. 54]`):**
* **Exact GP Calculation:** Because the income statement (`[D005, p. 54]`) has no Gross Profit line, Merchandise Gross Profit is derived strictly by deducting Cost of Merchandise Sales (R3,070.1m) from Merchandise Sales (R5,452.1m):



$$\text{Merchandise Gross Profit} = \text{R5,452.1m} - \text{R3,070.1m} = \text{R2,382.0m} \quad (\text{2025: R5,080.6m} - \text{R2,873.3m} = \text{R2,207.3m})$$


$$\text{Exact GP Margin (FY26)} = \frac{\text{R2,382.0m}}{\text{R5,452.1m}} = 43.69\% \quad (\text{FY25: } 43.45\% \rightarrow \text{exact expansion of } \mathbf{+24\text{ bps}})$$


* **FX Accounting Reclassification:** Explanatory Note 4 of the Five-Year Review (`[D004, p. 52]`) reveals that *"Foreign exchange gains and (losses) are included under operating costs from the 2025 financial year."* While spot import rates favoured landed merchandise cost in FY26, hedging/translation FX volatility sits inside operating costs (R3,509.9m) rather than COGS.


* **Guided Compression Drivers:** The **170–370 bps downward guidance** for FY27 (to 40–42%) reflects the reversal of spot FX tailwinds and severe supply chain/fuel cost inflation resulting from the February 2026 Middle East conflict (`[D002, p. 7, 10; D002.4, p. 16]`). Because the Working Paper capture stops at physical PDF page 55 (prior to Note 4 on pages 56–62), the exact rand split of supplier rebates, freight, and obsolescence inside Note 4 remains uncaptured in the dossier, though balance sheet inventories (`[D005, p. 55]`) confirm a disciplined COGS-based inventory turn of **4.0x** ($\text{R3,070.1m} \div \text{R768.7m} = 3.99\text{x}$, or ~91 days).





### Junction 3: Debtors Book Health, Arrears & IFRS 9 ECL Provisioning

* **Initial Narrative Claim:** The debtors portfolio is "sound," growing 15.2% to R9.2bn, with debtor costs improving from 15.0% to 14.3% of gross carrying value and net bad debts dropping from 10.6% to 8.8% (`[D002, p. 10; D003, p. 46]`).


* **Forensic Reconciliation (`[D003, p. 46]`, `[D004, p. 50, 52]`, `[D005, p. 54, 55]`):**
* **Book Growth vs. Sales Growth Divergence:** Gross debtors rose **15.2%** (R9,167m vs R7,955m), whereas credit merchandise sales rose only **9.6%** (R3,784m). The 560 bps excess growth is driven by compounding Effective Interest Income (+16.4% to R2,383.4m), ancillary/insurance charges attached to 36-month contracts, and a slowing collection rate (**78.1%** vs 78.9% in FY25 and 80.8% in FY23). Note also that the FY25 collections comparative (R6,253m) carries a restatement footnote (`* Correction to prior year figure`, `[D004, p. 50]`).


* **P&L Debtor Cost Composition Shift:** Total debtor costs rose **9.8%** to **R1,310.2m** (2025: R1,193.2m). Crucially, the P&L composition shifted heavily from realised write-offs to balance sheet provisioning (`[D005, p. 54]`):


* **Net bad debts written off:** Fell **3.6%** from R840.7m to **R810.2m** (8.84% of gross debtors).


* **Movement in impairment provision:** Surged **41.8%** from R352.5m to **R500.0m**.
The improvement in the headline debtor cost ratio from 15.00% ($\text{R1,193.2m} \div \text{R7,955m}$) to **14.29%** ($\text{R1,310.2m} \div \text{R9,167m}$) is entirely a **denominator effect** caused by the 15.2% expansion of the gross book.




* **Hidden Arrears Inside "Satisfactory Paid" Accounts:** On the balance sheet, the total ECL impairment provision increased by exactly R500m from R2,959m (37.20%) to **R3,459m** (**37.73%** of gross debtors), leaving net trade debtors at **R5,708m** (`[D003, p. 46]`). Reconciling to the Balance Sheet line *Trade and other receivables* of **R5,876.1m** (`[D005, p. 55]`) isolates **R168.1m** in non-customer receivables (prepayments, supplier development loans, etc.). Beneath the surface, **Contractual Arrears jumped 25.1% from R1,863m (23.4%) to R2,331m (25.4% of gross debtors)** (`[D004, p. 50]`). The Five-Year Review (`[D004, p. 52]`) exposes the critical split of this 25.4% arrears figure:


* **14.9%** of gross debtors (~R1,366m) are arrears on slow-paying/non-performing accounts (up from 13.1% in FY25).


* **10.5%** of gross debtors (~R963m) are **arrear instalments sitting on "satisfactory paid" accounts**. Thus, a substantial portion of the 82.6% "satisfactory" customer base is already in partial contractual arrears.







### Junction 4: Store Network Capex, Trading Density & Speciality Impairments

* **Initial Narrative Claim:** Record 58 net new stores opened (total 976) and 100 stores refurbished, yet 5-year cumulative capex is only R368m (`[D002, p. 8, 10]`).


* **Forensic Reconciliation (`[D004, p. 50, 52]` & `[D005, p. 54, 55]`):**
* **Micro-Format Expansion & Trading Density:** While store count grew **6.3%** (+58 net stores: +25 Traditional to 874; +33 Speciality to 102, driven by +36 Real Beds and -3 UFO closures), total trading space grew only **2.7%** (+7,148 m² to 271,497 m²) (`[D004, p. 50, 52]`). The implied average size of the net additions is just **~123 m²** vs. the legacy network average of **288 m²**, confirming a shift to low-capex, leased micro-formats. Because space grew only 2.7% while total revenue grew 11.1%, reported trading density (*Revenue per m²*) rose **8.2%** to **R38,011/m²** (`[D004, p. 52]`)—though on a pure merchandise sales basis (R5,452.1m), true retail trading density is **R20,082/m²** (+4.5% YoY).


* **Capex vs. IFRS 16 Capitalisation:** On-balance-sheet PPE increased marginally from R467.1m to **R482.1m** (`[D005, p. 55]`), consistent with ~R70–80m p.a. of shopfitting/refurbishment capex (R368m over 5 years). The physical store properties are funded off-capex via IFRS 16 leases (ROU assets of **R795.4m**).


* **Speciality Segment Collapse:** In `[D004, p. 50]`, the Speciality segment (UFO, Real Beds, Bedzone) grew merchandise sales by 5.4% (to R562m), but operating costs jumped **36.6%** (from R235m to R321m), causing pre-impairment operating profit to fall from R26m to **R20m**. Below this line, the Group recorded **R76.2m in impairments and capital items** (`[D005, p. 54]`), primarily ROU asset impairments in the Speciality segment, driving Speciality into a **R(53)m operating loss** (-9.5% margin).

Meanwhile, Current Assets rose to **R7,385.6m** (because all R5,876.1m of net 36-month receivables are classified as current within the normal operating cycle), mechanically inflating the Current Ratio from **2.65x** ($\text{R6,634.4m} \div \text{R2,507.7m}$) to **4.92x** ($\text{R7,385.6m} \div \text{R1,502.4m}$).

### Junction 6: Cash Conversion, ROE Basis & The Dividend Payout Denominator

* **Initial Narrative Claim:** Dividend increased 12.1% to 897c, maintaining a payout ratio of **55.0%** ("55% or higher"), ROE improved to **16.2%**, and cash flow from operations rose 19.9% to R1,119m with a 62.5% cash conversion ratio (`[D002, p. 7, 8; D004, p. 49, 51, 52]`).


* **Forensic Reconciliation (`[D004, p. 49, 51, 52]` & `[D005, p. 54, 55]`):**
* **Resolving the 55.0% Dividend Payout Discrepancy (Q1/Q54):** A naive calculation dividing total DPS (897.0c) by HEPS (1,753.4c) yields **51.16%**, which appeared to contradict management’s claim of a **55.0%** payout ratio. Extracting the Income Statement (`[D005, p. 54]`) and CFO Report (`[D004, p. 51]`) resolves the math:


* Basic EPS is **1,646.2c** (Net Profit R853.8m ÷ Basic WANS of **51.865m** shares) and Diluted EPS is **1,587.1c** (Diluted WANS of **53.796m** shares). Because no share buybacks occurred in FY26 (`[D004, p. 51]`), year-end shares in issue rose slightly from 51,482k to **51,646k** (`[D004, p. 52]`), while Treasury Shares increased by R5.4m to R(43.0)m (`[D005, p. 55]`).


* On a per-share basis, $897.0\text{c} \div \text{Basic EPS } 1,646.2\text{c} = \mathbf{54.49\%}$ (2025: $800.0\text{c} \div 1,456.9\text{c} = \mathbf{54.91\%}$).


* On a **total Rand basis**, management states total dividends declared for the year amounted to **R469m** (2025: R417m) (`[D004, p. 51]`, which includes dividends on treasury/incentive shares held across declaration dates, corroborated by the retained earnings roll-forward on `[D005, p. 55]`: $\text{Opening R5,046.5m} + \text{Net Profit R853.8m} + \text{Retirement OCI R14.5m} - \text{Closing R5,449.4m} = \text{R465.4m}$). Dividing the stated **R469m** total dividend by **Attributable Net Profit of R853.8m** yields **54.93% $\rightarrow$ 55.0%** (2025: $\text{R417m} \div \text{R754.9m} = \mathbf{55.24\% \rightarrow 55.3\%}$). **Conclusion:** Management computes the payout ratio on **statutory Attributable Earnings (basic EPS / total Rand dividend vs. Net Profit)**, *not* Headline Earnings (HEPS), because the R76.2m non-cash ROU impairment depressed basic EPS (1,646.2c) below HEPS (1,753.4c).




* **Proof of ROE Contradiction Against Footnote 1 (Q7/Q68):** Footnote 1 on `[D004, p. 52]` claims: *"All ratios are based on figures at the end of the period unless otherwise disclosed."* Testing Net Profit (R853.8m) against closing equity (R5,485.6m) yields **15.56%** (and Headline Earnings of R909.4m ÷ R5,485.6m yields **16.58%**). However, testing Net Profit against **Average Equity** ($\frac{\text{R5,078.4m} + \text{R5,485.6m}}{2} = \text{R5,282.0m}$) yields:



$$\text{ROE (Average Equity Basis)} = \frac{\text{R853.8m}}{\text{R5,282.0m}} = \mathbf{16.16\% \approx 16.2\%}$$



Thus, ROE is computed using **Net Profit ÷ Average Equity**, directly refuting Footnote 1.


* **Cash Conversion & Free Cash Flow:** Cash flow from operations (CFO) rose 19.9% to **R1,119m** (`[D004, p. 51]`), which equates to **2,156.6c/share** on ~51.89m shares (`[D004, p. 52]`). With a reported Cash Conversion Ratio of **62.5%** (`[D004, p. 52]`), the implied denominator (EBITDA before working capital movements) is $\text{R1,119m} \div 0.625 = \mathbf{\text{R1,790.4m}}$ (Operating Profit before impairments of R1,375.9m + ~R414.5m of PPE/intangible/ROU depreciation). Working capital absorbed **~R671m** (37.5% of EBITDA), driven by the R1,212m gross debtors expansion. Over 5 years, of the R6.3bn cash generated after tax and interest, **58.7% (R3.7bn)** was consumed simply funding the debtors book (`[D004, p. 49]`).
