### 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

**True Economic Performance:**
iOCO's FY2026 performance is a story of **financial engineering masking operational stagnation**. While the Group reported a 38.7% increase in profit after tax (R357m) and a 21% rise in "Adjusted EBITDA" (R624m), the underlying business contracted. **Revenue growth was a nominal 2.7%** (R5.73bn), which, when adjusted for the acquisition of MySky (R18m contribution), implies organic growth of less than 2.4%—below SA inflation. More critically, **gross margins compressed by 98bps to 27.7%**, driven by a massive deterioration in the "Reconciliation" line (head office costs and eliminations) which swung from a R201m drag to a R269m drag.

The "earnings growth" is largely attributable to a **R28.5m reduction in finance costs** (due to deleveraging) and a **R8.9m drop in the effective tax rate** (15.5% vs 22.4%), rather than operational leverage. The "Adjusted EBITDA" metric is aggressive, including R13.5m of "non-recurring legacy items" as an add-back, effectively treating costs as non-recurring while including them in the headline profitability metric.

**Balance Sheet Health:**
The balance sheet is **technically solvent but structurally weak**.
- **Goodwill Impairment Risk:** Goodwill stands at R608m (21% of total assets). The auditor's Key Audit Matter (KAM) highlights sensitivity, particularly in "conflict markets." The "Compute" CGU (R103.7m goodwill) has seen its headroom collapse from R9.1m to R222m (though the prior year was tight), and "Learning and Development" headroom is thin at R56.8m.
- **Working Capital:** The CEO admitted working capital management "fell short." This is confirmed by the cash flow statement: **Cash Generated from Operations (CFO) plummeted 35% to R368m**, with a **R217m working capital outflow** (driven by a R216m reduction in payables). This is a red flag for cash conversion quality.
- **Debt:** Gross debt reduced to R619m. Covenant headroom is comfortable (Net Debt/EBITDA 1.14x vs 2.00x limit), but the reliance on a R250m overdraft facility (undrawn) and the pledging of >70% of Group revenue/assets to the Security SPV indicates a constrained capital structure.

**Post-Balance-Sheet Liquidity Runway:**
The Group has **R417m in cash** (including restricted) and **R250m undrawn facilities**. However, the **R47m MySky purchase consideration was settled on 3 August 2026** (post-year-end), and the **Astraia acquisition** (R10.5m cash + contingent) was completed post-year-end. While liquidity appears adequate for the next 12 months, the **quality of cash generation is deteriorating**, and the Group is reliant on the undrawn overdraft to fund operations if the working capital cycle does not improve as targeted (45-day cycle incentive).

**Key Findings:**
1.  **Revenue Quality:** The "Recurring Revenue" claim (>60%) is inconsistent across divisions (ranging from 10% to 90% depending on definition). The "Application Managed Services -39%" fragment in D009 suggests a significant decline in a key recurring revenue stream, contradicting the growth narrative.
2.  **Profit Quality:** Operating profit is flattered by a R1.8m profit on asset disposal and a R12.7m deferred tax credit. The "Adjusted EBITDA" includes R13.5m of "non-recurring" costs as add-backs.
3.  **Cash Conversion:** CFO of R368m vs EBITDA of R624m represents a **59% cash conversion ratio**, well below the 80-90% expected for a services business, due to the working capital hemorrhage.
4.  **Acquisition Accounting:** The MySky acquisition (R64.5m consideration) generated R37.9m in goodwill and R15m in customer contracts. The contingent consideration (R12.5m) is based on aggressive EBITDA targets (R13.7m - R18m) vs the actual post-acquisition contribution (R4m PBT for 3 months).

### 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

#### 2.1 Contract Revenue Mix: Recurring ARR vs Lumpy System Integration
- **Claim:** Management claims "recurring revenue exceeding 60%" (D004) and divisions claim high renewal rates (D007: 97%, D012: 97%, D014: 92%).
- **Forensic Finding:** The definition of "recurring" is **inconsistent and potentially misleading**.
    - **D006 (Infrastructure Solutions):** Claims "10% of revenue recurring" but "90% Managed services." This implies "Managed Services" (often recurring) is not counted as "Recurring Revenue" in their KPI, or the 90% figure is aspirational.
    - **D009 (Software Engineering):** The fragment "Application managed services -39%" (D009, p. 64) is a **critical red flag**. If AMS (a key recurring stream) fell 39%, the "recurring revenue" base is eroding, not growing.
    - **D010 (Data & Security):** Claims "62% of base on subscription/renewal."
    - **D016 (UK/Europe):** Claims "Recurring managed-services revenue 40% of base."
- **Determination:** The Group's "recurring revenue" is likely a mix of multi-year contracts, software support, and managed services. The **-39% decline in AMS** (D009) suggests that the "lumpy" project work is masking a decline in the higher-quality annuity base. The 60% group claim is **not reliably supported** by the divisional data.

#### 2.2 Gross Profit Margin by Line of Business
- **Claim:** Group Gross Margin 27.7% (down from 28.7%).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **IT Services:** Margin improved to 30.0% (from 28.9%).
    - **Operational Technology (OT):** Margin improved to 34.6% (from 30.8%).
    - **International:** Margin **collapsed to 28.4%** (from 39.7%).
    - **Reconciliation (Head Office):** The drag increased to **-R269m** (from -R201m).
- **Determination:** The core SA businesses (IT Services, OT) are performing well on margin. The **International segment is the problem**, likely due to the "major contract losses" in the UK (D016) and the "geopolitical disruption" in the Middle East (D015). The **R68m increase in the head office drag** is unexplained and suggests either unallocated cost inflation or a change in intersegment charging.

#### 2.3 Direct Technical Headcount Utilization & Labor Costs
- **Claim:** "Radical autonomy" and "small head office" (D003).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **Employee Costs:** Fell R287m (-11.4%) to R2.23bn. This is a massive reduction.
    - **Headcount:** D003 claims 4,382 employees. D007 (Forensics) has 438. D008 (People) has 766 permanent/contact centre. D009 (Software) has ~500. D010 (Data) has 379. D012 (Enterprise) has 300+. D013 (CIE) has 309. D014 (MS&N) has 695. D015 (MEE) has 360. D016 (UK) has 17.
    - **Utilization:** No division discloses billable utilization %.
- **Determination:** The **R287m reduction in employee costs** is the primary driver of the EBITDA growth. This was likely achieved through retrenchments (restructuring) and the disposal of non-core businesses. However, the **lack of utilization disclosure** makes it impossible to determine if the remaining workforce is productive or if the Group has cut muscle (technical skills) to protect margins. The "High" risk rating for "Skills Availability" in D016 and "Talent Retention" in D015 suggests the cuts may have damaged capability.

#### 2.4 Contract Assets / WIP Unbilled Revenue & Deferred Revenue
- **Claim:** "Working capital management fell short" (D003).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **Contract Assets:** R136m (up from R124m).
    - **Contract Liabilities (Deferred Revenue):** R328m (down from R338m).
    - **Cash Conversion:** CFO of R368m vs EBITDA of R624m.
    - **Working Capital:** A **R217m outflow**, driven by a **R216m reduction in payables** (the Group paid suppliers faster or used less supplier credit) and a **R29m increase in receivables**.
- **Determination:** The Group is **funding its customers and suppliers**. The reduction in payables (R216m) is a massive cash drain. This could be due to suppliers demanding stricter terms (due to iOCO's credit profile) or the Group cleaning up its balance sheet. The increase in contract assets (unbilled WIP) suggests revenue is being recognized before cash is collected.

#### 2.5 Capitalized Software Development & IP Amortization
- **Claim:** "Own IP" strategy (D007, D013, D015).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **Internally Generated Software:** R47m (carrying value). Additions R20.5m, Amortization R14.5m.
    - **Impairment:** Nil.
- **Determination:** The capitalization of R20.5m in development costs is **aggressive** given the Group's profitability. This effectively boosts EBITDA by reducing opex. The "Own IP" narrative (NAMAA, ecWIN, etc.) is not yet generating sufficient revenue to justify the capitalization, and the risk of future impairment is high if these products fail to scale.

#### 2.6 Legacy Dispute / Litigation Provisions & Contingent Liabilities
- **Claim:** "No material litigation" (D003).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **Provisions:** R6.6m (down from R19.5m).
    - **Litigation Provision:** R2.3m (storage array dispute).
    - **SARS:** R3m PAYE dispute (Rosstone).
    - **Contingent Liability:** Parent company guarantee for PiA Solar (D010.7). The guarantee is for "underperformance." PiA has "operational challenges."
- **Determination:** The provisions are small relative to the balance sheet. However, the **PiA Solar guarantee** is a potential "black swan." If PiA fails, iOCO may be liable for significant damages. The "continued provision of the guarantee is under discussion" suggests iOCO is trying to exit this risk.

#### 2.7 Debt Restructuring / Deleveraging Milestones
- **Claim:** "Deleveraging to a sustainable level" (D010.1).
- **Forensic Finding:**
    - **Gross Debt:** R619m (down from R658m).
    - **Net Debt:** R202m.
    - **Covenants:** Net Debt/EBITDA 1.14x (limit 2.00x). Interest Cover 9.04x (limit 3.75x).
    - **Security SPV:** >70% of Group revenue/assets pledged.
- **Determination:** The deleveraging is real, but the **cost is high**. The Group has pledged its crown jewels (70% of revenue) to the lenders. The improvement in covenants is largely due to the **R287m reduction in employee costs** (boosting EBITDA) rather than revenue growth. The refinancing of the bullet loan to 2030 is a positive milestone.

#### 2.8 FCFE Conversion
- **Calculation:**
    - CFO: R368m
    - Capex (PPE + Intangibles): R82m
    - **Free Cash Flow (FCF): R286m**
    - Net Interest Paid: R67m
    - **FCFE (pre-debt repayment): R219m**
    - Debt Repayment: R60m
    - Buybacks: R69m
    - **Net Cash Movement: R9.9m**
- **Determination:** The Group is generating **R286m in FCF**, which is healthy. However, it is using this cash to **pay down debt (R60m) and buy back shares (R69m)**. The buyback (R69m) is controversial given the high debt levels and the need to invest in growth. The FCFE conversion is **weak** (FCF/EBITDA = 46%), reflecting the working capital drag.
