### Explicit Forensic Refinements to Stage 1 Baseline Determinations (Rule 1 Compliance)

In accordance with Operating Rule 1, we adopt the Stage 1 Forensic Audit Determinations as our 99% confidence baseline. However, rigorous cross-examination of the primary notes in `iOCO-AFS-July2026.pdf` reveals five exact quantitative refinements that must be formally declared and integrated into our institutional model:

1. **Exact Debt & Lease Liability Disaggregation (Refining Stage 1 Junction 7):** Stage 1 noted Gross Debt of R619,337k and subtracted the CFO's R480,000k bank debt figure to infer R139,337k of IFRS 16 lease liabilities. Primary AFS Evidence (`iOCO-AFS-July2026.pdf`, Statement of Financial Position p. 14, Note 18 p. 53–54, and Note 19 p. 54): R619,337k is solely the carrying value of *Other Financial Liabilities* (comprising R480,431k of bank loans secured via the Security SPV, R113,456k of Project Finance loans, R12,967k of unsecured bank loans, and R12,483k of non-interest-bearing MySky contingent consideration). *IFRS 16 Lease Liabilities* are reported separately on the balance sheet at **R78,778k** (R52,038k non-current + R26,740k current). Furthermore, Note 19 (p. 54) confirms lease liability additions were **R62,991k** (matching ROU building additions of R62,991k in Note 5, p. 37), whereas R78,778k is the *closing* lease liability balance. Consequently, **Total Consolidated Gross Debt + Lease Liabilities is R698,115k** (R619,337k + R78,778k), and **Consolidated Net Debt (including leases and earn-outs) is R281,065k** (R698,115k less Cash of R417,050k), or **R202,287k excluding IFRS 16 leases**.


2. **MySky Acquisition Cash Outflow Timing & Payables Distortion (Refining Stage 1 Junctions 4 & 5):** Stage 1 correctly identified that the Cash Flow Statement (p. 17) shows a net cash inflow of **+R7,914k** on acquisition of a subsidiary. Primary AFS Evidence (`iOCO-AFS-July2026.pdf`, Note 14 p. 47–48 & Note 20 p. 55): Total purchase consideration for 100% of MySky (effective 1 May 2026) was **R64,483k**, comprising **R47,000k in cash**, **R5,000k in ordinary shares to be issued**, and **R12,483k in fair-valued contingent consideration**. Crucially, Note 20 (p. 55) discloses: *"Included in other payables is an amount of R47 million of purchase consideration due for the acquisition of the MySky group of companies. This amount was settled on 3 August 2026."* Thus, only MySky's acquired cash (+R7,914k) hit the FY2026 cash flow statement before 31 July 2026, while the **R47,000k upfront cash outflow occurred on 3 August 2026 (Q1 FY2027)**. Moreover, because Trade and Other Payables at 31 July 2026 (R1,104,025k) included this R47,000k M&A payable, the underlying operational payables contraction in FY2026 was **−R216,105k** (per Note 30, p. 61), even more severe than the headline balance sheet movement suggests.


3. **Exact Share Count, WANOS & Buyback Price Range (Refining Stage 1 Junction 8):** Stage 1 estimated WANOS at ~615.8m shares based on rounded EPS of 57c. Primary AFS Evidence (`iOCO-AFS-July2026.pdf`, Note 16 p. 51, Note 28 p. 60, Note 29 p. 60, and Annexure 2 p. 96): Total ordinary shares in issue are **638,083,421**. Less treasury shares held in Share Trusts (**2,341,508**), V55 Investments (**14,774,740**), and other subsidiaries (**9,438**), the exact **effective number of shares in issue and Basic WANOS at 31 July 2026 is 620,957,735** (`620,958k`), and **Diluted WANOS is 622,158k**. ### Core Institutional Investment Thesis

We initiate coverage on **iOCO Limited (JSE: IOC)** with an institutional **BUY** recommendation and a 12-month target price of **R4.90 per share**, implying **+23.7% upside** from the reference price of R3.96/share.

Our investment thesis rests on a sober forensic arbitrage between **low expectations embedded in iOCO's distressed multiple** (trading at **4.6x FY26 Statutory EV/EBITDA** and **6.9x trailing HEPS**) and **tangible self-help cash flow catalysts in FY2027E–FY2028E**:

* **Structural De-Risking Complete, Shifting from Survival to Capital Allocation:** Over the past five years, iOCO (formerly EOH) reduced gross bank debt by 77% from R2,065m in FY21 to R480.4m at FY26 year-end. Refinancing of the R226m bullet loan and R209m RCF out to March 2030 at a 50 bps lower spread (effective 1 August 2026), combined with R417.1m in cash and R250m in undrawn overdraft facilities, eliminates balance sheet distress.


* **The FY2026 "Low-Quality Growth" Mirage Is Fully Diagnosed — And Reversible:** Stage 1 forensic analysis proved that FY26 nominal revenue growth (+2.67% to R5,732.6m) was entirely driven by a +49.8% spike in lower-margin OEM hardware pass-through (R736.9m), while core Services revenue contracted by −6.56% (from R4,177.8m to R3,903.6m) and International gross margins collapsed by 1,130 bps (from 39.7% to 28.4%) due to a +R58.7m cost-of-sales spike during 5 months of Middle East geopolitical disruption. Crucially, this creates an undemanding base for FY27E:


* **Contracted Backlog Expansion (+22.1% YoY):** Audited remaining performance obligations (Note 22, p. 57) rose **+22.1% YoY from R911.3m to R1,112.7m** (with multi-year obligations >1 year jumping **+57.2% from R369.6m to R580.9m**), backed by R2.1bn in new multi-year wins and a +56% YoY surge in Operational Technology forward load.


* **Bolt-on M&A Annualisation:** Full-year consolidation of **MySky** (acquired 1 May 2026; contributing R57m annualised revenue and ~R15m–R17m EBITDA vs. R18m revenue and R4m PBT in 3 months of FY26) and **Astraia Technology** (closed post-year-end) adds ~R65m of high-margin networking and cloud ERP revenue in FY27E.




* **Working Capital Release Is the #1 Cash Flow Catalyst in FY2027E:** In FY26, Net Operating Cash Flow fell 43.5% from R423.1m to R238.9m because working capital absorbed **R217.4m** (Note 30, p. 61), driven by a **−R216.1m payables outflow** as OEM hardware suppliers tightened credit terms while gross trade receivables + contract assets + prepayments sat at R1,414.0m (90.0 days total receivables DSO; 68.6 days pure trade receivables + contract assets DSO). Management has tied **30% of all 13 BU Managing Directors' FY27 bonuses to achieving a 45-day working capital cycle**. Even a conservative 8-day reduction in net working capital intensity over FY27E–FY28E unlocks **~R125m in cumulative cash flow**, lifting FCFE from R95.4m in FY26 to **R271.2m in FY27E** (after absorbing the R47m MySky and R10.5m Astraia cash settlements) and **R357.1m in FY28E** (an **14.6% FCFE yield** on current market cap).

## 6. Actionable Portfolio Monitoring Triggers & Catalysts ('What to Watch For That Will Move the Stock')

### Operational & Balance Sheet Red Lines (Downside Watchlist)

1. **Working Capital Cycle Failing to Compress Below 65 Days in H1 FY2027:** Total Trade and Other Receivables closed FY26 at R1,413,963k (90.0 days of revenue; pure Trade Receivables of R941.3m + Contract Assets of R136.0m = 68.6 days), while Trade Payables dropped by R166.5m to R377.1m. If H1 FY27 interim results show net working capital absorption continuing beyond the R47m MySky settlement, or if pure Trade + Contract Asset DSO fails to compress below 62 days despite the new 30% BU MD bonus weighting, our FCFE recovery thesis is invalidated.


2. **Central Segment Reconciliation Gross Profit Drag Exceeding −R280m (−4.7% of Revenue):** In FY26, unallocated central cost of sales and intersegment eliminations deteriorated by +33.8% from −R201.1m to −R269.1m. If the newly formed 12-division structure results in further cost-shifting into the central reconciliation column (>R140m drag in H1 FY27), it will confirm that divisional gross margins (28.4%–34.6%) are structurally overstated.


3. **International Segment Gross Margin Remaining Below 30.0%:** Following the 1,130 bps collapse in International gross margin from 39.7% to 28.4% in FY26, a failure to recover above 30.5% in H1 FY27 as delayed Middle East contracts resume and the Cairo CCOE scales would signal permanent pricing erosion rather than temporary geopolitical delay.


4. **Public Sector Receivables Delinquency or Contract Asset Write-Downs:** While overall public sector revenue declined from 12% to 9% of Group revenue in FY26 (`Note 22, p. 56`), several divisions (Infrastructure Solutions, Managed Services, Forensics/Inlexso, iOCO Enterprise) are actively targeting South African public-sector and municipal contracts as their primary FY27 growth engine. Note 38 (p. 71–72) already shows a **9% expected credit loss (ECL) rate on State-Owned Entities' contract assets** (R1,754k ECL on R16,268k gross) and R150.0m of combined National, Provincial, Municipal and SOE trade receivables. Any spike in >90-day public-sector arrears or delayed milestone sign-offs (such as the rescheduled securities exchange refresh in Infrastructure Solutions) is an immediate red flag.


5. **Thin Goodwill Impairment Headroom in Symplexity & Learning and Development:** Note 7 (p. 42) reveals that **Symplexity** (carrying R50.1m of goodwill) has only **R13.89m of valuation headroom** — a mere **1.4% decrease in revenue** or **2.0% decrease in Adjusted EBITDA margin** would trigger an IAS 36 goodwill impairment. Similarly, **Learning and Development** (carrying R93.5m of goodwill, the second-largest CGU in the Group) has **only a 0.2% Adjusted EBITDA margin buffer** and **2.2% revenue buffer** before impairment amidst SETA-to-QCTO regulatory transition risks.



### Quantified Sensitivity Impacts (Institutional Rules of Thumb)

* **EBITDA Margin Sensitivity:** Every **+100 bps expansion in Consolidated Statutory EBITDA Margin** (R60.6m pre-tax in FY27E) increases **Basic HEPS by +7.98 ZAR cents (+12.1%)** and lifts our **12-month target price by +R0.52 per share (+10.6%)** at a 5.25x multiple.
* **Working Capital (DSO) Sensitivity:** Every **5-day reduction in Trade Receivables & Contract Assets DSO** releases **R83.0m in one-off cash flow**, equivalent to **+13.6 ZAR cents per share in FCFE (+R0.14/share direct Enterprise-to-Equity value transfer)**, or enough to repurchase **3.4% of iOCO's issued share capital** at current prices.
* **Interest Rate (ZARONIA) Sensitivity:** Per Note 38 (p. 69), every **100 bps reduction in South African benchmark interest rates (ZARONIA)** reduces floating-rate debt service and increases post-tax profit by **R5.0m**, adding **+0.82 ZAR cents to Basic HEPS**.


* **Effective Tax Rate (ETR) Sensitivity:** Every **200 bps increase in the Effective Tax Rate** (as ring-fenced assessed tax losses are exhausted) reduces FY27E Attributable PAT by **R10.2m** and lowers **Basic HEPS by −1.67 ZAR cents (−2.5%)**.
