## 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

### True Economic Performance vs. Narrative Framing

In the front-half narrative of the FY2026 Integrated Annual Report (D002–D004), management frames FY2026 as a year of high-quality operational leverage, headlined by **Adjusted EBITDA of R624m (+21% YoY)**, **Operating Profit of R483.1m (+15% YoY)**, and **Profit After Tax of R357.1m (+38.7% YoY)** on modest nominal revenue growth of **+2.67% to R5,732.56m** (FY2025: R5,583.22m).

Forensic decomposition of the underlying audited financial statements and segmental notes reveals a fundamentally different economic reality:

1. **Top-Line Growth Was Driven by Low-Margin Hardware Pass-Through:** Total group revenue grew by **R149.34m** (+2.67% nominal; cited as +3% constant currency on a R5,753m highlight table that diverges by R20.44m from the audited R5,732.56m top line). However, **hardware resale surged by +49.8% to R736.93m** (an increase of ~R245.0m YoY). Stripping out low-margin hardware sales, the Group's core IT services, software, and operational technology revenues **contracted in both nominal and real terms** during FY2026.


2. **Gross Margin Compression & Head-Office Cost Shifting:** Consolidated gross profit rose only +0.8% to **R1,586.66m**, while the **consolidated gross margin compressed by 98 bps from 28.66% to 27.68%**. Simultaneously, the segmental reconciliation column (unallocated central/head-office cost of sales and intersegment eliminations) deteriorated by **33.8% from −R201.07m to −R269.14m**, while unallocated central employee expenses dropped by **−R128.72m** (from R179.47m to R50.75m) — indicating a structural reallocation of personnel costs between operating segments and the corporate centre during the reorganisation from 4 to 12 autonomous business units.


3. **Adjusted EBITDA Quality & Ongoing Restructuring:** Statutory EBITDA (Operating Profit of R483,137k + PPE/ROU Depreciation of R68,963k + Intangible Amortisation of R38,994k) equals **R591,094k**. Management's **R624m Adjusted EBITDA** relies on adding back **R32.91m** of non-operating/non-cash items, specifically **R17,084k of Share-Based Payment (SBP) expenses** and **R13,479k of "non-recurring legacy items"**. Because both the Chairman ("aspects of the turnaround journey remain ongoing") and CEO ("there will be more changes as we refine the model") confirm that organisational restructuring is a continuous multi-year process, treating the R13.48m legacy/restructuring charges and R17.08m ongoing employee dilution as "non-recurring" overstates sustainable cash EBITDA by ~5.3%.


4. **Earnings Growth Amplified by an Effective Tax Rate (ETR) Collapse:** A substantial driver of the +38.7% surge in bottom-line profit (R357.13m total; R351.01m attributable to equity holders) was a **690 bps drop in the effective tax rate from 22.38% to 15.48%** (tax charge of R65,414k on implied PBT of R422,541k), supported by the utilisation/recognition of tax losses out of the Group's **R1.1bn assessed tax loss pool** (carrying R130.37m in recognised Deferred Tax Assets).



### Balance Sheet Health & Working Capital Strain

While the Chairman and CFO claim "strong cash generation", the CEO candidly admits in D003 that **"our working capital management fell short of our expectations"**.

* **Working Capital Cash Drain:** Cash generated from operations was **R367,957k** against R591,094k of statutory EBITDA (62.3% conversion) and R483,137k of operating profit (76.2% conversion). Working capital absorbed over **R115m** during the year, driven primarily by a **−R167,517k cash outflow from trade and other payables**, partially offset by inventory reductions (+R26,965k) and minor trade receivable movements (+R24,021k).


* **Receivables Concentration & DSO Pressure:** Trade and other receivables closed at **R1,413,963k** (representing **90.0 days** of gross revenue at year-end). The CEO explicitly notes that extending 60-to-90-day credit terms forces iOCO to fund three months of non-negotiable payroll, prompting a **new FY2027 executive incentive structure where 30% of BU MD bonuses are tied to achieving a 45-day working capital cycle**.



### Post-Balance-Sheet Liquidity Runway & Debt Structure

At 31 July 2026, iOCO reported **Gross Debt of R619,337k** (comprising **R480,000k of bank debt** secured via the Security SPV and **R139,337k of IFRS 16 lease liabilities** after R78,778k of new lease liability additions). Against **closing cash and cash equivalents of R417,050k** (including R26k of restricted cash and foreign balances), **Consolidated Net Debt stood at R202,287k** (or **R62,950k on a bank-debt-only basis**).

* With **Net Debt / Statutory EBITDA at 0.34x** (0.32x on Adjusted EBITDA) and **Interest Cover of 7.13x** (Operating Profit R483.14m ÷ Finance Costs R67.72m), covenant headroom is comfortable.


* Liquidity runway into FY2027 is well-supported by the **R250m undrawn bank overdraft facility** (providing R667m of total liquidity alongside on-balance-sheet cash) and post-year-end working capital remediation. However, forensic scrutiny of the cash flow statement shows that **net operating cash inflows after interest (R67,410k) and taxes (R66,323k) fell 43.5% YoY from R423m in FY2025 to R239m in FY2026**. After funding capex and R68.84m of share buybacks, true free cash flow to equity (FCFE) was tight, requiring disciplined working capital release in H1 FY2027 to self-fund the Group's M&A pipeline.

## 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

### Junction 1: Contract Revenue Mix (Recurring ARR/Managed Services vs. Lumpy System Integration & Hardware Resale)

* **Initial Management Claim:** The CFO states that revenue increased by 3% on a constant-currency basis to R5.73bn (shown as R5,753m in the p. 15 highlight table) and that **"recurring revenue exceeded 60% during FY2026"** alongside **R2.1bn in new multi-year contract wins**.


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* Audited IFRS 15 revenue in the Consolidated Statement of Profit or Loss (D006) and Segmental Note (D009) is **R5,732,561k** (FY2025: **R5,583,220k**), representing nominal growth of **+2.67% (+R149,341k)**. The **R5,753m** figure in the CFO highlight table is an unadjusted constant-currency or pre-elimination illustrative metric that overstates audited revenue by **R20,439k**.


* Disaggregating the revenue streams (D009 / Note 22) exposes severe mix distortion:
* **Hardware Sales:** Surged **+49.8% YoY to R736,929k** (FY2025: ~R491,942k), representing **12.85%** of group revenue and contributing **+R244,987k** of top-line expansion (164% of the Group's total net revenue growth).


* **Software & Licence Contracts:** Totalled **R1,090,178k** (19.02% of group revenue; reported as +20% growth in the Software business per D004), blending recurring SaaS/SLA support with one-off licence resales and principal vs. agent (net) accounting adjustments.


* **Core IT Services, Managed Services & Project Consulting:** The remaining **R3,905,454k** (68.13% of group revenue) experienced a net contraction of ~R95.6m when stripping out hardware and software licence growth.




* **Divisional Contradictions in "Recurring Revenue" Definitions:**
* In **Infrastructure Solutions (D006, p. 43)**, the division states in its header table that **"10% of revenue [is] recurring"**, yet in the adjacent *"How we earn"* graphic claims **"90% Managed services and resource augmentation"** and **"58% Infrastructure solutions"**. Forensic conclusion: Management includes short-term time-and-materials (T&M) resource augmentation and cyclical hardware/infrastructure refresh projects inside "Managed Services" operationally, whereas strict IFRS multi-year contracted recurring SLA revenue in that division is only **10%**.


* In **Forensics, Legal & Human Capital Solutions (D007, p. 47)**, management discloses that **"Recurring and licensing revenue represented 60% of FY2026 revenue"** (with Contract at 15% and Professional Fees at 25%), while asserting that *"contracted recurring services and licensing represented 85% of the FY2027 opening revenue base"*. Bundling one-off/annual software licensing with recurring services inflates the true ARR figure, and the "85% opening base" is an unaudited forward-load ratio against contracted backlog rather than achieved revenue.






* **Definitive Determination:** The Group's ">60% recurring revenue" claim is an **operational APM that bundles multi-year T&M resource augmentation, renewable annual software licences, and hyperscaler consumption pass-throughs**. True high-margin contracted ARR/SLA revenue is materially lower, and FY2026 top-line growth was entirely reliant on cyclical, low-margin OEM hardware resales (+49.8%).

### Junction 3: Direct Technical Headcount Utilization, Labor Costs & Restructuring Reallocations

* **Initial Management Claim:** Employee costs are iOCO's largest expense; the Group underwent a decentralisation from 4 to 12 business units (13 BU MDs on EXCO), achieving "operational leverage" and disciplined cost management.


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* **Total Employee Costs (D009, p. 22):** Consolidated employee benefit expenses fell by **−R287,596k (−11.4% YoY)** from **R2,524,112k** in FY2025 to **R2,236,516k** in FY2026 (representing **39.01% of group revenue**).


* **Central vs. Segmental Labor Swing:** Within the segmental employee cost table (D009, p. 22), the Reconciliation (Head Office) column employee cost dropped by **−R128,719k (−71.7%)**, falling from **+R179,466k in FY2025 to +R50,747k in FY2026**. Rather than pure head-office retrenchments, a substantial portion of this R128.72m reduction reflects the **reallocation of former cluster/central executives and support staff into the newly created 12 autonomous business units**, alongside the **−R68.07m worsening in the Gross Profit reconciliation column**.


* **Headcount Discrepancies & Arithmetic Errors in Segment Tables:**
* In D003, the CEO states **"Our 4 000 people"** on p. 12, but states **"our 4 382 employees"** on p. 14. Using the exact closing headcount of **4,382 employees**, average employee cost is **R510,387 per employee per annum** (~R42.5k/month).


* In **D007 (Forensics, Legal & Human Capital Solutions, p. 47)**, the divisional table reports **Total People: 438**, split between **422 billable staff** and **16 non-billable staff** (422 + 16 = 438; 96.3% billable ratio). However, the text immediately beneath states: *"422 billable staff include 104 critical and scarce skills, 243 skilled permanent and fixed-term contracted staff, and 125 semi-skilled administrators, analysts and support staff"*. **Forensic arithmetic check:** $104 + 243 + 125 = 472$, which exceeds the stated 422 billable staff by exactly **50 heads**. Notably, the strategic inputs box on p. 47 states *"30 specialists at XTND and 50 in OneSymplexity and 24 in Inlexso"* ($30 + 50 + 24 = 104$ critical skills), suggesting the 50 OneSymplexity specialists were double-counted in the skilled permanent bucket.




* **Share-Based Payments (SBP) & Cash-Settled Liability:** Total SBP expense in the P&L is **R17,084k** (with a **R15,966k** reserve transfer to accumulated loss and **R23,982k** treasury share settlement in the Statement of Changes in Equity, D007 p. 18). Furthermore, Accounting Policy Note D010.1 (p. 32) confirms the existence of a **cash-settled SBP scheme**, meaning a portion of employee incentives sits as a financial/employee liability subject to fair-value P&L remeasurement rather than pure equity settlement.





### Junction 4: Contract Assets / WIP Unbilled Revenue, Receivables & Working Capital Cycle

* **Initial Management Claim:** The Chairman claims *"strengthening operating performance and cash generation"*, and the CFO claims *"strong cash generation across its diversified portfolio"*. Conversely, the CEO admits: *"Our working capital management fell short of our expectations... To plug the hole, 30% of the incentive is now based on achieving a 45-day working capital cycle."*


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* **Trade & Other Receivables (Note 12 / SoFP):** Closed at **R1,413,963k** (representing **48.85% of Group Total Assets of R2,894,467k**). On audited revenue of R5,732,561k, year-end **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) is 90.0 days** (or ~78.3 days on VAT-inclusive revenue) — **double the CEO's 45-day target cycle**.


* **Public Sector Exposure & ECL Risk:** Both Infrastructure Solutions (D006, p. 43) and Forensics/Legal/Human Capital (D007, p. 47) disclose that **22% of divisional revenue is derived from the South African public sector**, with Infrastructure Solutions explicitly targeting public-sector expansion and Inlexso executing a 3-year Department of Justice transcription contract across 3 provinces (expanding to 9 in FY2027). Moreover, in Infrastructure Solutions (D006, p. 45), a major trading-platform refresh deployment was **rescheduled pending third-party OS certification**, delaying milestone billing and trapping working capital in unbilled WIP / contract assets.


* **Working Capital Cash Drain (Note 30 / Cash Flow Statement D008):** While operating profit was **R483,137k** and statutory EBITDA was **R591,094k**, **cash generated from operations was only R367,957k**. The working capital bridge includes:


* **Trade and other payables:** **−R167,517k cash outflow** (creditors/OEM suppliers demanded faster payment, especially on the +49.8% hardware surge, creating a severe cash timing mismatch against 60–90 day customer collections).


* **Inventories:** **+R26,965k cash inflow** (working down hardware/stock buffers).


* **Trade and other receivables:** **+R24,021k cash inflow**.






* **Definitive Determination:** The CEO's candid admission in D003 overrides the Chairman/CFO narrative. iOCO suffered an acute **working capital squeeze in FY2026** driven by a **−R167.52m payables contraction** and a **90-day receivables/WIP book (R1.414bn)**. Achieving the 45-day working capital target in FY2027 is the single most critical liquidity catalyst for the Group.



### Junction 5: Capitalized Software Development, IP Amortization & Goodwill/M&A Accounting

* **Initial Management Claim:** The CFO states the Group invested **+R80m in organic growth** (technology skills and proprietary platforms) and **+R90m in inorganic M&A opportunities**, while the CEO states *"we have made two acquisitions, and a third is under discussion"* with an internal hurdle rate of **>20% ROI**.


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* **Intangible Assets & Capitalized Software (Note 6, D010.2 p. 41):** Net intangible assets increased by **+R16,618k** in FY2026, fully decomposed as follows:


* **Total Additions:** **+R43,087k**, of which **R20,512k represents internally generated software development** capitalized under IAS 38 (e.g., XTND forensic platforms *Veritas, VeriX, AuthentIQ, ExposeIT, VeriSENSE, GRaCE*, OneSymplexity HR/payroll, Inlexso *Lexi* AI transcription, and Cloud SA AI factory) and **R22,575k represents externally acquired/other intangibles**.


* **Business Combinations (Note 14 — MySky):** **+R15,050k** of acquired customer contracts/intangibles.


* **Amortisation Charge:** **−R38,994k** (comprising **R14,526k** amortisation on internally generated software, **R2,493k** on acquired customer contracts — implying a ~6-year useful life on the R15,050k addition — and **R21,975k** on other software/intangibles).


* **Disposals & FX:** Disposals of **−R942k** and foreign exchange movement of **−R1,583k**.


* **Net Capitalization Boost to P&L:** Internally generated software additions (**R20,512k**) exceeded internal software amortisation (**R14,526k**) by **+R5,986k**, providing a modest net upward boost to Operating Profit (and a **R20,512k** boost to EBITDA via capitalized developer salaries).




* **Goodwill & M&A Cash Flow Paradox (Notes 7, 14 & 15):**
* Goodwill increased by **+R37,913k** to a closing carrying value of **R608,091k** (FY2025: R570,178k), with **nil goodwill or intangible impairment** recognised in FY2026.


* **Resolving the "+R90m M&A" vs. "+R7,914k Cash Inflow" Paradox:** While the CFO's infographic (D004, p. 17) advertises **"+R90 million"** under *Inorganic opportunities (M&A)*, the Audited Consolidated Cash Flow Statement (D008, p. 19) records a **net cash INFLOW from acquisition of businesses of +R7,914k** (Note 14, MySky) and a **net cash OUTFLOW from disposal of businesses of −R3,765k** (Note 15, where cash disposed exceeded proceeds, with only R125k of IT equipment removed in the prior period).


* **Forensic Determination:** The "+R90m" figure in the CFO review is an **illustrative capital allocation envelope / multi-year commitment budget (including deferred/contingent earn-out obligations and the third pipeline target under discussion)**, NOT actual FY2026 cash M&A expenditure. In FY2026, upfront cash paid for MySky was exceeded by the cash balance on MySky's balance sheet at acquisition date by **R7,914k**, with the remainder of the purchase price structured via **contingent consideration (earn-outs) and deferred liabilities**.







### Junction 6: Legacy Dispute / Litigation Provisions, Tax Losses & Parent-Level Intercompany ECLs

* **Initial Management Claim:** The Directors assert that all legacy litigation, onerous contracts, and regulatory matters are adequately provided for (D010.1, p. 24), and the CEO highlights **"R1.1 billion of assessed tax losses"** available to shelter future acquisition profits.


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* **Provisions Release/Utilisation (Note 21 / Note 32):** Total provisions on the balance sheet decreased by **−R12,865k** during FY2026. Combined with the **R13,479k of "non-recurring legacy items"** added back to Adjusted EBITDA (D009, p. 22), this confirms that legacy EOH-era legal/onerous-contract clean-up continues to pass through both the P&L and cash flow statement (contributing to the payables/provisions cash drain).


* **Assessed Tax Losses & Effective Tax Rate (Notes 9 & 27):**
* The Group carries **R130,366k of recognised Deferred Tax Assets (DTAs)** on the balance sheet. At the South African corporate tax rate of **27%**, a recognised DTA of R130.37m corresponds to **~R482.8m of recognised tax losses/temporary differences**, leaving **~R617.2m of the CEO's cited R1.1bn assessed tax loss pool unrecognised** (due to ring-fencing in dormant or loss-making legacy legal entities).


* In FY2026, the consolidated income tax expense was **R65,414k** on profit before tax of **R422,541k**, resulting in an **Effective Tax Rate (ETR) of 15.48%** (down from **22.38%** in FY2025), while **cash tax paid was R66,323k**. Because assessed tax losses are trapped in specific legal entities, the CEO explicitly notes (D003, p. 14) that M&A transactions must be carefully structured into specific subsidiaries to utilise the R1.1bn tax shield.




* **Parent Company Intercompany Loan ECLs (KAM #2, Separate AFS D011/D015):** The Independent Auditor's Report (D004) flagged **two Key Audit Matters (KAMs)**: (1) Goodwill and investment impairment in subsidiaries, and (2) **Expected Credit Losses (ECL) on loans to Group companies** in the Separate Financial Statements (where parent company total assets are implied at **~R1,959m** based on audit materiality of R29,000k = 1.48%). This confirms significant intercompany loan subordination and historical credit impairment between the listed parent and legacy operating subsidiaries.

### Junction 8: Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) Conversion & Capital Allocation Reality

* **Initial Management Claim:** The CEO states: *"Each year, iOCO generates over R624 million of EBITDA. Some of the cash goes to taxes and interest, but most is available for two uses: reinvesting in iOCO... and returning excess capital to shareholders."* The CFO highlights **+R80m organic investment, +R90m M&A, +R100m balance sheet resilience, and +R70m share buybacks** (total **R340m** deployed).


* **Forensic Verification & Reconciliation:**
* **Resolving the "R239m Operating Cash Inflows" vs. "R367,957k Cash Generated from Operations" Conflict (Q79 / Q101):**
* In the Going Concern note (D010.1, p. 23), the directors state that **operating cash inflows were R239m in FY2026 (down 43.5% from R423m in FY2025)**.

$$\text{Net Cash Inflow from Operating Activities} = 367,957 + 4,709 - 67,410 - 66,323 = \mathbf{R238,933k} \ (\approx \mathbf{R239m})$$

## 3. Audit of Common Surface-Reading Pitfalls

A naive reader or surface-level equity analyst relying on the front-half commentary of the Integrated Annual Report would commit eight material analytical errors:

1. **Pitfall 1: Accepting "+3% Revenue Growth" as Broad-Based IT Services Demand.**
* *The Trap:* Management highlights +3% constant-currency revenue growth to R5,753m (and +4% in IT Services) as proof of software and digital transformation momentum.


* *The Forensic Reality:* Audited nominal revenue grew +2.67% (+R149.3m to R5,732.56m), but low-margin **hardware resale jumped +49.8% (+R245.0m to R736.9m)**. Excluding hardware pass-through, core services and software revenue shrank by ~R95.6m.




2. **Pitfall 2: Treating "R624m Adjusted EBITDA" as Clean Cash-Generating Operating Flow.**
* *The Trap:* The CEO states *"Each year, iOCO generates over R624 million of EBITDA... most is available for reinvesting and returning capital"*.


* *The Forensic Reality:* Statutory EBITDA is **R591.09m**, and **Cash Generated from Operations was only R367.96m** (59.0% of Adjusted EBITDA). Furthermore, Adjusted EBITDA adds back **R13.48m of "non-recurring legacy items"** and **R17.08m of SBP**, even though restructuring from 4 to 12 BUs is ongoing and **R20.51m of internal developer salaries were capitalized** into intangibles.




3. **Pitfall 3: Believing the Chairman's Claim of "Encouraging Progress" in the International & Middle East Business.**
* *The Trap:* Both the Chairman (D002, p. 9) and CFO (D004, p. 16) praise the resilience and progress of the International segment (+2% revenue growth despite 5 months of Middle East conflict).


* *The Forensic Reality:* International segment **gross margin collapsed by 1,130 bps from 39.7% to 28.4%** as cost of sales surged by **+R58.70m** on flat revenue, compounded by a **−R16.06m FX translation loss** in OCI.




4. **Pitfall 4: Taking the CFO's "+R90m Inorganic M&A Investment" Graphic as Actual Cash Outflow.**
* *The Trap:* The capital allocation diagram (D004, p. 17) displays *"Inorganic opportunities +R90 million"* alongside *"Share buybacks +R70 million"* and *"Balance sheet resilience +R100 million"*.


* *The Forensic Reality:* The Cash Flow Statement (Note 14) proves that acquisitions generated a **net cash INFLOW of +R7,914k** because cash acquired inside MySky exceeded upfront cash consideration paid. The R90m figure includes unspent M&A budget allocations, deferred/contingent earn-out liabilities, and pipeline targets.




5. **Pitfall 5: Accepting Segmental Gross Margins (30.0%–34.6%) Without Deducting the Unallocated Central Drag.**
* *The Trap:* Evaluating IT Services (30.0% GP margin), OT (34.6% GP margin), and International (28.4% GP margin) as standalone profit generators.


* *The Forensic Reality:* The Segment Reconciliation column holds a **−R269,138k gross profit drag** (up 33.8% from −R201,073k in FY2025), which pulls the consolidated Group Gross Margin down to **27.68%**. Simultaneously, central employee costs dropped by −R128.72m as costs were shifted between lines.




6. **Pitfall 6: Mistaking Client Case-Study Metrics for iOCO Divisional Revenue.**
* *The Trap:* In Cloud SA (D005, p. 41), the report highlights *"R212.1 million in electricity and water spend in FY2026"* and *"R8.46 million savings opportunity"*; in Forensics (D007, p. 48), it highlights *"R250 million of fraudulent life and funeral assurance claims"* and *"R40 million in non-compliance penalties"*.


* *The Forensic Reality:* These are **client utility bills and client fraud-prevention outcomes**, NOT iOCO revenue.




7. **Pitfall 7: Extrapolating Divisional "Market Share Ambitions" as Credible Revenue Guidance.**
* *The Trap:* Infrastructure Solutions (D006, p. 43) states a served market of **R400 billion** and a management ambition to **"Grow market share to 2% over 36 months"**.


* *The Forensic Reality:* 2% of R400bn equals **R8.0 billion** — which is **40% larger than the entire iOCO Group's consolidated revenue (R5.73bn)** for a division with just **125 staff**. The asterisk footnote explicitly caveats that the R400bn figure is *"Calculated according to iOCO market sizing methodology"* and *"Management ambition"*.




8. **Pitfall 8: Assuming the R1.1 Billion Assessed Tax Loss Pool Can Freely Shelter Group Profits.**
* *The Trap:* Modelling a near-zero cash tax rate for iOCO until R1.1bn of taxable income is absorbed.


* *The Forensic Reality:* iOCO paid **R66.32m in cash taxes** in FY2026. Only **R130.37m of DTAs** (~R483m of losses) are recognised on the balance sheet because the remaining ~R617m of assessed tax losses are ring-fenced inside specific legacy legal entities and can only be utilised if profitable operations or newly acquired targets are legally housed inside those exact entities.
