#### 2. Economic Mechanics & Quantification of the Unit Cost Distortion (+US$/oz)



Why does processing profitable third-party gold-bearing material **increase** Group AISC?

In South African toll-treatment / ore purchase arrangements, third-party feedstock is purchased from artisanal/surface suppliers or junior miners at a contractual percentage of the **prevailing spot gold price** (typically **60% to 70%** of contained gold value), plus transport, sampling, and high-cyanide/reagent milling costs (`D003` p. 12 & Note 12 fn 1).



* In FY26, with the average realised gold price surging to **US$4,235/oz**, the purchase + processing cost of third-party buy-in ounces is ~**US$2,900/oz to US$3,050/oz** (generating a healthy **US$1,200–US$1,335/oz** cash margin, or ~**28%–31%**, to utilise spare mill capacity at Kinross, Barberton, and MTR).

* However, because **US$2,950/oz** is substantially higher than Pan African’s organic AISC, blending these ounces into the consolidated denominator inflates reported unit costs.



**Exact Forensic Quantification of the Distortion:**



1. **Evander Mines Underground Distortion:**

* In Note 3 (`D006.2`, p. 21 & 23), Evander’s "Processing and metallurgy" cost jumped by **US$17.19m** (+51.5%, from US$33,349k to US$50,541k) as third-party tonnes at the Kinross plant surged nearly 6-fold from 34,411t in FY25 to **202,555t** (**6,609 oz**) in FY26.





* Reported Evander Mines AISC (`D008.2`, p. 42) is **US$90.2m** across **47,112 oz** sold = **US$1,914/oz**.





* Attributing the **US$19.5m** of third-party ore purchase and dedicated circuit processing costs (~US$2,950/oz $\times$ 6,609 oz) to the buy-in stream leaves US$70.7m of organic AISC across **40,503 organic oz sold**:



$$\text{Evander True Organic AISC} = \frac{\text{US\$70.7m}}{40,503\text{ oz}} = \mathbf{US\$1,745/oz}$$





* **Evander Unit Cost Distortion:** Third-party buy-in material distorted Evander’s reported AISC upward by **+US$169/oz** (within a forensic sensitivity band of **+US$160/oz to +US$185/oz** depending on fixed plant overhead absorption).

### Junction 7: South African Section 36 Mining Tax & Cash Tax Step-Up Dynamics



#### 1. Mechanics of the South African Gold Mining Tax Formula & Section 36



South African gold mining companies are taxed under two specialised statutory regimes that create a massive divergence between **Accounting Tax Expense (IAS 12)** and **Cash Tax Paid (SARS)**:



1. **The Gold Mining Tax Formula:**



$$Y = 33 - \frac{165}{X}$$







Where $Y$ is the percentage tax rate applied to gold mining taxable income, and $X$ is the **profitability ratio**—defined as **Taxable Mining Income (after 100% capital expenditure redemption)** divided by **Gross Gold Mining Revenue**, expressed as a percentage ($X = \frac{\text{Taxable Income post-Capex}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100$).

* **Tax Tunnel:** If $X \le 5\%$, then $Y = 0\%$ (complete tax exemption).

* **Asymptotic Ceiling:** In a boom year where $X = 50\%$, $Y = 33 - \frac{165}{50} = \mathbf{29.70\%}$. Even at $X = 60\%$, the formula rate is **$30.25\%$** (and mathematically can never reach $33\%$). Non-mining income (such as corporate/treasury interest) is taxed at the standard SA corporate rate of **27.0%** (`D006.4`, p. 26), while Australian operations (Tennant Mines) are taxed at the federal corporate rate of **30.0%**.









2. **Section 36(11) of the SA Income Tax Act (100% Upfront Capex Redemption):**

Unlike normal companies that depreciate PP&E over its life-of-mine, SA gold mines deduct **100% of qualifying capital expenditure (both sustaining and expansion)** against taxable mining income in the exact year it is incurred. Any capex exceeding current-year mining profit is carried forward indefinitely as an **Unredeemed Capital Expenditure (UCR)** tax shield (ring-fenced to that specific mining right).



#### 2. Why FY26 Cash Tax Was Only 12.3% of EBITDA (US$74.1m) vs. 33.2% Accounting ETR (US$177.5m)



In FY26, Pan African reported Profit Before Tax (PBT) of **US$534,368k** and an Accounting Income Tax Expense of **US$177,457k** (**33.21% ETR**), yet **Cash Income Tax Paid** on the Cash Flow Statement (`D005.1`, p. 18) was only **US$74,135k** (**12.16%** of Adjusted EBITDA / **13.87%** of PBT).



Note 6.1 and Note 6.2 (`D006.4`, pp. 25–26) prove the exact mechanics:



* **Total Accounting Tax (US$177,457k)** = Current Tax of **US$80,285k** (SA current tax **US$73,486k** + Aus current tax **US$6,799k**) + Deferred Tax Charge of **US$97,172k**. (Cash tax paid of **US$74,135k** is simply the **US$80,285k** current tax charge less the **US$11,352k** net increase in current tax liabilities on the balance sheet from $435k to $11,868k, plus FX/provisional tax timing).





* **Why Current Tax Was Only US$80.3m (and Cash Tax US$74.1m):**

1. **MTR Operation (Zero Cash Tax in FY26):** Look at Note 3 (`D006.2`, p. 21): MTR generated **US$139,116k** of PBT and recorded an accounting tax expense of **US$38,605k** (27.75% ETR), yet **100% of MTR's tax charge was non-cash Deferred Tax**!
