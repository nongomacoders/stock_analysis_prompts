### 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

This forensic audit of Telkom’s FY2026 Integrated Report and Annual Financial Statements reveals a group in a state of successful, albeit challenging, transition. The headline narrative of "market-leading growth" and "resilient free cash flow" is substantiated by the underlying financial data, but only when viewed through the lens of specific, non-obvious accounting and operational adjustments.

**True Economic Performance:** The group's core operations are healthier than the 1.4% revenue growth suggests. The true driver of profitability is a significant, structural reduction in the cost base, particularly in employee and maintenance expenses, which outpaced the decline in legacy revenue. The 5.8% growth in EBITDA is a direct result of this cost discipline, not just top-line momentum. The reported 21.5% HEPS growth is a cleaner indicator of this operational improvement than BEPS, which was distorted by prior-year one-off items.

**Balance Sheet Health:** The balance sheet is robust. The reported net debt to EBITDA of 0.5x is a conservative and accurate reflection of the group's leverage. A deeper analysis reveals that the group is in a **net cash position of R1.19 billion** when excluding lease liabilities, a critical distinction that highlights significant financial flexibility. The decrease in cash from R11.0bn to R7.7bn is fully explained by the prior year's once-off Swiftnet proceeds, not operational cash burn.

**Post-Balance-Sheet Liquidity Runway:** Liquidity is exceptionally strong. With R7.7 billion in cash, R6.4 billion in undrawn facilities, and a free cash flow generation of R3.1 billion, the group has a substantial runway to fund its R6.4 billion capex program, meet its debt obligations, and sustain its enhanced dividend policy without requiring new external financing.

**Key Findings:**
1.  **Revenue Quality is Improving:** The growth in mobile data (10.5%) and fibre revenue (8.2%) is real and is successfully offsetting the anticipated decline in legacy fixed-voice services.
2.  **Profitability is Cost-Driven:** The EBITDA margin expansion to 28.1% is a result of deliberate cost optimization, not just operating leverage from revenue growth.
3.  **Balance Sheet is Fortress-Like:** The net cash position (excluding leases) and low leverage provide a significant margin of safety and strategic optionality.
4.  **Cash Flow is Sustainable:** The R3.1bn FCF is supported by underlying operational improvements and lower finance costs, not just one-off working capital movements.
5.  **Key Risks are Acknowledged:** The auditors' key audit matter on revenue recognition (IFRS 15) and management's disclosure of the Google Equiano financing component are critical areas of accounting judgment that have been adequately disclosed and addressed.

### 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

#### Junction 1: Service Revenue vs. Device Financing (IFRS 15)

*   **Initial Claim (IAR):** The GCEO and GCFO reports highlight strong "mobile service revenue growth of 6.8%" and "mobile data revenue growth of 10.5%". The narrative focuses on the success of the data-led strategy.
*   **Forensic Reconciliation (AFS):**
    *   **Note 3.2.1 (Disaggregation of Revenue):** This note is the key. It separates revenue into "recognised over time" (service revenue) and "recognised at a point in time" (device sales).
    *   **Note 3.2 (Significant Judgements):** The note explicitly details the "significant financing component" in contracts. It states that for contracts over 12 months, a financing element is separated. This is crucial. It means a portion of the revenue from a handset contract is not device revenue but interest revenue, which is recognised over time.
    *   **Note 3.2.4.1 (Contract Assets):** This note reveals that Telkom derecognises contract assets by selling them to financial institutions (factoring). The gross amount factored was R1.245 billion, with a profit of R209 million (Note 3.3). This is a significant off-balance-sheet financing activity that accelerates cash collection but complicates the revenue picture.
*   **Determination:** The reported service revenue growth is genuine. However, a naive reader might misinterpret the total revenue growth. The forensic reality is that Telkom is effectively acting as a financing provider for device sales, and it is actively managing this book by factoring receivables. The "service revenue" line is the true indicator of recurring operational performance, and its 6.8% growth is a solid result. The factoring of contract assets is a sophisticated cash flow management tool, not a sign of underlying distress.

#### Junction 2: ARPU Trajectory & Subscriber Churn

*   **Initial Claim (IAR):** The GCEO report states, "post-paid subscribers and average revenue per user (ARPU) remained stable." It also highlights "pre-paid subscriber growth of 11.9%".
*   **Forensic Reconciliation (AFS):**
    *   **Note 3.1 (Segment Information):** The segment report shows Telkom Consumer revenue growing from R27.689bn to R28.774bn. This is consistent with the narrative.
    *   **Note 3.2.3 (Customer Relationship Periods):** This note is a critical forensic anchor. It states the average customer relationship period for voice is 5.5 years, wholesale is 4 years, and non-voice is 3.5 years. These periods are used to amortise installation costs and revenue. A stable relationship period suggests that churn is not accelerating, which corroborates the "stable ARPU" claim.
    *   **Note 3.2.4.2 (Contract Costs):** The note shows that contract costs capitalised (commissions) are amortised over the contract term. A stable amortisation charge (R271m vs R266m) despite revenue growth suggests that the cost of acquiring a subscriber is being managed efficiently.
*   **Determination:** The claim of stable ARPU is credible. The growth is coming from expanding the subscriber base (especially pre-paid) and increasing data usage per subscriber, not from price hikes. The stable customer relationship periods are a strong indicator of healthy churn levels. The forensic risk here is that ARPU is a blended metric; the growth in data ARPU could be masking a decline in voice ARPU, but the overall stability is a positive sign.

#### Junction 3: Tower Lease Commitments & IFRS 16 Liabilities

*   **Initial Claim (IAR):** The balance sheet shows lease liabilities of R7.556 billion. The cash flow statement shows a R1.91 billion repayment of principal lease liabilities. The narrative mentions "lower lease liability repayments" as a driver of FCF.
*   **Forensic Reconciliation (AFS):**
    *   **Note 6.3 (Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities):** This note is a goldmine. It details that the group leases network equipment (masts and towers), property, and vehicles.
    *   **Note 6.3.1 (Right-of-use assets):** The reconciliation shows "New leases entered into" of R1.193bn and "Lease remeasurements" of R1.417bn. This is a massive non-cash addition to the lease liability. The remeasurements are due to CPI escalations and lease modifications. This means the R7.556bn liability is not static; it is growing due to inflation and contract changes.
    *   **Note 3.7 (Changes in liabilities):** This note confirms the non-cash movements. It shows "New leases entered into" (R1.193bn), "Lease remeasurement" (R1.417bn), and "IFRS 16 interest capitalised" (R630m) all adding to the lease liability.
    *   **Note 6.7.2 (Lease commitments):** This note discloses "Future payments on leases committed but not yet commenced" of R359 million, up from R10 million. This is a significant increase and represents a future cash flow obligation not yet on the balance sheet.
*   **Determination:** The IFRS 16 liability is a major, and growing, financial obligation. The "lower lease liability repayments" in FCF are somewhat misleading. While principal repayments were lower, the total liability increased by R636 million (from R6.920bn to R7.556bn) due to new leases and remeasurements. A naive reader might see the lower cash repayment as a permanent improvement. The forensic reality is that the underlying lease obligations are escalating, and the R359m in committed but not-yet-commenced leases is a future headwind to FCF.

#### Junction 4: Spectrum Auction Capex vs. Network Modernization Capex

*   **Initial Claim (IAR):** The GCEO report mentions the "approval of a transaction between a mobile network operator and a fibre infrastructure provider" and the "last spectrum auction materially improved industry capacity." The GCFO report states capex was R6.434 billion, focused on "mobile and fibre infrastructure, network modernisation."
*   **Forensic Reconciliation (AFS):**
    *   **Note 5.1 (Property, Plant and Equipment):** This note breaks down the R5.626bn in PP&E additions. It states: "Expansion of the mobile network contributed 38% of the additions... 28% of capital expenditure was on the deployment of fibre, 14% was on the next-generation Packet Optical Transport Network (POTN) core network, and 13% relates to information technology solutions."
    *   **Note 5.2 (Intangible Assets):** This note shows R808m in additions, primarily for software (R603m) and under-construction assets (R205m). This aligns with the "IT and digital platform investment" narrative.
    *   **Note 6.7.1 (Capital Commitments):** This note shows authorised capital expenditure not yet contracted at R8.544 billion, a massive increase from R3.913 billion. This indicates a significant pipeline of future capex.
*   **Determination:** The capex is clearly directed towards growth and modernization, not just maintenance. The breakdown in Note 5.1 confirms the strategic focus on mobile and fibre. The key forensic finding is the huge increase in authorised but uncontracted capital commitments (R8.5bn). This signals that the R6.4bn capex in FY2026 is not a peak; it is part of a multi-year investment cycle. This has significant implications for future FCF, as this committed capex will need to be funded.

#### Junction 5: Foreign Exchange Remeasurement & Hyperinflation Adjustments

*   **Initial Claim (IAR):** The GCFO report notes that "earnings were further supported by... a 28.3% decrease in finance charges" and that performance was "partially impacted by negative fair value adjustments from the rand strengthening against the US dollar."
*   **Forensic Reconciliation (AFS):**
    *   **Note 6.2 (Net finance charges and fair value movements):** This note shows a foreign exchange gain of R145m and fair value adjustments of -R372m. The net impact is a loss of R227m. The note explains the loss is "mainly due to the strengthening of the rand against the US dollar which was not anticipated when foreign exchange contracts were entered into."
    *   **Note 7.1.6 (Market risk):** This note provides a sensitivity analysis. A 10% strengthening of the rand would have a negative R116m impact on profit. This is a moderate exposure.
    *   **Note 2.2.1 (New accounting pronouncements):** The note discusses the IAS 21 amendment regarding hyperinflationary economies but states it is "not expected to have a material impact on the Group." This is a key disclosure. It confirms that Telkom's foreign operations are not in hyperinflationary economies (e.g., Zimbabwe), and therefore, no complex hyperinflation accounting adjustments are required.
*   **Determination:** The foreign exchange exposure is real but manageable. The loss is due to hedging activities (forward exchange contracts) that moved against the company. This is a normal part of treasury operations for a company with international procurement. The explicit statement that hyperinflation accounting is not applicable is a critical finding that simplifies the analysis and removes a potential area of significant accounting distortion.

### 3. Audit of Common Surface-Reading Pitfalls

A naive reader of the Telkom report would likely make the following errors:

1.  **Pitfall: "Telkom's cash position collapsed by 30%."**
    *   **Forensic Reality:** The R3.3 billion decrease in cash is almost entirely explained by the prior year's once-off R6.6 billion Swiftnet disposal proceeds. The prior year's cash balance was artificially inflated. The current R7.7 billion cash balance is the result of strong, recurring free cash flow generation of R3.1 billion, which is a sign of underlying health, not distress.

2.  **Pitfall: "The company's debt is 0.5x EBITDA, which is low, but its lease liabilities are huge."**
    *   **Forensic Reality:** This is a correct observation but an incomplete conclusion. The 0.5x net debt to EBITDA ratio *includes* lease liabilities. The forensic finding is that **excluding** lease liabilities, Telkom is in a net cash position of R1.19 billion. This is a crucial distinction that demonstrates immense financial strength and flexibility, far beyond what the headline 0.5x ratio suggests.

3.  **Pitfall: "HEPS grew 21.5%, so the company is firing on all cylinders."**
    *   **Forensic Reality:** The 21.5% HEPS growth is impressive, but it's partly due to a lower finance charge (down 28.3%) and a lower effective tax rate. The core driver was a 5.8% increase in EBITDA, which itself was driven more by cost-cutting (employee expenses down 3.7%, maintenance down 5.9%) than by revenue growth (up only 1.4%). A naive reader would over-attribute the success to revenue growth, while a forensic auditor recognizes it as a cost-driven, margin-expansion story.

4.  **Pitfall: "The company is investing R6.4 billion in capex, which is a huge drag on cash flow."**
    *   **Forensic Reality:** While capex is high, it is strategically allocated to growth areas (mobile and fibre). More importantly, the R8.5 billion in *authorised but uncontracted* capital commitments (Note 6.7.1) reveals that this is a multi-year investment cycle. A naive reader sees a one-year cash outflow; a forensic auditor sees a sustained future funding requirement that must be factored into long-term cash flow projections.
