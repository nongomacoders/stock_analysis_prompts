## 1. EXECUTIVE FORENSIC AUDIT SUMMARY

### 1.1 True economic performance
The Group’s headline “core” presentation is materially stronger than its IFRS and cash-flow reality:

- **Core revenue and other income:** R1,610m; **core EBITDA:** R790m.
- **IFRS revenue:** R711m; **IFRS operating loss:** R(79)m; **IFRS loss after tax:** R(63)m.
- **Operating cash flow:** **negative R(111)m**.
- **Core EBITDA is not cash flow.** The R790m core EBITDA is a pre-interest, pre-tax, pre-PBD-amortisation measure. It does not reflect the cash cost of replenishing the PBD book, interest paid, or working-capital absorption.

The principal PBD book is barely profitable before fair-value gains:

| PBD principal book — H1 2026 | Rm |
|---|---:|
| CAEIR imputed interest revenue | 383 |
| Interest expense | (177) |
| Impairment loss on principal book | (167) |
| **Net principal-book income before FV gains** | **39** |
| Fair-value movements on other financial assets | 30 |
| **Net income from PBD per IFRS** | **69** |

On average principal-book carrying value of approximately R3,910m, the R39m pre-FV net income is only **~1.0% for the half-year, or ~2.0% annualised**. The R167m impairment charge is **~4.9% of average gross carrying value for the half-year, or ~9.8% annualised**, and **21.6% of gross collections**. The risk-adjusted NIM is therefore thin and highly dependent on fair-value gains and collection outperformance.

### 1.2 Balance sheet and liquidity
- **Total assets:** R6,444m; **total equity:** R1,329m.
- **NAV per ordinary share:** R1,329m / 780.651m shares = **R1.70/share**. This is the correct statutory ordinary NAV. The R5,410m share capital line equals **R6.93/share** and is **not** NAV.
- **PBD carrying value:** R4,489m, of which **R3,845m principal book is 100% pledged** as security for interest-bearing liabilities and overdrafts. Trade receivables and cash are also pledged.
- **Interest-bearing liabilities including overdrafts:** R4,016m. **R1,396m matures within 12 months**; R2,620m thereafter.
- **Confirmed undrawn overdraft facilities:** R475m. The management claim of “approximately R0.8 billion in unutilised facilities” is **not corroborated** by the captured debt note. No covenant ratios, LTV, interest-cover, net-debt/EBITDA, or ABS trigger disclosures are provided.
- **Cash and cash equivalents:** R112m; other investments R81m. Cash is partly pledged. Liquidity headroom is therefore tight relative to the 12-month maturity wall and negative operating cash flow.

### 1.3 Key forensic findings
1. **PBD “ECL allowance” of R431m is not loss coverage.** It is a **positive cumulative impairment gain** added to gross carrying value: gross R3,414m + cumulative gain R431m = net R3,845m. The 12.6% ratio is a cumulative outperformance percentage, not a conventional loss reserve. The reduction from R624m to R431m is a **R193m deterioration in expected outperformance**, driven by payer drop-offs and reduced settlement activity.
2. **PBD amortisation of R597m reconciles** once signs are correctly applied: R728m net collections + R116m other cash collections + R167m impairment − R383m CAEIR interest − R30m FV gains = **R598m**, rounding to R597m. The earlier apparent arithmetic failure was a sign-reading error.
3. **PBD amortisation must not be treated as a non-cash add-back** in any cash-flow or FCFE analysis. Portfolio replenishment is required to sustain collections. Net cash from PBD before interest was R728m + R116m − R382m − R28m = **R434m**, not R597m of “add-back”.
4. **IFRS 9 staging:** PBDs are purchased credit-impaired (POCI) and permanently Stage 3. No Stage 1/2 migration is possible. ECL is modelled on a **rolling 12-month net cash-flow horizon**, not full lifetime. This is a methodological concern for lifetime ECL adequacy; no vintage loss curves, DPD buckets, or tail beyond 12 months are disclosed.
5. **Trade receivables ECL is lenient:** 0% provision until 90+ days overdue; 90+ days fully provided. Total ECL rate 1.34%. The book is small (R374m gross), but the 90-DPD threshold is a policy flag.
6. **ERC of R8,366m is unverified.** It is not referenced in the primary financial statements or PBD note. No methodology, discount rate, or reconciliation to IFRS carrying value is provided. The implied ERC/carrying value ratio is 8,366 / 4,489 = **1.86x**.
7. **H1 2026 is unaudited and unreviewed.** All statements are marked “Unaudited”; the auditors have not reviewed or reported on them. Restatement risk is material.
8. **No subsequent SENS disclosures are included in the dossier.** Post-balance-sheet liquidity runway, refinancing, covenant waivers, or restatements cannot be verified.

## 2. DETAILED FORENSIC REASONING & DETERMINATIONS

### 2.1 IFRS 9 Stage 1/2/3 ECL migration and qualitative macro overlays

**PBDs — POCI / Stage 3 by definition**
- Note 6.1 states PBDs are classified as purchased credit-impaired (POCI) financial assets (**Stage 3**) on initial recognition. The classification is irrevocable, even if expected cash flows return to full contractual terms.
- Therefore, there is **no Stage 1/2/3 migration** for the PBD principal book. The entire R3,845m net principal book is credit-impaired from acquisition.
- The “ECL allowance” of R431m is a **cumulative impairment gain**, not a loss allowance. It is added to gross carrying value to arrive at net carrying value. The economic meaning is that collections are expected to outperform the acquisition base case by R431m. The reduction from R624m at 31 March 2025 and R598m at 30 September 2025 to R431m at 31 March 2026 is a **deterioration in expected outperformance**, not a collapse in loss coverage.

**12-month rolling ECL horizon**
- Note 6.1 states each PBD is modelled on a **rolling 12-month net cash-flow period**, discounted at the credit-adjusted effective interest rate (CAEIR).
- IFRS 9 requires lifetime ECL for Stage 3/POCI assets. A rolling 12-month horizon may understate lifetime loss allowance if the collection tail extends beyond 12 months. No tail methodology, vintage loss curves, or sensitivity analysis is disclosed.
- **Determination:** The 12-month rolling horizon is a **methodological concern**; lifetime ECL adequacy is **NOT VERIFIED**.

**Macro overlay**
- Management attributes accelerated amortisation to “forward looking macro-economic assumptions including an elevated interest rate forecast relative to our expectations” and to “interest rates, inflation and consumer health & payment behaviour.”
- CAEIR rose to **19.2%** (31 March 2025: 17.9%; 30 September 2025: 18.4%). The increase mechanically inflates imputed interest revenue and amortisation. The precise split between portfolio ageing/volume and assumption changes is **not disclosed**.
- **Determination:** The macro overlay is qualitatively disclosed but **not quantified**. The CAEIR increase is a double-edged sword: it raises P&L interest revenue but also raises the discount rate applied to future collections, depressing carrying value. The economic driver — market rates vs credit-risk premium — is **NOT VERIFIED**.

**Trade receivables — SICR and 90-DPD threshold**
- Note 6.1 trade receivables ECL table shows:
  - Current: 0.00% loss rate
  - 30 days overdue: 0.00%
  - 61–90 days overdue: 0.00%
  - 90+ days overdue: 100.00%
  - Total ECL rate: 1.34%
- This is a **90-DPD provisioning threshold**, not a 30-DPD backstop. The “low credit risk” exemption is applied. The book is small (gross R374m; loss allowance R5m), but the policy is lenient relative to standard Stage 2 migration at 30 days.
- **Determination:** The 90-DPD threshold is a policy flag. Given the small balance, it is not a material balance-sheet risk, but it is **not a conservative ECL policy**. SICR policy and 30-DPD backstop are **NOT VERIFIED**.

**ERC R8,366m**
- ERC is disclosed only in the Section 1 highlights. It is **not referenced in the primary financial statements or PBD note**.
- No methodology, discount rate, or reconciliation to IFRS carrying value is provided. The implied ERC/carrying value ratio is 8,366 / 4,489 = **1.86x** (H1 2025: 7,808 / 4,353 = 1.79x).
- **Determination:** The carrying value arithmetic is verified. The ERC of R8,366m is a management metric and is **NOT VERIFIED** against the IFRS carrying value or collection curves.

**Fair value vs carrying value**
- Note 6.3 states the fair value of principal book portfolios is determined using a current market-related discount rate of **18.50%** (31 March 2025: 15.81%; 30 September 2025: 15.93%), with expected cash flows adjusted for risk. Management states fair value approximates carrying value (both R3,845m).
- CAEIR is **19.2%**. A lower discount rate (18.50%) would normally increase present value, but management states cash flows are also risk-adjusted downward. The net effect is not independently verifiable.
- **Determination:** The fair value approximation is **plausible but NOT VERIFIED**. The 70bps gap between CAEIR and market discount rate is a tension that requires the cash-flow risk adjustment to be disclosed.

### FINAL AUDITOR’S CONCLUSION

Nutun’s H1 2026 presentation shows a business with **strong headline collections and core EBITDA**, but **weak IFRS profitability, negative operating cash flow, a thin risk-adjusted PBD NIM, a fully encumbered PBD book, a material 12-month maturity wall, and unverified covenant headroom**. The PBD accounting is POCI/Stage 3, and the R431m “ECL allowance” is a **positive cumulative impairment gain**, not loss coverage. The R597m PBD amortisation reconciles once signs are correctly applied, but it must not be treated as a non-cash add-back. The correct NAV anchor is **R1.70/share**, not R6.93. The R0.8bn unutilised facilities claim is **not corroborated**; only R475m undrawn overdraft is quantified. No subsequent SENS disclosures are available, so post-balance-sheet liquidity and refinancing remain **NOT VERIFIED**. The H1 2026 results are unaudited and unreviewed, and restatement risk is material.
