### Core Institutional Investment Thesis

We initiate/update our institutional coverage on **Accelerate Property Fund Limited (JSE: APF)** with a contrarian **SELL** recommendation and a 12-month target price of **R0.48 per share** (48.0 ZAR cents), implying **-20.0% downside** from the FY2026 reference price of R0.60 per share and a **0.0% forward dividend yield** for FY2027E.

While surface-level market commentary has cheered APF’s apparent FY2026 turnaround—specifically the swing in reported SA REIT Funds From Operations (FFO) from a loss of -3.97 cents per share (cps) in FY2025 to **+6.25 cps (R127.84 million)** in FY2026 and the narrowing of the IFRS net loss from R1,269.6 million to **R45.9 million**—our forensic audit of the consolidated financial statements and segmental disclosures demonstrates that **APF’s underlying operational cash flow remains deeply value-destructive**.

At R0.60 per share, APF trades at a seemingly deep **66.9% discount to its reported SA REIT NAV of R1.81 per share** (R3,701.0 million book equity across 2,045.495 million net shares). However, we view this discount as a classic **distressed REIT value trap** rather than an institutional buying opportunity, driven by four structural realities:

1. **Illusory FFO Turnaround & Negative Free Cash Flow (AFFO):** Reported FY2026 SA REIT FFO of R127.84 million (6.25 cps) was artificially propped up by the inclusion of the non-recurring **R82.5 million** (excl. VAT) Covid-19 business interruption insurance settlement inside `Other income` (which spiked from R5.92 million in FY25 to R167.06 million in FY26). Stripping out this one-off cash windfall reveals that **sustainable recurring FFO was just R45.34 million (2.22 cps)**. Against annual capital expenditure of **R110.35 million**, APF generated a recurring **Adjusted Funds From Operations (AFFO) deficit of -R65.0 million (-3.18 cps)**, requiring debt redraws and rights issue proceeds simply to fund defensive capex.


2. **Severe Negative Financial Leverage (6.44% Property Yield vs. 9.90% Cost of Debt):** Across a total property portfolio valued at **R6,600.0 million**, APF generated cash Net Property Income (NPI excluding straight-line accruals) of **R424.77 million**—an implied backward-looking cash yield of just **6.44%**. More alarmingly, the Segmental Analysis (p. 202) reveals that APF’s flagship 50% stake in **Fourways Mall**—carried on the balance sheet at **R4,228.8 million** (64.1% of Group property value)—generated cash NPI of just **R125.03 million** ($\text{R121.74m IFRS NPI} + \text{R3.28m straight-line reversal}$), equating to an **implied cash yield of 2.96%**! Funding a 2.96%–6.44% yielding property base with debt costing a blended **9.90%** locks in permanent economic value erosion for equity holders.


3. **Acute FY2027E Commercial Office Income Cliffs:** In FY2026, the small Commercial Office portfolio (R970.6 million fair value; 48,427 m² GLA) cross-subsidised the Group by generating **R191.22 million of Cash NPI** ($\text{R139.22m IFRS NPI} + \text{R52.00m straight-line reversal}$), or **45.0% of total Group Cash NPI**. 4. **The 31 March 2027 Debt Maturity Cliff, ICR Covenant Breach & Swap Over-Hedging:** **100% of APF’s R3,002.0 million debt stack matures on 31 March 2027**, creating a R2,328.3 million working capital deficit. ### Resolution of the Stage 1 Sectoral Vacancy Anomaly

Stage 1 flagged a critical mathematical contradiction on page 85 of the Integrated Report: Retail vacancy of 11.0% on 187,495 m² (20,624 m²) plus Commercial vacancy of 45.2% on 48,427 m² (21,889 m²) equals **42,513 m² (18.0%)**, which contradicts the audited Group vacant GLA of **25,631 m² (10.9%)**. Cross-referencing the property-level vacancy disclosures (pages 72–75 and 182) resolves this:

* At 31 March 2026, Fourways Mall was 9.7% vacant ($8,753\text{ m}^2$), Cedar Square was 1.4% vacant ($619\text{ m}^2$), the 5 transferred retail properties contained $9,430\text{ m}^2$ of vacant GLA, and BMW Fourways + Beacon Isle contained $780\text{ m}^2$ of vacant GLA. Including ~1,042 m² at Bela-Bela (6.5% vacant) sums to **$20,624\text{ m}^2$ (11.0% Retail vacancy)**.


* Subtracting $20,624\text{ m}^2$ of Retail vacancy from total portfolio vacancy of $25,631\text{ m}^2$ proves that **true in-force Commercial Office vacancy at 31 March 2026 was exactly $5,007\text{ m}^2$ (10.34% of 48,427 m²)**—comprising $2,553\text{ m}^2$ at Citibank (20.5% of 12,453 m²) and $2,454\text{ m}^2$ at Thomas Pattullo (41.1% of 5,967 m² following Altron’s exit), while KPMG Crescent, Oceana House, KPMG Polokwane, and KPMG Secunda were 0% vacant at 31 March 2026. The 45.2% figure printed on page 85 erroneously divided the prior-year unadjusted office vacant GLA (~21,889 m² before Portside/Charles Crescent/Pri-movie disposals) by the smaller closing GLA of 48,427 m².


* In **FY2027E**, however, true Commercial Office vacancy *will* spike sharply on **31 July 2026** when Oceana Group vacates Oceana House (7,597 m²). Assuming iSON Experiences and partial re-letting absorb ~2,597 m² across Oceana House and Thomas Pattullo, net Commercial vacant GLA will rise by +5,000 m² to **10,007 m² (20.66% of 48,427 m²)** at 31 March 2027E.

## 4. Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) NAV Valuation & Yield Benchmarks

### Forensic Audit of Book Valuations vs. Economic Capitalisation Rates

As highlighted in Stage 1 Junction 6, PwC’s Key Audit Matter (pp. 187–188) confirms that APF values its properties on a 3-year rotational external basis, with management valuing the remainder internally. When we test the balance sheet carrying values against the actual segmental Cash NPI disclosed on page 202 of the AFS, severe overvaluation at Fourways Mall and KPMG Crescent becomes undeniable:

1. **Fourways Mall (50% Share — Book Value: R4,228.80 million; 90,242 m²):**
* At R4,228.80 million, Fourways Mall is carried at **R46,861/m²**. Yet in FY2026, it generated Cash NPI of only **R125.03 million** (2.96% implied cash yield). Even if we credit Fourways Mall with 100% of its segment `Other income` (R84.25 million, bringing total segment cash inflow to R209.27 million), the implied yield is still just **4.95%**.


* In our FY2027E forecast, with Fourways View launching on 1 October 2026, vacancy dropping to 7.5%, and solar PV savings kicking in, Fourways Mall’s sustainable forward Cash NPI rises to **R141.50 million** (and **R156.80 million** in FY2028E).


* In the South African institutional property market, super-regional malls with recovery potential and dominant nodal footprints trade at **exit capitalisation rates of 8.00% to 8.50%** (and discount rates of 13.0%–13.5%). Capitalising Fourways Mall’s stabilised forward NPI + structural solar/ancillary income (~R165.0 million stabilised run-rate) at an institutional **8.25% exit cap rate** yields a warranted economic value of **R2,000.0 million** (R22,163/m²)—representing a **-R2,228.8 million (-52.7%) economic haircut** to APF’s carrying value! Even if an institutional buyer paid a generous 6.50% initial yield on R165.0m to capture long-term bulk/turnaround upside, the asset is worth at most **R2,538.5 million** (-40.0% below book).


2. **Retained Other Retail — Cedar Square & Bela-Bela (Book Value: R918.80 million; 60,201 m²):**
* At 31 March 2026, non-Fourways Retail comprised R1,023.80 million in continuing `Investment property` plus R376.80 million in `Held-for-sale` = **R1,400.60 million**. Deducting the 7 sold/concluded retail properties (R483.3m total book value excluding R17m Beacon Isle if in Commercial/Retail, or R500.3m total pipeline) leaves **~R900.3 million** for Cedar Square (44,210 m²) and Bela-Bela (15,991 m²).


* Forward FY2027E Cash NPI on these two well-let retail assets (1.4% vacancy at Cedar Square) is **R77.85 million** (rising to **R83.13 million** in FY2028E)—an implied book yield of **8.65%**. Capitalising FY2027E NPI of R77.85 million at a market-related **9.00% cap rate** yields **R865.0 million** (only a modest -3.9% adjustment to book value, confirming that Cedar Square and Bela-Bela are realistically valued).




3. **Commercial Offices (Continuing Book Value: R770.60 million; HFS: R200.00 million; Total: R970.60 million; 48,427 m²):**
* While FY2026 Cash NPI was R191.22 million (a 19.7% backward yield inflated by KPMG’s expiring R432/m² lease), forward FY2027E Cash NPI drops to **R152.40 million** (and **R133.00 million** in FY2028E excluding Cape Town).


* Applying an institutional **10.75% exit cap rate** to the continuing Gauteng/regional office portfolio (KPMG Crescent post-reset, Citibank Sandton at 20.5% vacancy, KPMG Polokwane/Secunda; forward stabilised NPI of ~R130.0m) yields **R605.0 million** (vs. R770.6m book, a **-R165.6m / -21.5% write-down** reflecting the KPMG rent reset), while the Cape Town HFS office assets (Oceana House & Thomas Pattullo) are valued at their expected net realisable disposal value of **R175.0 million** (vs. R200.0m book, a -12.5% discount following Oceana’s exit).

## 6. Actionable Portfolio Monitoring Triggers & Catalysts ("What to Watch For That Will Move the Stock")

### A. Operational & Balance Sheet Red Lines (Downside Triggers)

1. **Failure to Secure Formal Lender Extension of the 1.1x ICR Waiver:** APF’s actual ICR is 1.20x (and projected at 1.13x in FY2027E), whereas the contractual covenant stepped up on 1 April 2026 to **1.4x** (Audit Committee Report, p. 168) or **1.6x** (CFO Review, p. 45). Any refusal by the seven-lender syndicate to formally extend the **1.1x ICR threshold** through 31 March 2027, or any drop in actual ICR below **1.05x**, triggers an immediate event of default and potential distressed equity cure (a third dilutive rights issue at $\le\text{R0.30/share}$).


2. **KPMG Crescent Renewal Below R210/m² or Space Downsizing (>25% GLA Contraction):** KPMG Crescent (20,096 m² at R432.00/m² gross rental) represents ~R104.2 million of annualised gross revenue. Our base case models a downward market rent reset to ~R250/m² (-R33m to -R40m annualised impact). If KPMG downsizes its Parktown footprint by >5,000 m² or resets below **R210/m²**, Group ICR falls below **1.0x**, wiping out 100% of recurring FFO.


3. **Fourways Mall Cash Collection Ratio Dropping Below 90.0%:** Fourways Mall Q4 FY2026 cash collections averaged **92.81%** (~R3.5m/month shortfall). A deterioration below **90.0%**—or failure of the **Fourways View** lifestyle precinct (4,342 m²) to launch on **1 October 2026**—would stall the mall's NPI recovery and breach lender milestone covenants.


4. **Covenant LTV Re-Breaching 43.0% Post-Valuations:** While BMW Fourways’ transfer lowers pro-forma LTV to **39.6%**, any year-end property fair value write-down exceeding **R350 million** (or failure to execute the R1.2 billion SPV 1 disposal target) that pushes Covenant LTV back above **43.0%** permanently locks the Section 46 dividend blockade.


5. **Swap Break-Cost Realisation on Over-Hedging:** With **R2.50 billion** in active swaps extending to April/August 2027 and October 2028, any debt reduction below R2.50 billion before April 2027 forces APF into an **over-hedged position (>100% of debt)**, exposing the income statement to unhedged derivative mark-to-market losses or cash termination penalties.



### B. Quantified Institutional Sensitivity Rules of Thumb

* **Capitalisation Rate Sensitivity:** Every **+25 bps expansion** in the weighted average portfolio capitalisation rate reduces SOTP Economic NAV by **R132.5 million** (**R0.065 / 6.5 ZAR cents per share**) and lowers our 12-month target price by **R0.05 per share (-10.4%)**.
* **Cost of Debt / Refinancing Margin Sensitivity:** Every **+50 bps increase** in the blended cost of debt (on FY27E average debt of R2.56 billion, including refinancing margin step-ups or unhedged JIBAR shifts) increases annual finance costs by **R12.8 million**, reducing FY27E recurring DIPS by **0.63 ZAR cents per share (-31.8%)** and compressing Group ICR by **-0.06x**.
* **Vacancy Rate Sensitivity:** Every **+100 bps increase** in consolidated portfolio vacancy (~1,989 m² at the average WARP of R201/m²) reduces annualised Cash NPI and FFO by **R4.80 million** (**0.23 ZAR cents per share**, or **-11.9%** of FY27E DIPS).
* **KPMG Crescent Rental Reset Sensitivity:** Every **R25/m² downward reset** in KPMG Crescent’s gross rental across its 20,096 m² GLA reduces annualised Cash NPI and recurring FFO by **R6.03 million** (**0.29 ZAR cents per share**, or **-15.0%** of FY27E DIPS).

1. **Multi-Year Debt Refinancing Package (2–3 Year Tenor) Before 31 March 2027:** Securing a binding term sheet with the 7 core funders to roll the R2.57 billion debt cliff into a 2029/2030 maturity profile without punitive margin step-ups, alongside a formal **1.1x–1.2x ICR covenant reset**.
