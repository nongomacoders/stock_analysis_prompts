## 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

### True Economic Performance vs. Headline Optics

Accelerate Property Fund Limited’s (“APF” or “the Group”) FY2026 reporting presents a narrative of operational turnaround—highlighted by a return to positive SA REIT Funds From Operations (FFO) of **6.25 cents per share** (FY2025: loss of 3.97 cents per share) and a narrowing of the IFRS net loss after tax to **R45.888 million** (FY2025: loss of R1,269.635 million).

Forensic decomposition of the underlying drivers reveals that this headline recovery is heavily reliant on non-recurring cash inflows and non-cash accounting adjustments rather than organic portfolio cash generation:

* **Cash Net Property Income (NPI) Contraction:** Cash-basis NPI (excluding straight-line rental accruals) contracted by **14.1% YoY**, falling from **R494.737 million** in FY2025 to **R424.773 million** in FY2026. While asset disposals drove R34.3 million of the top-line rental decline, organic like-for-like rental growth was a muted **1.4%**, constrained by negative lease renewal reversions.


* **Reliance on Non-Recurring Insurance Proceeds:** The positive SA REIT FFO of ~**R127.8 million** (6.25 cps on 2,045.495 million net shares) was materially propped up by the August 2025 settlement of a legacy Covid-19 business interruption insurance claim, which injected **R82.5 million** (excluding VAT) into `Other income`. Directors’ commentary explicitly acknowledges that positive FFO was achieved in part through the receipt of this one-off settlement. Stripping out the R82.5 million non-recurring insurance windfall reduces underlying recurring FFO by ~64.5% to ~**R45.3 million** (~**2.22 cents per share**).


* **Non-Cash Balance Sheet Derecognition:** The IFRS net loss of R45.888 million was flattered by the non-cash derecognition of the **R300.0 million** Azrapart "Rebuilt Claim" related-party payable, following management's reassessment that no present enforceable obligation existed after the 2022 and 2024 settlement agreements lapsed. Without this accounting credit, the IFRS pre-tax loss would have been ~**R345.9 million**, weighed down by **R428.9 million** in negative investment property fair value adjustments.



### Balance Sheet Health & Covenant Fragility

Despite retiring **R777.3 million** of debt via FY2026 property disposals (reducing total borrowings from R3.7 billion to **R3.0 billion**) and raising **R100.0 million** (R95.6 million net) via a dilutive rights issue at 40 cents per share, APF’s balance sheet remains acutely distressed:

* **Technical Working Capital Insolvency:** Because 100% of the Group’s R3.0 billion debt facilities mature on **31 March 2027**, the entire debt stack was reclassified from non-current to current liabilities. Consequently, Group current liabilities of **R3.1 billion** exceed current assets (even when including R576.8 million of non-current assets held-for-sale) of **R809.5 million** by **R2.3 billion** (and by **R3.0 billion** at the Company level).


* **Immediate FY2027 ICR Covenant Step-Up Trap:** At 31 March 2026, APF reported an Interest Cover Ratio (ICR) of **1.2x** (unchanged from FY2025). The Group avoided a year-end default solely because lenders granted a temporary covenant reduction to **1.1x** through 31 March 2026. However, from 1 April 2026, the ICR covenant steps up (disclosed conflictingly as **1.6x** in the CFO Review and **1.4x** in the Audit & Risk Committee Report). Under either threshold, APF’s actual 1.2x run-rate places the Group in immediate technical breach in FY2027 unless lenders formally extend the 1.1x waiver.


* **LTV Above Target:** Year-end Covenant LTV improved to **43.3%** (versus a 50.0% lender ceiling) and SA REIT LTV reached **43.7%**, but both remained above the Board's going-concern and distribution-reinstatement threshold of **<40.0%**.

* During FY2026, **R1.0 billion** of that R2.0bn swap was extended by 18 months to **October 2028** at an amended rate of **7.05%** (leaving **R1.0 billion** maturing in **April 2027** at **7.38%**).


* A separate **R500 million swap** (entered August 2024 for two years) was extended by one year to **August 2027** at **6.96%**.


* **Total Active Swap Notional:** $\text{R1.0bn} + \text{R1.0bn} + \text{R0.5bn} = \mathbf{\text{R2.50 billion}}$ (which equals 86.5% of **R2.89 billion** core interest-bearing debt).








* **Forensic Determination:** There are two structural mismatches in the treasury book:
1. **Maturity Mismatch:** All three swap tranches (maturing April 2027, August 2027, and October 2028) extend **beyond** the contractual maturity of the underlying debt facilities (31 March 2027).


2. **Deleveraging Over-Hedge Trap:** Post-year-end transfers (R278.2m) and BMW Fourways (R174.0m) will reduce gross debt from ~R2.9–R3.0 billion to **~R2.45–R2.55 billion**, pushing the hedge ratio to **~98%–102%** immediately. Furthermore, the Board has committed to reducing total debt to **R1.3 billion** (`[D009, p. 167]`). Reducing debt below R2.5 billion before April/August 2027 (or below R1.0 billion before October 2028) will leave APF over-hedged, requiring costly swap terminations or ineffective hedge mark-to-market volatility through profit and loss.

### Junction 7: Dividend Payout Sustainability & Section 46 Solvency/Liquidity Blockade

* **Evidence Trail:** `[D002, p. 34–35]`, `[D003, p. 38]`, `[D004, p. 41, 49]`, `[D009, p. 166–168]`, `[D010, p. 178, 180]`.

* Despite reporting positive SA REIT FFO of 6.25 cps, the Board declared **Nil dividend (0% payout ratio)** for FY2026 (FY2025: Nil).


* The Directors’ Report (`[D010, p. 178]`) confirms that the decision was taken after performing the statutory **Solvency and Liquidity test under Section 46 of the Companies Act**, factoring in working capital cash flow forecasts and defensive capex requirements.


* While the Group satisfies the balance-sheet *solvency* test (Group total assets of **R6.8 billion** exceed total liabilities of **R3.1 billion** by **R3.701 billion**, matching PwC's 1% materiality benchmark of R37.01m; SA REIT NAV is **R1.81 per share** after **20 cents** of dilution from the 40c rights issue), it faces a severe *liquidity* deficit:


* Group current liabilities (**R3.1 billion**) exceed current assets (**R809.5 million**, including R576.8m of HFS properties) by **R2.3 billion** (and by **R3.0 billion** at the Company level).


* Excluding HFS properties, true liquid current assets (cash and trade receivables) are just **R232.7 million** ($\text{R809.5m} - \text{R576.8m}$).


* Undrawn debt facilities declined from R110.7 million to **R75.4 million**.


* Capital expenditure consumed **R110.4 million** in FY2026 (flat vs R110.4m in FY2025), and lender milestones require deploying a further **R43.5 million** of insurance proceeds into Fourways Mall capex.






* **Forensic Determination:** APF has **zero free cash flow capacity** to pay dividends. After stripping out the R82.5 million non-recurring insurance settlement, recurring FFO (~R45.3 million) covered less than 42% of the Group’s R110.4 million annual capex bill. Distributions cannot be sustainably reinstated until all five Board conditions are met: (1) refinancing the 31 March 2027 debt cliff, (2) reducing LTV below 40% (achieved pro-forma at 39.6% once BMW Fourways transfers), (3) curing the FY2027 ICR covenant step-up (1.2x actual vs 1.4x/1.6x threshold), (4) funding Fourways Mall capex, and (5) generating recurring cash earnings after capex.
