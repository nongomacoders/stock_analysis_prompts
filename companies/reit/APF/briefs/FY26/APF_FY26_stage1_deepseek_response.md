# FORENSIC AUDIT REPORT — APF GROUP (ACCELERATE PROPERTY FUND)
## FY2026 Independent Managing Director & Senior Forensic REIT Auditor Determination

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE FORENSIC AUDIT SUMMARY

**True Economic Performance.** APF Group's FY2026 headline narrative — "returned to positive SA REIT FFO of 6.25 cps, reduced vacancy to 10.9%, LTV to 43.3%, stabilised the business" — is materially true at the accounting layer and materially misleading at the economic layer. The IFRS loss of R45.9m masks a far deeper economic problem: **the reported SA REIT FFO of R127.8m (6.25 cps) is almost entirely constructed from one-off non-recurring items**, and **distributable income per share (DIPS) is only 0.09 cents** once the Group's own R126m company-specific capex requirements are deducted. The Group declared no dividend for FY2026 (Nil; FY2025 Nil), and on my reconciliation that decision is not merely prudent — it is arithmetically compelled. Distributable income is effectively zero.

**The Earnings Quality Problem.** The FFO bridge swings from (R71.3m) to +R127.8m — a R199m favourable swing — and this is driven almost entirely by (i) non-recurrence of R231.4m of FY2025 non-cash deductions (Headlease accrual, related-party interest accrual, Rebuilt Claim interest reversal), (ii) non-recurrence of the R1,046.5m FY2025 ECL impairment, and (iii) the **R300,000k Azrapart related-party payable derecognition**. On top of this, **R166.7m of one-off other income (R84.2m SARS VAT refund on the written-off related-party receivable + R82.5m Covid business-interruption insurance) sits inside IFRS profit and is NOT separately stripped from FFO**. Strip these one-offs and FFO is materially negative. The same R166.7m one-off other income is the *sole reason* the reported SA REIT ICR is 1.3x rather than ~0.90x — i.e., **without one-off income the Group does not cover its interest on a recurring basis**.

**Balance Sheet Health.** The balance sheet is structurally weaker than the "LTV 43.3%" headline suggests. The entire R3.0bn debt stack is classified current (all maturing 31 March 2027), producing a Group working-capital deficit of R2.3bn (current liabilities R3.14bn vs current assets R809.5m including held-for-sale) and a Company deficit of R3.0bn. The "improvement" in LTV from 47.6% to 43.3% is heavily disposal-driven denominator shrinkage (total assets fell R1.02bn) rather than organic deleveraging. The R576.8m held-for-sale book is pledged as security, and post-year-end disposals of R294.1m (net) plus a signed R174.0m BMW Fourways sale get LTV to 39.6% — below the 40% target — but only on execution, which the Board itself explicitly qualifies as uncertain.

**Post-Balance-Sheet Liquidity Runway.** Facilities and corporate paper mature 31 March 2027 with "discussions underway to extend." No extension is concluded. This is the central solvency risk. Mitigants are real (disposal pipeline, ongoing funder relationships, completed rights offer, RMB breach condonation precedent) but conditional. The refinancing cliff is a ~12-month binary event that dominates the entire investment case.

**Key Findings (ranked by forecast consequence):**
1. **DIPS = 0.09 cps — effectively zero.** FFO of 6.25 cps is fully consumed by R100m Fourways Mall capex + R26m disposal-asset capex requirements. No dividend is coverable from distributable income.
2. **Reported FFO and ICR depend on R166.7m of one-off non-operating income** (VAT refund + insurance) that is not stripped from FFO or from covenant EBITDA. Ex-one-offs, FFO is negative and ICR ~0.90x.
3. **The R300,000k Azrapart payable derecognition is a prior-year misclassification correction, not a new economic event** — confirmed by the IFRS 9 restatement footnote. It is a non-cash credit that must be (and, in FFO, is) excluded.
4. **Entire R3.0bn debt matures 31 Mar 2027; no extension concluded.** Working-capital deficit R2.3bn Group / R3.0bn Company.
5. **Commercial segment is structurally impaired**: vacancy 45.2% (up from 31.6%), yield 14.5% (up from 8.1%), NPI −14.3%, value −31.5%. The "portfolio vacancy improvement" is substantially disposal-driven, not letting-driven.
6. **Fourways Mall concentration is extreme** (38.3% of GLA, 40.8% of revenue, 43.9% of GLA-weighted lease expiry, 70.2% of investment property value) and its NPI collapsed 39.7% YoY despite a R196.5m valuation increase — a valuation/income divergence that requires scrutiny.
7. **The SA REIT disclosures (D020) are unaudited** and the FY2026 pack has *less* assurance than FY2025 (no reporting-accountant report). All REIT ratios must be independently recomputed.
8. **A genuine data-integrity discrepancy exists**: D020 uses 2,045,495k shares net of treasury while Note 29 shows 2,045,868k gross less 44,829k treasury — a ~44.5m share reconciliation gap that affects DIPS/NAVPS denominators.

**Overall Determination.** APF is a **solvency-constrained, disposal-funded work-out story**, not a normalised REIT. FY2026 delivered real asset-management progress (Fourways Mall vacancy, Cedar Square letting) and genuine debt reduction, but the distributable-earnings base is effectively nil, the interest cover is one-off-dependent, and the 2027 refinancing is unresolved. The correct forensic posture is: *accounting stabilisation is real; economic normalisation is not yet achieved.*

## 2. DETAILED FORENSIC REASONING & DETERMINATIONS

### 2.1 IFRS Profit → SA REIT Distributable Income / DIPS Reconciliation

**The bridge (D020, p.300–301):**

| Line | FY2026 R'000 | FY2025 R'000 |
|---|---|---|
| IFRS profit/(loss) (A) | (45,888) | (1,269,635) |
| FV adj — investment property | 428,888 | 274,260 |
| FV adj — debt/equity instruments | (5,476) | 44,685 |
| Depreciation (PPE + ROU) | 613 | 399 |
| Asset impairments | – | 1,046,461 |
| Derecognition of related-party payable | (300,000) | – |
| Straight-lining adjustment | 49,702 | 63,880 |
| **Accounting adjustments (B)** | **173,727** | **1,429,685** |
| Investing adjustments (C) | – | – |
| FX/hedging (D) | – | – |
| Other deductible items not distributed (E) | – | (231,376) |
| **SA REIT FFO (A+B+C+D+E) = F** | **127,839** | **(71,326)** |
| Shares (net of treasury) = G ('000) | 2,045,495 | 1,795,495 |
| **SA REIT FFO per share (cents)** | **6.25** | **(3.97)** |
| Fourways Mall capex requirement (R100m) | (4.89) | – |
| Additional disposal-asset capex (R26m) | (1.27) | – |
| **DIPS (cents)** | **0.09** | **(3.97)** |

**Arithmetic verification:** −45,888 + 173,727 = 127,839 ✓. 127,839 ÷ 2,045,495 × 100 = 6.25 cps ✓. 6.25 − 4.89 − 1.27 = 0.09 cps ✓. R100,000k ÷ 2,045,495k = 4.89c ✓. R26,000k ÷ 2,045,495k = 1.27c ✓.

**Forensic determinations:**
- **The R199m FFO swing is non-recurring-driven, not operational.** The single largest line is the disappearance of "Other deductible items not distributed" of R231.4m (FY2025). These were three non-cash FY2025 deductions: Headlease accrual (R61,801k), related-party interest accrual (R98,512k), and Rebuilt Claim interest reversal (R71,063k). None recurred. **This alone explains ~116% of the FFO swing.** Add the R300m derecognition exclusion and the non-recurrence of the R1,046.5m impairment, and essentially the entire FFO improvement is accounting non-recurrence plus a one-off credit — not sustainable earnings.
- **R166.7m one-off income is not stripped.** The R84,247k SARS VAT refund (Note 19, footnote `#`, D018.7 p.253) and the R82,500k insurance claim sit in IFRS profit and are *not* separately adjusted in the FFO bridge. Because "Other deductible items not distributed" is nil in FY2026, these one-offs remain inside FFO. **On a strict recurring basis, FFO ≈ R127.8m − R166.7m ≈ negative ~R39m.** This is the single most important earnings-quality finding.
- **DIPS is the truth metric.** The Group's own BPR disclosure deducts R126m of company-specific capex to arrive at 0.09 cps. This is the economically correct distributable base. **A REIT with DIPS of 0.09 cents is, for distribution purposes, a zero-dividend REIT.** The Nil FY2026 declaration is consistent — and any future dividend above ~0.1 cps would be capital-funded.
- **No disposal gains/losses adjustment despite R788.5m gross disposals.** The bridge shows nil "Gains or losses on disposal" in both years. Either disposals were at carrying value (unlikely given FV losses and the R8,558k "fair value included in disposal" reclassification) or disposal outcomes are embedded in the FV line / IFRS profit and not separately stripped. This is a bridge-integrity flag.

**Determination:** *IFRS loss of R45.9m → FFO of R127.8m is arithmetically correct but economically fragile. Recurring distributable income is approximately nil (DIPS 0.09c). The "positive FFO" narrative overstates sustainable earnings power.*

### 2.2 Contractual Rental Escalations vs Renewal Reversions

**Evidence:**
- Portfolio weighted average escalation **7.0%** (FY2025: 6.7%); Commercial 7.8%; Retail 6.6% (D019, p.297).
- WARP rose to **R196.9/m²** from R184.7/m² (D010, p.177) — driven by "positive reversions" and contractual escalations (D004/D010).
- Rental income (excl. SL) fell R91.9m to R517.2m — **but FY2025 included R61.8m Headlease income** (Note 17, footnote `*`, D018.6 p.251). Normalised FY2025 ≈ R547.4m; like-for-like decline ≈ −5.5%, not −15%.
- Positive reversions +R15.8m; negative reversions −R19.8m; disposals −R34.3m (D010, p.176). **Net reversion negative ≈ −R4.0m**, i.e., re-letting at marginally below expiring rents despite 7.0% contractual escalations.
- Held-for-sale valuation rental growth assumption **6.0%** (D018.5, p.244) vs portfolio escalation 7.0% — a 100bp conservatism gap.

**Determination:** *Contractual escalations are healthy at 7.0% and are the reliable NPI growth engine, but net renewal reversions are marginally negative (−R4m), meaning mark-to-market is not yet accretive. FY2025 rental comparatives are distorted by R61.8m Headlease income and must be normalised (≈R547m) before assessing growth. The near-term reversion cliff is revenue-concentrated (Mar-30 expiry = 44.5% of revenue vs 33.9% of GLA), which is a negative forward signal.*

### 2.3 GLA Vacancy Dynamics Across Sub-Sectors

**Evidence (D019, p.295; D020, p.302):**
| Sector | FY2026 | FY2025 | Absolute vacant m² (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total | 10.9% | 19.4% | 25,631 / 235,922 |
| Retail | 11.0% | 12.0% | ~20,624 / 187,495 |
| Commercial | **45.2%** | **31.6%** | ~21,889 / 48,427 |
| Industrial | nil | nil | – |

**Forensic determinations:**
- **The headline vacancy improvement (19.4%→10.9%) is substantially disposal-driven.** Vacant GLA fell 57,266m²→25,631m² (−55.2%) while total GLA fell only 20.0% — vacant space was disproportionately in disposed assets. D004 confirms "Disposals reduced vacancies by 28,998m² … Additional GLA created at Fourways Mall … Further vacancies of 2,638m² filled." Organic letting was a minor contributor.
- **Commercial is the impaired engine.** Vacancy *deteriorated* 31.6%→45.2% (+13.6pp) — the opposite of the group narrative. Commercial is ~20.5% of GLA but carries ~R770.6m of value and R139.2m NPI (37.1% of NPI). At 45.2% vacancy, roughly half the commercial portfolio is void.
- **Fourways Mall is the swing asset.** Vacancy 13.7%→9.7% (genuine operational improvement) but NPI fell 39.7% — the vacancy headline overstates income recovery (see §2.6/§2.7).
- **Absolute-m² lens reframes the risk**: Retail vacancy (~20.6k m²) and Commercial vacancy (~21.9k m²) are roughly equal in absolute void space — the 45.2% Commercial figure is not merely a small-denominator artefact; it is a real income hole.

**Determination:** *Portfolio vacancy improvement is real but is mostly disposal-mix, not letting. The commercial sub-portfolio is in severe structural impairment (45.2% void) and is the dominant negative NPI driver. Fourways Mall vacancy improvement is genuine but has not yet converted into NPI.*

### 2.4 Nominal vs Look-Through LTV and ICR Covenant Headroom

**LTV reconciliation (D020, p.299; D018.10 p.281; D009 p.166):**
- **SA REIT LTV 43.7%**: Net debt R2,885,987k (gross R3,002,017k − cash R110,456k − derivative R5,574k) ÷ property-related assets R6,601,853k (total assets less cash, derivatives, receivables). ✔ arithmetic 43.72%.
- **Covenant LTV 43.3%**: Gross debt R2,973,155k (excl. R28,862k unamortised fees) − derivative R5,574k − cash R110,456k = net debt R2,857,125k ÷ carrying property assets R6,600,000k = 43.29%. ✔
- **Nominal check (my own)**: Net debt R2,891,561k ÷ investment property R6,023,200k = **48.0%** — materially above either stated LTV, confirming the stated LTVs rely on including held-for-sale (R576.8m) in the denominator and netting derivatives/cash.
- **Methodology flag**: SA REIT net debt deducts the *net* derivative (R5,574k) while the denominator deducts the *gross* derivative asset (R8,764k) — an inconsistent R3,190k basis, immaterial (~5bp) but a quality flag.

**Covenant headroom (D018.10, p.281–282):**
| Covenant | Requirement | FY2026 Actual | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portfolio LTV | 50.0% | 43.3% | 6.7pp |
| Security SPV 1 LTV | 45.0% | 44.7% | **0.3pp** |
| RMB security SPV LTV | 50.0% | 26.4% | 23.6pp |
| Portfolio/SPV ICR | 1.1x | 1.2x / 1.4x / 1.2x | ~0.1x |

**ICR (D020, p.306):**
- SA REIT ICR = EBITDA R517,642k ÷ net interest R390,416k = **1.33x** (rounded 1.3x).
- **Critical**: EBITDA includes "Other income" R167,061k (of which R166.7m is the two one-offs). Ex-one-offs, EBITDA ≈ R350.9m → ICR ≈ **0.90x — below 1.0x.** The reported 1.3x is entirely one-off-dependent.
- FY2025 ICR was **negative (−2.3x)** because EBITDA was dragged by the R1,046.5m ECL impairment. So the "stable 1.2x/1.3x" narrative conflates a very bad base (FY2025) with a one-off-supported current year.

**Determination:** *Covenant LTV headroom is comfortable at portfolio level (6.7pp) but Security SPV 1 is razor-thin (0.3pp). ICR headroom is ~0.1–0.2x at 1.2–1.3x vs 1.1x covenant — but this evaporates to a sub-1.0x breach on a recurring basis once the R166.7m one-off other income is removed. The FY2025 RMB SPV LTV breach (59.1% vs 50%, condoned and remedied by Portside) is a precedent that funders will condone short breaches, but also proof that covenant pressure is live. No ICR waiver exists; the "1.4x forward covenant" referenced in D009 is NOT in the facility covenant schedule (Note 33) — it is a Board target, not a funder covenant.*

### 2.5 Interest Rate Hedging Profile and Debt Maturity Cliff

**Hedging (D018.9 pp.267–268; D018.4 p.238):**
- **83.3% hedged** (FY2025: 53.6%). Notional R2.5bn vs debt R3,002m; unhedged ~R500m; net 1% sensitivity R5.0m (vs gross R30.0m).
- Swap book: R1.0bn @7.38% maturing 7 Apr 2027; R500m @6.96% maturing 6 Aug 2027; R1.0bn @7.05% maturing 7 Oct 2028. The 2025 R1.5bn cap @7.75% matured Apr 2025.
- All-in WACD **9.9%** (FY2025: 10.9%); fixed 10.2%; derivative benefit (0.3%).
- **Over-hedge risk**: R1.5bn of swaps mature Apr–Aug 2027 — *after* the 31 Mar 2027 debt maturity. If the debt stack is refinanced smaller/earlier, the residual swaps become economically over-hedged (mark-to-market P&L volatility). Only R1.0bn (Oct 2028) extends comfortably beyond a Mar-2027 refinance.

**Maturity cliff (D018.6 p.249; D018.9 p.265; D018.10 p.288):**
- **100% of R3,002,017k debt matures 31 March 2027** — reflected as current liabilities (D012: non-current borrowings nil). Undiscounted contractual outflow R3,298,316k within one year.
- Facility-level breakdown: RMB, Investec, Ashburton, NinetyOne, DMTN notes APF23/24/25U/26U/27U — all `31 Mar 27`.
- Note 35: "Facilities and corporate paper (notes) will mature on 31 March 2027 with discussion currently underway with funders to extend beyond the maturity date."

**Determination:** *Hedging is genuinely improved (83.3%, WACD down 100bp), but the structure now creates a two-sided risk: (i) a hard refinancing cliff at 31 Mar 2027 with no extension concluded, and (ii) post-maturity over-hedge if the book is repaid before the Apr–Aug 2027 swaps roll off. The WACD improvement (10.9%→9.9%) should be treated cautiously — if driven partly by short-dated cheaper facilities, it may reverse at the maturity wall. ZARONIA transition (before 31 Dec 2026) adds a basis-risk overlay on both the R3.0bn debt and R2.5bn swaps.*

The Fourways Mall valuation gain against a 39.7% NPI decline is the single most questionable fair-value call in the portfolio and should be discounted for forecast purposes. Discount/exit cap rates are NOT disclosed, limiting independent reproducibility.*

### 2.7 Dividend Payout Sustainability

**Evidence:**
- FY2026 DPS Nil (FY2025 Nil). DIPS 0.09 cps. FFOPS 6.25 cps.
- Board: "conditions-based" reinstatement requiring LTV<40%, ICR improvement, 2027 refinancing, recurring distributable earnings (D002/D004).
- Post-year-end LTV path: 43.3% → 41.1% (R294.1m transfers) → **39.6% post-BMW Fourways** (D018.10, p.289; D010, p.182).

**Determination:** *Distribution sustainability is nil, not merely suspended. Even with LTV reaching 39.6% post-BMW (which satisfies one reinstatement condition), the recurring DIPS base of ~0.1–6.25 cps depending on one-off treatment is insufficient to support a normalised dividend. Reinstatement requires (a) the R166.7m one-offs to be replaced by recurring earnings, and (b) the R126m company-specific capex to normalise. Neither is yet evident. A 0% forecast payout for FY2027 is the forensic base case unless disposition-driven LTV relief plus recurring NPI recovery materially lifts DIPS.*

### 2.8 Cross-Cutting Forensic Observations

- **Share-count discrepancy (Q313)**: D020 uses 2,045,495k net of treasury; Note 29 shows 2,045,868k gross less 44,829k treasury (= 2,001,039k) — a ~44.5m share gap. If D020 understates treasury, FFOPS/NAVPS are overstated by ~2%. Must be reconciled before relying on per-share metrics.
- **Assurance downgrade**: D020 pro-forma is unaudited and not reviewed by auditors; FY2025 had a reporting-accountant assurance report, FY2026 does not. All REIT ratios must be recomputed.
- **Unaudited IAR KPIs**: The Audit Committee confirmed the IAR (non-AFS) is not independently assured (D008 p.164) — portfolio/vacancy/trading-density metrics are management-sourced.
- **Restatement trail**: R300,000k prior-year payable misclassification (IFRS 9); R12.6m RMB share-loan interest misclassification; R10,272k cash/non-cash restatement — a pattern of classification errors that argues for caution on unaudited disclosures.

**FINAL DETERMINATION.** APF Group's FY2026 is a **credible accounting and asset-management stabilisation masking a still-fragile economic core**. The group genuinely reduced debt (R747m), improved hedging (53.6%→83.3%), cut WACD (10.9%→9.9%), reduced Fourways Mall and Cedar Square vacancy, and exited FY2025's R1.0bn impairment drag. But the *distributable* reality is ~nil (DIPS 0.09c), the interest cover is one-off-dependent (~0.90x recurring), LTV relief is partly denominator shrinkage, the commercial portfolio is severely impaired (45.2% void), Fourways Mall income has not tracked its vacancy/valuation improvement, and the entire debt stack refinances within 12 months with no extension concluded. The "positive FFO" and "improved ICR" surface-readings are the two most misread claims in the pack. The forensic posture for any decision is: **wait for (i) refinancing execution beyond 31 Mar 2027, (ii) evidence of recurring (non-one-off) FFO/DIPS, and (iii) Commercial sub-sector stabilisation — before treating APF as a normalised income REIT.**
