**Core Institutional Thesis: The Illusion of Stability**

Calgro M3's FY2026 headline results appear resilient: revenue up 2.8% to R893.2m and PAT down only 3.5% to R160.2m. This stability is a mirage. The earnings composition is deeply compromised, and the balance sheet carries significant hidden risks that are not reflected in the headline numbers. Our forensic analysis reveals a company whose underlying operating profit is materially weaker than reported, whose cash generation has turned negative, and whose liquidity is entirely dependent on continuous debt refinancing.

The earnings quality is poor. The R160.2m PAT is flattered by R11.5m of non-cash fair-value gains (primarily on investment property) and a R7.7m non-recurring ECL credit (driven by a mechanical cut in government ECL rates and a JV deconsolidation). Stripping these out, underlying operating profit is significantly lower. Furthermore, the R24.0m JV profit includes R11.6m from Bankenveld which was eliminated upon reclassification to a joint operation; continuing JV profit is only R12.4m. The group's gross margin of 27.20%, while above its 20-25% target, is a blend of a deteriorating Residential business (24.24%, down 341 bps) and a strong Memorial Parks business (54.90%). Management explicitly attributes the above-target margin to non-recurring legacy infrastructure costs, and its own goodwill impairment model assumes a normalization to 23.23%.

The balance sheet is levered and illiquid in substance. Borrowings rose 24.8% to R1,380.3m, all unsecured. Net debt of R1,196.6m is \~2.18x market cap. The critical finding is the R2,107.2m "construction contracts" balance. Only R1,270.3m is true contract assets; R852.1m is "development cost for future contract assets"—a form of inventory with no related customer contract, no revenue, and no ECL cushion. This is a hidden impairment exposure. Even more concerning, contract-asset "specific exposure" (credit-impaired) jumped from R393.9m to R938.1m (+R544m), a massive reclassification not reflected in the R4.2m ECL charge.

Most importantly, the liquidity runway is debt-funded, not operationally self-sustaining. Cash generated from operations was negative R14.8m, a R122.7m swing from the prior year's positive R107.9m. The FCFE proxy is negative R221.5m, funded entirely by R250.5m of net new debt. The Group's reported DSCR of 1.54x is flattered by including R566.4m of new debt in "available cash flow"; excluding it, the DSCR is deeply sub-1.0x (\~0.05x). With \~R173m of DMTN notes maturing in FY2027 and the Group stating repayment depends on "refinancing," the liquidity risk is acute.

The BDC accounting treatment is internally contradictory (Note 39 says joint operation, Note 48 says subsidiary), and the dividend/share-count arithmetic does not reconcile, flagging data-integrity issues. These must be resolved before any forecast can be relied upon.

**Structural Headwinds:**
- **Refinancing Cliff:** \~R173m DMTN maturity in FY2027 with negative CFO.
- **Hidden Impairment Risk:** R852m future cost asset with no ECL and R544m reclassified contract-asset exposure.
- **Earnings Quality:** Dependence on non-cash fair value gains and non-recurring ECL credits.
- **JIBAR Transition:** Unquantified repricing risk on R1,093m of JIBAR-linked debt as it transitions to ZARONIA.
- **BDC Funding Gap:** BDC requires \~R150m to finalize phase 1 infrastructure with no final cost recovery agreement.

**Structural Tailwinds:**
- **Memorial Parks:** A high-margin (54.9%), cash-generative segment with a R2.16bn pipeline.
- **Core Pipeline:** A R29.2bn residential pipeline with >30,000 opportunities provides long-term visibility.
- **Non-Core Asset Sales:** Potential to unlock cash and reduce debt, though progress has been slow.
- **BDC Potential:** The 20,000-unit project is strategically located and could be transformative if funded and executed successfully.
- **Affordable Housing Demand:** A resilient market segment supported by government policy and urbanisation.

### 2. Detailed Development & Operational Forecast (FY27E & FY28E)

**Residential Property Development**
We forecast residential revenue to grow modestly as the group continues to trade out of non-core projects while ramping up BDC infrastructure spend. The gross margin is expected to continue its downward normalization as legacy infrastructure costs are depleted. We model a 200 bps contraction in FY27E to 22.2%, aligning with management's own goodwill model assumption of 23.23%. Unit deliveries will be supported by the completion of projects like Scottsdene and La Vie Nouvelle, but the shift towards lower-margin subsidized units will continue to pressure profitability. We assume 968 serviced stands from FY26 are monetized in FY27E.

**Memorial Parks**
The Memorial Parks segment is the group's crown jewel. Revenue grew 25% to R86.2m, and we forecast continued double-digit growth as the Rustenburg park matures and the layby book (R59.7m) converts to revenue. The gross margin of 54.9% is sustainable, though it is structurally depressed by a non-cash maintenance provision charge. Cash collections (R106.9m in FY26) are a key metric; we expect this to grow to R118m in FY27E, covering Group overheads but still falling short of total interest expense. This segment provides a crucial, stable annuity income stream.

### 6. Actionable Portfolio Monitoring Triggers & Catalysts ('What to Watch For That Will Move the Stock')

**Operational & Balance Sheet Red Lines**

1.  **Liquidity & Refinancing:** Failure to refinance the \~R173m DMTN notes maturing in FY2027 on acceptable terms. This is the single largest near-term risk.
2.  **Contract Asset Write-Downs:** Any impairment or write-down on the R852m "future contract asset costs" or the R938m "specific exposure" contract assets would be a material negative catalyst.
3.  **BDC Funding Gap:** The need for the parent to fund BDC's R150m Phase 1 completion without a cost recovery agreement would further strain liquidity.
4.  **CFO Deterioration:** A continued negative trend in cash generated from operations would confirm the unsustainable nature of the current operating model.
5.  **BDC Classification:** A ruling or restatement to clarify the contradictory subsidiary vs. joint operation treatment would be a governance flag.

**Quantified Sensitivity Impacts**

- **Gross Margin:** Every 100 bps shift in group gross margin impacts HEPS by approximately **7 cents** and our fair value by **R0.45/share**.
- **Residential Sales:** A 10% drop in residential unit sales volume impacts HEPS by **12 cents** and our fair value by **R0.80/share**.
- **Borrowing Costs:** A 100 bps increase in the average cost of debt (e.g., from JIBAR transition) impacts HEPS by **9 cents** and our fair value by **R0.60/share**.
- **Memorial Park Collections:** A 10% increase in Memorial Park cash collections has a minimal direct HEPS impact in the short term (due to revenue recognition policy) but improves liquidity and supports our SOTP valuation by **R0.30/share**.

**Re-Rating Catalysts (Required to Upgrade)**

- **Major Infrastructure Project Award:** A government or private sector contract award for infrastructure (e.g., BDC cost recovery) that materially de-risks the R852m future cost asset.
- **Acceleration of Retail Land Parcel Sales:** A significant land sale (e.g., R100m+) at or above book value, providing a non-debt source of liquidity.
- **Debt De-gearing Milestone:** A clear plan, backed by signed agreements, to reduce net debt to below R1bn within 12-18 months.
- **BDC Collaboration:** A binding public-private partnership (PPP) agreement for BDC that includes funding for infrastructure.
