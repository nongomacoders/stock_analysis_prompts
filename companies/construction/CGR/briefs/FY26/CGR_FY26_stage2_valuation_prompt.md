The construction-contract balance of R2.107bn is **not** a contract asset: R852.1m is "development cost for future contract assets" (inventory-like, no revenue, **no ECL**), and only R1.270bn is a true contract asset carrying a 1.20% ECL provision. Against this: **R224.3m of undiscounted borrowings fall due within 12 months** (R83m carrying), **~R173m of DMTN notes mature in FY2027** (CGR 52/53/55/56), and the NHFC loan begins nine instalments from May 2026. The parent company's going concern is **explicitly dependent on a "commitment" from its subsidiary** — an undefined, unquantified support letter. The Group is **refinancing-dependent**, not self-funding. The 15 May 2026 dividend (8.63703 cps, ~R8.2m) is being paid while FCFE is deeply negative (≈ −R221.5m) — funded by debt.

## 2. DETAILED FORENSIC REASONING & DETERMINATIONS

### 2.1 Percentage-of-Completion (POC) Revenue Recognition & IFRS 15 Timing

**Determination: Compliant but judgement-concentrated; the mix shift is the real story.**

The Group uses the **input method** (cost incurred ÷ forecast cost at completion per approved feasibility) for over-time contracts, and point-in-time recognition on transfer of control (title deed / grave certificate / full payment) for completed units and burial rights. The auditor (Forvis Mazars) identified Residential contract revenue as the **sole Key Audit Matter**, testing feasibilities, cost contingencies, and prior-year revisions — a clean but concentrated opinion.

The forensic issue is **not** the method but the **mix shift**:
- **Over-time (POC) revenue fell 19.9%** (R602.1m → R482.0m).
- **Point-in-time revenue rose 54.1%** (R266.8m → R411.2m).
- Within Residential: **Infrastructure revenue collapsed 50.7%** (R549.5m → R270.8m) while **subsidised units rose 127%** (R176.6m → R401.4m) and **non-subsidised units rose 103%** (R63.1m → R128.4m).

This is a **shift from lump-sum infrastructure contracting toward government-subsidised unit delivery** — which carries higher municipal/government debtor risk and lower margin (Residential margin fell 341 bps). The "expected aggregate revenue still to be recognised on the contract asset balance is in excess of R2,107,206,825" disclosure is **internally inconsistent**: it is stated on the full R2.107bn balance, but R852.1m of that balance is explicitly "no revenue or profit impact." The true contracted revenue backlog is therefore closer to the R1.270bn contract-asset base, not R2.107bn.

**Verification:** Note 23 confirms variations are accounted for as contract modifications with cumulative catch-up for over-time contracts; no quantum of catch-up is disclosed. The "scope of construction agreements" is flagged as a significant judgement (Note 3.4) — the boundary between over-time and point-in-time is management-determined and drives the revenue timing.

### 2.2 Gross Margin Bridge — Residential vs Memorial Parks vs Infrastructure

**Determination: The 27.20% Group margin is a legacy artefact and will normalise toward 24–25%.**

| Segment | FY2026 GM | FY2025 GM | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residential Property Development | **24.24%** | 27.65% | **−341 bps** |
| Memorial Parks | **54.90%** | 50.09% | +481 bps |
| **Group** | **27.20%** | 29.43% | **−223 bps** |

The Group margin beat vs the 20–25% target is **entirely Memorial Parks** (a small, high-margin segment) offsetting a **deteriorating Residential margin**. Management attributes the beat to "historic land and infrastructure costs" — i.e., legacy low-cost infrastructure already sunk into cost of sales. As that legacy base is consumed and replaced with current-cost infrastructure (and as the BDC bulk/link works — which are cost-recovery-contingent — flow through), **Residential margin should trend toward the 20–25% band**. The goodwill impairment test itself assumes a **23.23% gross margin** (down from 27.45%), corroborating management's own internal expectation of normalisation.

**Forensic flag:** The R62.4m "Residential property development other costs" line (+46.2% YoY) is undecomposed — it is the only plausible home for pre-contract bid costs (expensed per policy) and any cost overruns. Without a nature-of-expense build (materials/subcontractors/labour/plant), the margin bridge cannot be fully validated.

### 2.3 Contract Assets / Unbilled WIP Days vs Contract Liabilities & Customer Advances

**Determination: The headline R2.107bn is NOT a contract asset; true contract assets are R1.270bn with 953 unbilled days — a severe working-capital red flag.**

The construction-contract balance decomposes as:
- **Gross contract assets (unbilled WIP): R1,270,275,234** (2025: R1,092,024,671)
- **Less ECL provision: (R15,192,569)** (2025: R10,988,850)
- **Plus "development cost for future contract assets": R852,124,160** (2025: R587,247,311) — inventory-like, no revenue, **no ECL**
- **= Net SOFP balance: R2,107,206,825**

Contract liabilities ("excess billings over work done") are only **R15,245,364** — trivial relative to the asset. **Unbilled revenue days** on the true contract-asset base = R1,270.3m ÷ R806.96m Residential revenue × 365 = **~575 days** (or ~953 days if computed on the full R2.107bn — which would be wrong). Either way, this is an extraordinarily long unbilled position, driven by:
- Sectional-title units in Fleurhof and Jabulani **not yet transferred** at year-end (revenue recognised, cash not collected).
- "Significant infrastructure and internal services work" performed but not yet billed.
- The **R852.1m future-cost bucket grew +R264.9m** — the single largest driver of the R439.7m net construction-contract increase, and it is **pure cash absorption with no matching revenue**.

**Forensic flag (critical):** The **"specific exposure" bucket within contract assets jumped from R393.9m to R938.1m (+R544.2m)** while the government-institution bucket fell from R633.1m to R149.4m. This is a **R483.7m reclassification of government-linked contract assets into the individually-assessed (credit-impaired) bucket** — yet the contract-asset ECL charge was only R4.2m. Either the reclassification is a presentational relabel (benign) or it signals a material deterioration in assessed recoverability that is **not** provisioned (serious). This must be resolved against Note 9 and Note 3.10.2 before any revenue-quality conclusion.

### 2.4 Retentions Receivable and Trade Debtors Aging / IFRS 9 ECL

**Determination: The R7.7m ECL credit is non-recurring and flatters FY2026 earnings; receivables quality is deteriorating beneath the release.**

The net ECL **credit of R7,692,016** decomposes as:
- Trade & other receivables: **credit R6,413,164** (driven by settlement of the prior-year R40.9m investment-sale receivable, a fall in the government ECL floor rate from 1.38% to 1.19%, and mix change — **not** improved collections on the enlarged book).
- Contract assets: **charge R4,203,719** (in line with the gross balance increase).
- JV loans: **credit R5,482,571** (driven by the Bankenveld deconsolidation removing a R165.0m loan — **structural, not operational**).

Meanwhile, **gross trade receivables grew 91.2%** (R82.3m → R157.3m) against revenue growth of 2.8%. The "<30 days" bucket is an implausible **R112.0m** (2025: R0.7m) — consistent with progress billings moving from contract assets to receivables on invoicing, but also consistent with a definitional reclassification. **No collateral is held** on any receivable.

**Retentions:** The Group asserts retentions are "usually released within three to six months" and are "not variable consideration" — a **contradiction of the conventional 5–10% long-dated retention assumption**. Retention *creditors* (payable side) are R5.1m; retention *receivables* are not separately disclosed and must be extracted from Note 7.

**Determination:** Strip the R7.7m ECL credit from underlying earnings. The forward ECL run-rate should be modelled as a **charge**, not a credit, with the contract-asset ECL (R4.2m) as the baseline and the trade-receivable release treated as one-off.

### 2.5 Joint Ventures vs Proportionately Consolidated Joint Operations (BDC / Bankenveld District City)

**Determination: The Bankenveld JV→joint-operation reclassification is the largest comparability distortion in the accounts and creates a single-point-of-failure funding structure.**

Effective **1 September 2025**, Bankenveld District City (Pty) Ltd ("BDC") was reclassified from equity-accounted joint venture to **proportionately consolidated joint operation** (IFRS 11.20–21), applied **prospectively** (no restatement). Effects:
- **R165,006,544 JV loan eliminated** (non-cash).
- **R206m asset brought onto the consolidated balance sheet** (not in comparatives).
- **R11,599,528 of FY2026 JV profit eliminated** on change in control — meaning the reported JV profit of R23,982,406 **overstates continuing JV contribution** by R11.6m; continuing JV profit is only **R12,382,878**.
- **R19,252,380 direct debit to retained income** (equity, not P&L).
- **FY2027 will carry a full year** of BDC proportionate consolidation vs 4 months in FY2026 — a mechanical revenue/cost step-up.

**Critical structural finding:** Note 48 (Company) treats **Belhar Calgro M3 Development Company** (49% held, 100% board control) as a **subsidiary**, while Note 39 treats **BDC** as a **joint operation**. These are **two distinct legal entities** (per the entity-acronym disambiguation guardrail) and must not be conflated. However, the **parent's going concern is explicitly dependent on a "commitment" from Calgro M3 Developments** (Note 65), and the **DMTN is guaranteed by Calgro M3 Developments** (Note 63). The parent's R1.338bn treasury loan to that subsidiary is the *only* asset backing the parent's ability to repay its R1.330bn borrowings. This is a **single-point-of-failure structure**: if the subsidiary's construction cash flows deteriorate (Group CFO was −R14.8m), the parent has no security and no disclosed ECL provision on the treasury loan.

**JV loan reconciliation gap:** Balance-sheet JV loans fell R91.4m (R493.1m → R401.8m) but the cash-flow statement shows net advances of ~R113.7m — a **~R199.6m unexplained gap**, largely the non-cash Bankenveld elimination (R165.0m) plus interest accrual and the Table View loan reduction. This must be formally bridged.

### 2.6 Borrowing Costs Capitalised into Construction Assets

**Determination: R102.1m of capitalised interest (up 60% YoY) is the single largest earnings-quality issue; it suppresses P&L finance costs and inflates the contract-asset balance.**

| Item | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross finance cost | R145,636,241 | R136,195,746 |
| Less: capitalised to construction contracts | (R102,143,192) | (R63,728,343) |
| Less: capitalised to inventory | – | (R12,164,129) |
| **P&L finance cost** | **R43,493,049** | **R60,303,274** |

The 27.9% fall in P&L finance costs is **entirely a capitalisation effect** — gross finance cost actually *rose* 6.9%. The capitalisation is on **general borrowings** (explicitly stated), at an incremental borrowing rate of **12.72%** (2025: 11.76%). The R102.1m sits inside the construction-contract balance and is excluded from P&L.

**Forecast implication:** If capitalisation normalises (e.g., as BDC bulk/link works complete and developments are handed over), **R100m+ of interest would flow to P&L**, materially depressing earnings. The capitalised interest is also embedded in contract cost-to-complete, which **distorts the POC margin** — the 24.24% Residential margin is *after* capitalised interest, so the true cash margin is lower.

### 2.7 Onerous Contract Provisions & Performance Guarantee/Bonding Facilities

**Determination: No onerous-contract provisions are disclosed; the "no contingent liabilities" representation is contradicted by the DMTN subsidiary guarantee.**

Note 3.8 states **"No contingent assets or liabilities existed at year-end."** This is **contradicted** by Note 63, which discloses that **Calgro M3 Developments guarantees the repayment of the DMTN listed debt programme** — a material contingent liability of the subsidiary that is not reflected in the consolidated contingent-liability note. Either the guarantee is treated as an intra-group matter eliminated on consolidation (defensible, since the subsidiary is consolidated) or the "no contingent liabilities" statement is incomplete. **No performance bonds or bonding facilities are disclosed anywhere** — a notable omission for a construction group, and a constraint on future tendering capacity.

**No IAS 37 onerous-contract provision** is recognised on any loss-making construction project. The only IAS 37 provision is the **Memorial Parks maintenance provision (R66.3m)**, which is a *revenue-linked* obligation (recognised in cost of sales at point of grave sale), not an onerous contract. The R66.3m provision is **materially unfunded**: the ring-fenced maintenance fund is only R20.7m, leaving a **~R45.6m future cash obligation with no dedicated assets**.

### 2.8 DMTN Corporate Bond and Bank Debt Covenant Headroom

**Determination: Covenant headroom exists but is thin and flattered; the FY2027 refinancing cliff is the key solvency risk.**

| Covenant | Threshold | FY2026 | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Debt/Equity | <1.5:1 | **0.74:1** | Comfortable vs covenant; **thin vs 0.75:1 internal target** |
| DSCR | >1.2x | **1.54x** | Narrowing (2025: 1.74x) |
| Liquidity ratio | >1.2:1 | **1.93:1** | Comfortable |

**Critical qualifications:**
- The **DSCR of 1.54x is flattered** because "available cash flow" includes **R566.4m of new financial indebtedness incurred** — i.e., the ratio is boosted by the very debt it is meant to service. Excluding new debt, DSCR would be **negative** (CFO was −R14.8m).
- **Net Debt/Equity of 0.74:1** is flattered by the R102.1m capitalised interest (which reduces P&L finance cost and thus boosts retained earnings) and the R7.7m ECL credit.
- **All borrowings are unsecured** — no asset encumbrance, but also no secured lending headroom. The only credit support is the **DMTN guarantee from Calgro M3 Developments**.
- **FY2027 refinancing cliff:** ~R173m of DMTN notes mature (CGR 52 R50m exp 30 Jun 2026; CGR 53 R40m exp 28 Feb 2026; CGR 55 R50m + CGR 56 R33m exp 2 Oct 2026), plus NHFC instalments begin May 2026. The undiscounted <1yr borrowings are **R224.3m** vs cash of R183.7m.
- **JIBAR→ZARONIA transition** (JIBAR ceases 31 Dec 2026) introduces unquantified repricing risk on ~R1,093m of JIBAR-linked debt; a 25bp spread widening = ~R2.7m p.a.

### 2.9 Cash Tax vs Deferred Tax

**Determination: The tax charge is overwhelmingly deferred; cash tax is negligible and the DTA recognition is judgement-dependent.**

| Item | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Current tax | R2,716,478 | R2,060,427 |
| Deferred tax | R44,654,228 | R44,367,679 |
| **Total tax expense** | **R47,370,706** | **R46,428,106** |
| Tax paid (cash) | R3,890,706 | R2,659,727 |
| Effective tax rate | **23.87%** | 21.84% |

The deferred tax charge is dominated by the **construction-contract DTL of R437.7m** (deferred tax on unbilled POC revenue) — it will unwind as contract assets convert to cash. **Recognised tax-loss DTAs rose from R9.5m to R24.3m** (+R14.8m), a direct P&L credit that flatters the tax charge; estimated tax losses available rose to **R91.97m** (from R34.2m). This DTA recognition is based on management's "approved business plans and budgets" — a soft test that is at risk if forecast profits disappoint.

**Forecast implication:** Cash tax will remain low in the near term (deferred tax dominates), but the **DTA write-down risk** is material if the R91.97m assessed losses are not utilised within the forecast horizon.
