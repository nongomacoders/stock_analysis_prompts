# FORENSIC AUDIT REPORT — CALGRO M3 HOLDINGS (CGR)
## Independent Managing Director & Senior Forensic Construction/Infrastructure Financial Auditor

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE FORENSIC AUDIT SUMMARY

**True economic performance.** Calgro M3 is a leveraged, cash-absorbing residential developer whose FY2026 headline profitability (PAT R160.2m, HEPS 156.76c) is **not cash-backed and is materially flattered by non-cash and non-recurring items**. Group revenue grew only 2.8% to R893.2m while cost of sales grew 6.0%, compressing gross margin 223 bps to 27.20%. The reported profit is propped up by: (i) a **R10.0m non-cash investment-property fair-value gain** (52% of "other income"); (ii) a **R7.7m net ECL credit** (a release, not a charge); (iii) **R102.1m of borrowing costs capitalised** into construction contracts (up 60% YoY), which suppressed P&L finance cost to R43.5m against R145.6m gross; and (iv) a **R44.7m deferred tax charge** that is non-cash. Strip these out and the underlying operating cash generation is deeply negative.

**Balance sheet health.** Total assets R3.69bn, equity R1.57bn, borrowings R1.38bn (all **unsecured**), net debt ~R1.20bn. The balance sheet is dominated by a **R2.11bn "construction contracts" asset**, of which only R1.27bn is a true contract asset (gross of a R15.2m ECL) and **R852.1m is "development cost for future contract assets"** — inventory-like land/development WIP carrying **no ECL and no revenue**. This R852m bucket grew R264.9m in the year and is the single largest driver of the R439.7m construction-contract build and the cash burn. Receivables grew 93% to R159.6m (driven by a Belhar land sale), and the **contract-asset "specific exposure" bucket exploded from R393.9m to R938.1m** while the government-exposure bucket collapsed from R633.1m to R149.4m — a reclassification that signals deteriorating assessed recoverability not reflected in the modest R4.2m contract-asset ECL charge.

**Post-balance-sheet liquidity runway.** This is the critical finding. **Cash generated from operations was −R14.8m** (2025: +R107.9m) and **net operating cash was −R99.1m**. The R29.0m increase in cash to R183.7m was **entirely debt-funded** (R566.4m drawn, R297.5m repaid, net +R268.9m). Free cash flow to equity is approximately **−R221.5m**. The Group's own DSCR of 1.54x is **flattered by including R566.4m of new debt in "available cash flow"** — excluding new borrowings, debt-service capacity is negative. The parent company's going concern is **explicitly dependent on a "commitment" from its main trading subsidiary** (Calgro M3 Developments) to provide liquidity, and the DMTN bond programme is **guaranteed by that subsidiary**. All R1.38bn of borrowings are unsecured. The FY2027 maturity wall includes ~R173m of DMTN notes (CGR 52/53/55/56) plus NHFC instalments commencing May 2026. **The liquidity runway is refinancing-dependent, not cash-generative.**

**Key findings (definitive determinations):**
1. **Revenue quality is deteriorating** — over-time (POC) revenue fell 19.9% while point-in-time revenue rose 54.1%; the mix has shifted toward completed-unit sales and away from construction-contract POC, and the R852m future-cost bucket carries no revenue.
2. **The 27.20% gross margin is not sustainable** — it is a Group figure aided by legacy low-cost infrastructure; Residential margin compressed 341 bps to 24.24%, and management's own goodwill impairment model now assumes 23.23% (down from 27.45%).
3. **The ECL credit is largely structural, not operational** — the R7.7m net credit comprises a R6.4m trade-receivable release (Belhar land sale + prior-year investment-sale receivable settlement + rate reassessment) and a R5.5m JV-loan release (Bankenveld deconsolidation), offset by a R4.2m contract-asset charge. It will not recur at this magnitude.
4. **The BDC reclassification is a comparability break** — Bankenveld moved from JV (equity method) to joint operation (proportionate consolidation) effective 1 Sep 2025, prospectively, with a R19.25m direct debit to retained income. FY2027 will carry a full year of BDC proportionate revenue/costs, creating a step-up unrelated to organic growth. Note 48 simultaneously describes BDC as a *subsidiary* (49% holding, 100% board control) — an internal inconsistency requiring resolution.
5. **The parent is a treasury conduit with negative carry** — it borrows externally (R1.33bn) and on-lends to Calgro M3 Developments (R1.31bn), earning a thin spread; parent standalone ICR is deeply negative (finance cost R131.6m vs PBT R8.2m).
6. **Covenant headroom is asserted, not quantified** — the Group states it is "not in breach … or at risk of breaching" DSCR >1.2x, Net Debt/Equity <1.5:1, and liquidity >1.2, but discloses no actual ratios or headroom. Net debt/retained income computes to ~0.78:1, which would breach the stated <0.75:1 *target* (though the covenant is 1.5:1).

# 2. DETAILED FORENSIC REASONING & DETERMINATIONS

## 2.1 Percentage-of-Completion (POC) Revenue Recognition & IFRS 15 Timing

**Determination: POC is input-method based on approved feasibilities; revenue quality is weakening and the contract-asset base is materially overstated relative to true unbilled WIP.**

- The auditor's sole Key Audit Matter is Residential contract revenue over time, using the **input method** (costs incurred ÷ forecast cost at completion), with feasibilities prepared by management and internal experts (town planners, QS, architects) and contractors on a **fixed-cost basis** [D002 AFS, pp.13–14].
- Note 23 confirms the input method and that **feasibilities are reviewed regularly and approved by Exco**; variations are contract modifications with cumulative catch-up for over-time contracts [D005.15, pp.56–57].
- **Revenue mix shift is the key finding**: over-time revenue fell from R602.1m to R482.0m (−19.9%) while point-in-time revenue rose from R266.8m to R411.2m (+54.1%) [D005.1, p.20]. Infrastructure revenue collapsed from R549.5m to R270.8m (−50.7%), offset by subsidised units rising from R176.6m to R401.4m (+127%) and non-subsidised units from R63.1m to R128.4m (+103%) [D005.15, p.61].
- **The R852.1m "development cost for future contract assets"** is explicitly excluded from the input method and carries **no revenue or profit impact and no ECL** [D005.5, p.38]. This is inventory-like land/development WIP sitting inside the construction-contract caption. It grew R264.9m in the year — the dominant driver of the R439.7m net construction-contract build.
- **Unbilled revenue days** on the true gross contract asset (R1,270.3m) against Residential revenue (R807.0m) = **~575 days** — a very long conversion cycle. On the headline R2,107m it would be ~953 days, which is misleading because R852m is not a contract asset.
- **Determination**: Revenue recognition is policy-compliant, but the quality is deteriorating. The shift to point-in-time and the growth of the no-revenue future-cost bucket mean reported revenue understates the cash absorbed and overstates the conversion of the asset base. The auditor's testing of "reasonability of changes in forecast costs and revenue from the prior year" [D002 AFS, p.14] implies prior feasibilities were revised — a margin-restatement risk that is not quantified.

## 2.2 Gross Margin Bridge — Residential vs Memorial Parks vs Infrastructure

**Determination: The 27.20% Group margin is a legacy-cost artefact, not a sustainable run-rate. Residential compressed 341 bps; Memorial Parks expanded 481 bps but is small.**

| Segment | FY2026 Revenue | FY2026 COS | FY2026 GM% | FY2025 GM% | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Residential Property Development | R806.96m | R611.38m | **24.24%** | 27.65% | **−341 bps** |
| Memorial Parks | R86.21m | R38.88m | **54.90%** | 50.09% | +481 bps |
| **Group** | **R893.17m** | **R650.26m** | **27.20%** | 29.43% | **−223 bps** |

[D005.1, p.21]

- The compression is **entirely Residential**: COS rose 5.6% on revenue +0.9%. Memorial Parks COS rose 13.2% on revenue +25.2%, so its margin expanded.
- Management attributes the above-target Group margin to "past infrastructure costs" and "favourable project and product mix" [D002, p.23; D004, p.41] — i.e., legacy low-cost infrastructure already sunk into COS. This is **non-recurring in nature**; as legacy infrastructure is consumed, margin should trend toward the 20–25% target.
- **Corroboration**: the goodwill impairment model's gross-margin assumption was cut from 27.45% to **23.23%** [D005.10, p.49] — management's own forward view is below the reported 27.20%.
- **Determination**: Forecast gross margin should be set at **23–25%**, not 27.20%. The Memorial Parks 54.90% margin is structurally depressed by the non-cash maintenance provision charge embedded in COS (R4.6m provision + R2.4m unwinding per Note 21/30), so cash gross margin is higher than reported.

## 2.3 Contract Assets / Unbilled WIP Days vs Contract Liabilities & Customer Advances

**Determination: The construction-contract caption is a three-part composite; only R1.27bn is a true contract asset, and the "specific exposure" reclassification is a material quality red flag.**

- **Reconciliation** [D005.5, p.38]:
  - Gross contract assets: **R1,270,275,234** (2025: R1,092,024,671)
  - ECL provision on contract assets: **(R15,192,569)** (2025: R10,988,850)
  - Development cost for future contract assets: **R852,124,160** (2025: R587,247,311)
  - = Statement of financial position balance: **R2,107,206,825** (2025: R1,668,283,132)
- **Contract liabilities** ("excess billings over work done") = **R15,245,364** (2025: R11,808,500), sitting in trade and other payables (Note 19) [D005.13, p.52].
- **Disaggregation of gross contract assets** [D005.5, p.38]: Infrastructure R796.0m; fully/partially subsidised units R366.9m; non-subsidised units R107.4m.
- **The critical anomaly** [D005.2, pp.27–28]: within contract assets, the **"specific exposure" bucket jumped from R393.9m to R938.1m (+R544.2m)** while the **government-institution bucket fell from R633.1m to R149.4m (−R483.7m)**. This is a near-offsetting reclassification of government-linked contract assets into the individually-assessed (credit-impaired) bucket. Yet the contract-asset ECL charge was only **R4.2m** (R10.99m → R15.19m). A R544m reclassification into "specific" with only a R4.2m charge implies either (a) the reclassification is presentational and the assets remain performing, or (b) provisioning is inadequate. **This is the single most important forensic flag in the contract-asset note and must be resolved against Note 9 and Note 23.**
- **Determination**: Unbilled WIP days on the true contract asset are ~575 days. The R852m future-cost bucket carries no ECL — a hidden impairment exposure if developments are abandoned before customer contracts are signed. The specific-exposure reclassification warrants a provisioning adequacy challenge.

## 2.4 Retentions Receivable and Trade Debtors Aging / IFRS 9 ECL

**Determination: The ECL credit is largely structural and non-recurring; receivables growth is driven by a single Belhar land sale; retentions are asserted short-dated (3–6 months) but unverified.**

- **ECL decomposition** [D005.16, p.62]: net credit R7,692,016 = trade & other receivables credit R6,413,164 + contract assets charge R4,203,719 + JV loans credit R5,482,571.
- **Trade receivables** [D005.4, p.36]: gross R157.3m (2025: R82.3m, +91.2%); ECL allowance fell R9.5m → R3.1m. The release is attributed to (i) a **Belhar Project land sale** receivable "anticipated to be recovered in the short term", (ii) settlement of the prior-year investment-sale receivable (MS5 Pennyville, 2025: R40.9m, now R48,750), and (iii) ECL rate reassessment (government floor rate fell 1.38% → 1.19%) [D005.2, pp.26–29].
- **Aging anomaly** [D005.4, p.37]: "<30 days" trade receivables R112.0m (2025: R0.7m) vs "≥30 days" R4.8m (2025: R30.3m). The R112m "<30 days" is implausibly large relative to monthly revenue (~R74m) and suggests progress billings were reclassified from contract assets into trade receivables on invoicing — a timing artefact, not genuine current aging.
- **Retentions**: Note 23 asserts retention debtors are "usually released within three to six months" and are not variable consideration [D005.15, p.56]. Retention *creditors* (payable side) are R5.1m [D005.13, p.52]. Retention *receivables* are not separately disclosed — a gap.
- **Determination**: The R6.4m trade-receivable ECL release is **non-recurring** (Belhar sale + MS5 settlement + rate change) and must be stripped from the FY2027 base. The R5.5m JV-loan release is **structural** (Bankenveld deconsolidation). Forward ECL should be modelled as a modest charge, not a credit.

## 2.5 Joint Ventures vs Proportionately Consolidated Joint Operations (BDC / Bankenveld District City)

**Determination: Bankenveld moved from equity-accounted JV to proportionately consolidated joint operation effective 1 Sep 2025, prospectively; this is a comparability break and the notes are internally inconsistent on BDC's classification.**

- **Note 39** [D005.20, p.74]: 50% interest in BDC; historically a JV (equity method); following revised contractual terms effective **1 September 2025**, the Group obtained rights to assets and obligations for liabilities, so BDC is classified as a **joint operation** under IFRS 11, applied **prospectively** (no restatement). The Group now presents its proportionate share of revenue, COS, admin, finance, tax, rental and operating expenses instead of a single "share of profit of equity-accounted investees".
- **The transition table in Note 39 is blank** — no amounts are disclosed for the assets/liabilities brought on [D005.20, p.74]. The R19,252,380 direct debit to retained income (per SOCE) is not visible in Note 39.
- **Internal inconsistency**: Note 48 (Company) describes BDC as a **subsidiary** — 49% holding but 100% board control and chairman casting vote [D011.2, pp.81–82]. This contradicts the joint-operation treatment. Either the classification changed during the year (JV → joint operation → subsidiary) or the notes are inconsistent.
- **JV profit** fell 44.1% to R24.0m (2025: R42.9m) [D005.7, p.41]. Driver: South Hills profit after tax fell from R67.4m to R21.5m (revenue −63.9%, margin 39.6% → 34.4%); Witpoortjie generated **zero revenue** (2025: R83.7m) with profit of only R117k; Bankenveld's R11.6m share of profit is included then eliminated on change in control. **Continuing JV profit = R12.4m** (Witpoortjie R0.1m + South Hills R12.4m + Table View −R0.1m).
- **Determination**: FY2027 will carry a full year of BDC proportionate revenue/costs — a step-up unrelated to organic growth. The continuing JV profit base is R12.4m, not R24.0m. The BDC classification inconsistency must be resolved before forecasting.

## 2.6 Borrowing Costs Capitalised into Construction Assets

**Determination: R102.1m of borrowing costs were capitalised in FY2026 (up 60.3%), suppressing P&L finance cost to R43.5m against R145.6m gross. This is a material earnings-quality issue.**

- **Note 30** [D005.17, p.63]: gross finance cost R145,636,241 (bank R5.4m + maintenance unwinding R2.4m + lease R2.8m + interest-bearing borrowings R135.0m), less **R102,143,192 capitalised to construction contracts** (2025: R63,728,343) and nil to inventory (2025: R12,164,129), = **R43,493,049 recognised in P&L**.
- The capitalisation is on **general borrowings** (explicitly stated) [D005.17, p.63]. The incremental borrowing rate is **12.72%** (2025: 11.76%) [D005.13, p.54].
- **Determination**: P&L finance cost is only ~30% of gross finance cost. If capitalisation normalises (as construction completes or if the qualifying-asset test tightens), R100m+ of interest would flow to P&L, materially depressing earnings. The capitalised interest is embedded in contract cost-to-complete and therefore affects the POC margin — a circularity that flatters both margin and profit. Forecast must model a rising P&L finance cost as capitalisation tapers.

## 2.7 Onerous Contract Provisions & Performance Guarantee / Bonding Facilities

**Determination: No onerous-contract provisions are disclosed; no performance-bond or bonding-facility line is disclosed anywhere in the notes; the DMTN is guaranteed by the main trading subsidiary.**

- **Note 3.8** [D005.2, p.24] states **"No contingent assets or liabilities existed at year-end."** This is a strong representation that must be tested — no performance bonds, guarantees, litigation or JV joint-and-several contingencies are disclosed.
- **However**, Note 63 (Company) discloses that **Calgro M3 Developments guarantees the repayment of the DMTN listed debt programme** [D011.6, p.89–90]. This is a material guarantee that sits outside the "no contingent liabilities" representation at Group level and must be reconciled to Note 21.
- **No onerous-contract provision** is disclosed in Note 9 or Note 21. Losses on contracts are netted in the contract position, not separately provisioned [D005.5, p.37].
- **Determination**: The absence of any bonding/guarantee disclosure in a construction group's notes is a verification gap. The DMTN subsidiary guarantee is a real contingent obligation. Onerous-contract provisioning adequacy cannot be confirmed.

## 2.8 DMTN Corporate Bond and Bank Debt Covenant Headroom

**Determination: Covenants are asserted compliant but not quantified; the debt is entirely unsecured; the FY2027 maturity wall is a refinancing cliff.**

- **Covenants** [D005.13, p.53]: DSCR >1.2x (Group level); Net Debt/Equity <1.5:1; minimum liquidity ratio 1.2. "At the end of the financial year, the Group was not in breach … or at risk of breaching."
- **Actual ratios** [D005.3, pp.33–34]: Net Debt/Equity **0.74** (2025: 0.65); DSCR **1.54** (2025: 1.74); liquidity ratio **1.93** (2025: 1.97). All within covenant.
- **But**: (i) the DSCR of 1.54 **includes R566.4m of new financial indebtedness in "available cash flow"** [D005.3, p.33] — excluding new debt, debt-service capacity is negative; (ii) Net Debt/Equity of 0.74 is **above the stated <0.75:1 target** (though below the 1.5:1 covenant); (iii) **all borrowings are unsecured** [D005.13, p.53]; (iv) the **entire R1,380.3m is classified as current** in the liquidity table [D005.3, p.34] while the maturity table shows only R224.3m (incl. interest) due <1yr [D005.3, p.32] and Note 20 shows R133.0m <12m [D005.13, p.54] — three inconsistent current/non-current figures.
- **Maturity wall** [D005.13, p.53]: DMTN CGR 52 (R50m, exp 30 Jun 2026), CGR 53 (R40m, exp 28 Feb 2026), CGR 55 (R50m, exp 2 Oct 2026), CGR 56 (R33m, exp 2 Oct 2026) — **~R173m maturing in FY2027**; NHFC R215m repayable in 9 instalments from May 2026 (~R24m each); Prescient R100m bullet Oct 2027; ABSA 1 R155m ÷ 4 from Sep 2027; ABSA 2 R200m ÷ 8 from Mar 2028; Stanlib R250m ÷ 8 from Sep 2028.
- **Determination**: Covenant headroom is asserted but not demonstrated. The DSCR is flattered by new debt. The FY2027 refinancing requirement (~R173m DMTN + ~R191m NHFC instalments) against negative CFO is the primary solvency risk. The current/non-current classification inconsistency must be resolved.

## 2.9 Cash Tax vs Deferred Tax

**Determination: The tax charge is overwhelmingly deferred; cash tax is minimal; the deferred tax liability is dominated by construction-contract timing and the DTA recognition relies on management budgets.**

- **Note 31** [D005.17, p.63]: current tax R2,716,478; deferred tax R44,654,228; total R47,370,706. Effective tax rate **23.87%** (2025: 21.84%) — the earlier 22.82% figure is contradicted.
- **Tax paid** [D005.17, p.64]: R3,890,706 (2025: R2,659,727) — far below the P&L charge, consistent with the deferred-tax dominance.
- **Deferred tax** [D005.11, p.50]: net DTL R399,357,590 (2025: R354,703,361), dominated by **construction contracts DTL R437,663,706** (2025: R381,147,320). Recognised tax-loss DTAs rose from R9.5m to **R24.3m** (+R14.8m) on "approved business plans and budgets" — a P&L credit that flatters the tax charge. Estimated tax losses available rose to **R91,971,218** (2025: R34,222,876).
- **Determination**: Cash tax is low and will remain low while the construction-contract DTL unwinds. The R14.8m tax-loss DTA recognition is a judgement that could reverse if profits disappoint. Forward ETR should be modelled at ~24–27%, with cash tax well below the P&L charge.

**Audit conclusion.** Calgro M3's FY2026 results are **policy-compliant but economically weak**. The reported profit is a composite of non-cash fair-value gains, a non-recurring ECL release, aggressive interest capitalisation, and deferred tax. The cash flow statement tells the true story: **operations consumed R14.8m, and the R29.0m cash increase was entirely borrowed**. The balance sheet is dominated by a R2.1bn construction-contract caption of which R852m is no-revenue WIP carrying no ECL, and the contract-asset "specific exposure" reclassification is an unresolved provisioning red flag. The debt is entirely unsecured, the DSCR is flattered by new borrowings, and the FY2027 maturity wall (~R173m DMTN + ~R191m NHFC) against negative CFO is the primary solvency risk. The parent's going concern depends on a subsidiary "commitment", and the DMTN is subsidiary-guaranteed — obligations not captured in the "no contingent liabilities" representation. **The equity is a leveraged, refinancing-dependent claim on a cash-absorbing development pipeline whose reported margin is a legacy-cost artefact.**
