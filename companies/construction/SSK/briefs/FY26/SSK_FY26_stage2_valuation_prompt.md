### 1. Executive Forensic Audit Summary

**True Economic Performance:**
Stefanutti Stocks has delivered a robust operational turnaround, significantly overshadowed by a massive, one-off legal settlement. The core business is profitable with an operating margin of 4.4% on R7.4bn revenue, which is a commendable recovery from the previous year's 1.2% margin. The R2.3bn increase in the order book to R17.2bn paints a promising forward picture, albeit heavily weighted toward the Africa Region.

**Balance Sheet Health & Post-Balance-Sheet Liquidity Runway:**
The balance sheet has been fundamentally deleveraged. The successful refinancing of the historical debt with a new R850 million Standard Bank facility and the subsequent application of Kusile settlement proceeds have reduced interest-bearing debt to R668 million from R1.24 billion. However, a critical forensic finding is that the **current liabilities continue to exceed current assets by R133 million**. The group is technically in a working capital deficit. The immediate liquidity crisis appears to have been averted by aggressive prepayments on the new facility, reducing its drawn balance to R223 million, but the reliance on **advance payments and milestone billings (R2.65 billion in excess billings)** to fund day-to-day operations remains a key risk. The R1.1 billion cash buffer is healthy, but a large portion (R189 million) is restricted cash held within joint operations. The post-balance-sheet release from guarantee providers is a positive liquidity signal.

**Key Findings:**
1.  **Kusile Settlement:** This is the financial year's defining event. The settlement was an accounting event, *not* a cash collection for work performed in the current year. Its validation is central to the company's improved solvency and profitability.
2.  **Discontinued Operations:** The disposals of SS Mozambique and non-core Eswatini joint operations were loss-making (R12.7m loss), masking the strong performance of the core.
3.  **Working Capital Management:** The business is effectively being financed by its customers through large excess billings. The sustainability of this model is the single most critical forward-looking question. The significant road infrastructure receivable is a key collection target.
4.  **Goodwill & Impairments:** A R27 million impairment on land and buildings contrasts with the elevated goodwill (R272m) valuation. The WACC used for goodwill testing has decreased, creating more headroom, but the underlying EBITDA assumptions in the impairment model appear optimistic given the reliance on the current order book.
5.  **Governance & Internal Controls:** The ARCO has explicitly identified revenue recognition on construction contracts as a significant audit risk. It is the key area where management judgement can be subject to bias.

### 2. Detailed Forensic Reasoning & Determinations

**Junction 1: POC Revenue Recognition & IFRS 15 Timing**
*   **Initial Claim:** Revenue is recognised on a stage-of-completion basis, measured by "surveys of work performed" — an output method deemed to be the best indicator. The ARCO identifies this as a key audit matter due to significant management judgement.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The "surveys of work performed" method is standard for construction but is highly subjective and susceptible to manipulation. The audit committee's and auditor's explicit focus on this confirms the risk. The accounting policy states revenue is payable when control is transferred. The R448 million revenue from the Kusile settlement was non-current-year work, and was recognised *at a point in time* upon settlement. This was a transaction price adjustment regarding a past event, not the ongoing POC model. The core POC model is the key driver of the R7.4bn of *real* revenue.
*   **Determination:** The POC output method is theoretically sound but requires constant verification. The recognition of the Kusile settlement is a distinct event and its treatment as a revenue adjustment is appropriate under IFRS 15 as a variable consideration or transaction price adjustment. The core business remains reliant on management's forecasts of costs to complete, which is a permanent area of forensic risk.

**Junction 2: Gross Margin Bridge & Segment Profitability**
*   **Initial Claim:** Group operating profit of R689m was driven by the Kusile settlement. A "normalised" operating profit of R328m (4.4% margin) is presented as the true operational performance. This is composed of Inland (R285m), Coastal (R75m), Western Cape (R48m), and Africa (R-38m).
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The "normalised" R328m margin of 4.4% is the core of the forensic bridge.
    *   **Inland:** Profitability was driven by Roads & Earthworks, Geotechnical, and Electrical disciplines, primarily in the private sector. This is a positive sign of a healthy core market.
    *   **Africa:** The R38m loss masks a complex story. Eswatini and Botswana were profitable, while **Zambia swung from a R92m profit to a loss**. This was attributed to holding costs on delayed awards and a stronger Kwacha. This regional volatility represents a significant earnings risk.
    *   **Coastal:** Margin improved from R65m to R75m on lower revenue, indicating disciplined execution and cost control.
*   **Determination:** The "normalised" operating profit of **R328m** is a fair and reasonable representation of the underlying business's earning power. The margin of 4.4% is thin for the construction industry, confirming the sector's low-margin, high-risk nature. The African region's profitability is volatile and requires close monitoring.

**Junction 3: Contract Assets / Unbilled WIP vs. Contract Liabilities & Customer Advances**
*   **Initial Claim:** Excess billings over work done (a contract liability) surged to R2.65bn from R1.2bn. This is an "inflow from working capital" of R947m, funding operations. Contracts in progress (an asset) increased to R705m.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** This is the most critical balance sheet finding. The business's cash flow from operations of R1.67bn is almost entirely due to **R1.64bn in increased excess billings**. This means the group is aggressively billing clients ahead of the work performed. This is not necessarily bad — it can represent sound commercial management of milestone-based contracts. However, it creates a future liability. If projects are delayed or cancelled, this cash may need to be returned, creating a liquidity crunch. The R2.65bn liability is a heavy anchor. The increase in "contracts in progress" (unbilled WIP) of R71m is much smaller and relates to a wastewater project, which is a normal operational timing difference.
*   **Determination:** The driver of cash generation is a massive increase in customer advances (contract liabilities). This does **not** represent a sustainable operating cash flow and creates a potential future liquidity liability. The group's working capital model is currently heavily reliant on client funding, which is a significant risk factor.

**Junction 4: Retentions Receivable and Trade Debtors Aging/IFRS 9 ECL**
*   **Initial Claim:** Retentions receivable increased to R312m. Trade receivables increased to R1.9bn gross. An ECL of R169m is held. A "significant receivable, relating to a road infrastructure project, remains outstanding."
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The aging of trade receivables shows a concerning concentration. The **over 180-day bucket contains R121.5m from South African private clients** and a massive **R205m from a Zambian public (government) client** which has an ECL of 66%. The text confirms this is a government debtor, and while the company is "involved in discussions," the history of non-payment from African government entities is a high-risk profile. The road infrastructure receivable is likely the one driving the "private" overdue balance. The concentration of R220m in high-risk, overdue government debt (mainly Zambian) is a major red flag.
*   **Determination:** The ECL provision of R169m appears *conservative* but for a specific reason: it is dominated by the 65.9% provision on a single Zambian government debtor. While provisioned, the risk of non-recovery remains. The significant overdue road infrastructure receivable is the single largest collection risk in the South African portfolio. The overall receivables book is high-risk.

**Junction 5: Joint Ventures vs. Proportionately Consolidated Joint Operations (e.g., BDC / Bankenveld District City)**
*   **Initial Claim:** The group's JV structures are classified as "joint operations" and proportionately consolidated.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** Note 11 lists several joint operations, but **there is no mention of a "Bankenveld District City" or "BDC" project as a material joint operation.** This is a critical, unexplained omission. The extracted text does not provide any evidence of a significant JV or property development by that name. This is a potential blind spot or an entity that has been wound down or is immaterial. The disclosure does not support a significant current exposure to this specific type of major property development JV.
*   **Determination:** Based on the provided working papers, **no evidence exists to support the claim of a material "Bankenveld District City" joint operation.** The forensic file is silent on this matter, and the list of active joint operations does not include it. This is flagged as a **discrepancy with the initial (implied) claim set.**

**Junction 6: Borrowing Costs Capitalised into Construction Assets**
*   **Initial Claim:** Finance costs are expensed (R142.8m) and include interest on facility (R80m), instalment sales (R25m) and leases (R13m). No mention is made of borrowing costs being capitalised.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The review of the accounting policies and notes does not indicate that the group capitalises borrowing costs into the cost of constructed or developed assets. All interest is expensed in the statement of profit or loss. For a construction group that also has a property development subsidiary ("Masingita Towers"), a portion of borrowing costs should be capitalised against the cost of those assets. The group's policy of not capitalising any borrowing costs, even for long-duration assets, is a point of forensic scrutiny.
*   **Determination:** The group **does not capitalise borrowing costs**. This is a conservative but slightly unusual policy for a construction group with long-term assets. It means the full cost of financing is immediately recognized in profit or loss, which lowers current reported earnings but avoids future asset impairments.

**Junction 7: Onerous Contract Provisions and Performance Guarantee/Bonding Facilities**
*   **Initial Claim:** A provision for onerous contracts exists within the R827m total provisions. An example of a loss-making project in Mauritius required a provision. Performance guarantees of R4.08bn are outstanding, with financial guarantees for the Standard Bank Facility of R150m.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The use of "over the full expected loss" is standard IFRS, but the existence of onerous contracts (like the Mauritius project) indicates that the company is taking on, or has legacy, loss-making projects. The significant guarantee line of R4.08bn for construction performance guarantees is far larger than the R580m (to be settled) or R223m (current) facility. This R4bn+ exposure is a significant off-balance-sheet risk. The R150m financial guarantee provided to the bank confirms the group's exposure to its own facility.
*   **Determination:** The guarantees of R4.08bn are a **major off-balance-sheet risk**. While the company has not had a catastrophic call-up, the scale of these guarantees means a single large project failure could have a severe financial impact. The capitalisation of R150m against the facility is a tangible on-balance-sheet manifestation of the risk. The ongoing onerous contract provisions are a reminder of the industry's high-risk nature.

**Junction 8: DMTN Corporate Bond and Bank Debt Covenant Headroom**
*   **Initial Claim:** No DMTN corporate bond exists. The debt is a "Standard Bank Facility" of R850m, drawn down to R223m. The new facility has standard covenants.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The old loan was fully settled on 31 October 2025 and replaced by the new facility. There is no evidence of a DMTN bond in the extracted text. The covenants of the new facility are strong: Total Debt Service Cover ratio (4.66x vs required 1.00x), Leverage Ratio (0.79x vs required <2.5x), and Interest Cover Ratio (6.34x vs required >2.00x). The company has substantial headroom. The facility is partially supported by guarantees from "the group's guarantee providers" which were released after year-end, creating a positive post-balance-sheet event.
*   **Determination:** The company has **excellent financial covenant headroom** on its new bank facility. The successful refinancing and deleveraging significantly reduce medium-term liquidity risk. The post-year-end release of the guarantee providers strengthens the group's solvency and reaffirms its ability to fund itself without third-party support.

**Junction 9: Cash Tax vs. Deferred Tax**
*   **Initial Claim:** A cash tax payment of R148m was made against a current tax charge of R80.7m. This difference is driven by:
    *   **Utilisation of assessed losses:** The complex tax reconciliation shows a R130m tax benefit from utilising assessed losses.
    *   **Recognition of Deferred Tax Asset:** A R35m deferred tax asset was raised "on these losses," suggesting the company now believes future profits are probable.
*   **Forensic Reasoning:** The high cash tax payment is likely due to withholding taxes and non-deductible expenses. The recognition of the R35m deferred tax asset is a strong signal of confidence from management in the group's turnaround. The "use of assessed losses" is a key value driver. The fact that South Africa has a "limited to 80% of taxable income" rule means that the R570m in unrecognised assessed losses will take years to utilise, another sign of an optimistic long-term model.
*   **Determination:** The **R35 million** deferred tax asset recognition is a key positive signal from management. The use of assessed losses is a significant economic benefit. The cash tax outflow is higher than the profit charge due to the Non-Refundable Withholding Tax on dividends from foreign operations (R1.4m) and disallowable expenditure. This is a sustainable cash tax profile at current profit levels.
