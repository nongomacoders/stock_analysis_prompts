**Executive Investment Thesis:**
Stefanutti Stocks has successfully navigated a existential restructuring, culminating in the settlement of the Kusile Power Project claim (R580m) and the refinancing of debt via a new R850m Standard Bank facility. The "headline" profit of R632m is distorted by the one-off Kusile windfall (R492m post-tax). The *true* underlying operating profit is approximately **R328m** (4.4% margin), a commendable recovery but still thin for the sector.

The balance sheet has been deleveraged (interest-bearing debt down to R668m from R1.24bn), and the order book has doubled to R17.2bn. However, the company remains technically insolvent on a working capital basis (current liabilities exceed current assets by R133m) and is heavily reliant on **R2.65bn in excess billings (customer advances)** to fund operations. This is a non-sustainable source of cash and a future liability. While the order book provides revenue visibility, the African region (specifically Zambia) remains a drag, and the R4.08bn in performance guarantees represent a significant off-balance-sheet risk.

At R5.78, the market is pricing in a successful execution of the new order book. We initiate with a **HOLD** rating and a target price of **R6.20**, implying 7.3% upside. The risk/reward is balanced; significant upside requires flawless execution and a reduction in reliance on customer advances, while downside is protected by the restructured balance sheet and strong covenant headroom.

### 6. Actionable Portfolio Monitoring Triggers & Catalysts

**Operational & Balance Sheet Red Lines:**
1.  **Excess Billings Unwinding:** A sharp decline in the R2.65bn excess billings liability without corresponding cash generation would signal a liquidity crunch.
2.  **Zambian Receivable:** The R205m Zambian government receivable (66% provisioned) remains a key collection risk. Any further delays or write-offs would impact earnings.
3.  **Performance Guarantees:** The R4.08bn in guarantees are a major off-balance-sheet risk. A call-up on a large project would be catastrophic.
4.  **Covenant Compliance:** The new Standard Bank facility has strong headroom (Leverage 0.79x vs 2.5x limit), but a deterioration in earnings could pressure covenants.

**Quantified Sensitivity Impacts:**
*   **Gross Margin:** Every 100 bps shift in gross margin impacts HEPS by ~25 cents and fair value by ~R1.00/share.
*   **Interest Rates:** A 100 bps increase in JIBAR would reduce pre-tax profit by R15m (based on AFS sensitivity).

**Re-Rating Catalysts:**
*   **Major Contract Award:** Securing a large-scale renewable energy or data centre project in South Africa.
*   **Debt Reduction:** Further deleveraging below R500m.
*   **Dividend Resumption:** A signal that the balance sheet is fully repaired.
*   **Zambian Receivable Collection:** Successful recovery of the R205m government debt.
